## 正文

Will AI steal your job? It is the question that haunts office corridors and dinner tables alike. A team of leading academic and think tank researchers recently set out to forecast AI's effects on the economy between now and 2030. They surveyed economists, AI experts, and "superforecasters" — people with a proven track record of predicting global events. Their findings offer little comfort to anyone seeking a simple answer.

The most striking conclusion concerns the top of the occupational ladder. Most experts agreed that higher-ranking roles — chief executives, senior managers, legislators — would continue to grow even under a "rapid" AI scenario. These positions involve strategic decision-making and complex human relationships, tasks that current AI systems still struggle to handle. After all, an algorithm cannot negotiate a merger or inspire a discouraged team. In other words, the people least likely to be replaced are those already at the summit.

For the vast majority of white-collar workers, however, the forecast was far murkier. Researchers could not reach a consensus on whether AI would eliminate more middle-skill jobs than it creates. Some economists argued that automation has historically raised overall employment by spawning entirely new industries; others countered that AI is fundamentally different because it targets cognitive rather than manual labour. A lawyer might lose document-review tasks to an algorithm, but a paralegal might gain new responsibilities supervising that same algorithm. Who, then, counts as a "winner" and who a "loser"?

Part of the difficulty lies in measurement. Hard numbers on AI-driven job displacement are surprisingly scarce. Companies rarely disclose how many positions they have cut because of automation, and workers who are replaced often drift into unrelated fields, making their trajectories difficult to track. Official unemployment figures seldom capture this hidden reshuffling. Meanwhile, the technology itself evolves faster than researchers can design studies. A model that cannot write a coherent email today may manage an entire customer-service desk within months. By the time a study is published, its conclusions may already be outdated.

So, will AI destroy your career? The honest answer is: it depends — on your occupation, your adaptability, and how quickly institutions respond. AI is undeniably reshaping the economy, but the process is neither uniform nor inevitable. Some jobs will vanish; others will transform beyond recognition; still others will emerge that nobody has yet imagined. What is clear is that standing still is the riskiest strategy of all. Workers who invest in skills that complement AI — rather than compete with it — will be best positioned to navigate whatever comes next. The future belongs not to those who fear the machine, but to those who learn to work alongside it.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> Some economists argued that automation has historically raised overall employment by spawning entirely new industries; others countered that AI is fundamentally different because it targets cognitive rather than manual labour.

**结构拆解**：

- **分句1**：Some economists argued that automation has historically raised overall employment by spawning entirely new industries
  - argued 后接宾语从句：automation has historically raised overall employment
  - by spawning entirely new industries：介词短语作方式状语，by + doing 表示"通过……方式"
- **分号**连接两个对比观点
- **分句2**：others countered that AI is fundamentally different because it targets cognitive rather than manual labour
  - countered 后接宾语从句：AI is fundamentally different
  - because 引导原因状语从句：it targets cognitive rather than manual labour
  - rather than：而不是，连接两个并列形容词

**翻译**：一些经济学家认为，从历史上看，自动化通过催生全新的行业提高了整体就业；另一些人则反驳说，AI有本质不同，因为它针对的是脑力劳动而非体力劳动。

### 句子2

> Workers who invest in skills that complement AI — rather than compete with it — will be best positioned to navigate whatever comes next.

**结构拆解**：

- **主干**：Workers will be best positioned to navigate whatever comes next
  - be positioned to do：处于有利地位去做某事
  - whatever comes next：宾语从句，作 navigate 的宾语，"无论接下来发生什么"
- **定语从句1**：who invest in skills 修饰 Workers
- **定语从句2**：that complement AI 修饰 skills
- **插入语**：— rather than compete with it — 补充说明 complement，形成对比：与AI互补而非竞争

**翻译**：那些投资于与AI互补——而非与之竞争——的技能的劳动者，将最有能力应对未来的任何变化。

## 文章大意

本文探讨AI对就业的影响这一热门议题。一组顶尖学者和智库研究员试图预测AI在2030年前对经济的影响，调查了经济学家、AI专家和"超级预测者"。研究发现，高管、管理者等高层职位即使在AI快速发展的情景下仍将持续增长，因为这些岗位涉及战略决策和复杂人际关系。然而，对大多数白领而言，前景模糊不清：研究者无法就AI是否会消灭更多中等技能岗位达成共识。原因在于数据稀缺、技术演进过快，使得真实影响难以量化。作者最终指出，AI正在重塑经济，但过程并不均一——与其担心被取代，不如培养与AI互补的能力。

## 思考题

**1. According to the research, higher-ranking occupations are likely to survive AI disruption because ____.**

A. they require less interaction with technology  
B. they involve strategic and interpersonal tasks that AI cannot easily replicate  
C. they are protected by government regulations  
D. they have already adopted AI tools extensively  

**2. Why is it difficult to measure AI's actual impact on job displacement?**

A. Workers who lose jobs to AI always find better positions immediately  
B. Researchers refuse to study the problem due to its complexity  
C. Companies conceal automation-related job cuts and displaced workers shift to unrelated fields  
D. AI technology has not yet caused any significant job losses  

**3. What does the author ultimately suggest workers should do?**

A. Resist AI adoption and protect traditional job structures  
B. Wait for institutions to solve the problem through regulation  
C. Focus on skills that work alongside AI rather than against it  
D. Transition entirely to higher-ranking managerial positions  

<details>
<summary>参考答案</summary>

1. **B** — 文章指出高层职位涉及"strategic decision-making and complex human relationships, tasks that current AI systems still struggle to handle"，即战略决策和复杂人际关系是AI难以胜任的。

2. **C** — 原文明确提到"Companies rarely disclose how many positions they have cut because of automation, and workers who are replaced often drift into unrelated fields, making their trajectories difficult to track"，即公司很少公开因自动化裁减的岗位数量，被替代的工人往往转入不相关领域，导致轨迹难以追踪。

3. **C** — 文章最后一段指出"Workers who invest in skills that complement AI — rather than compete with it — will be best positioned to navigate whatever comes next"，即应培养与AI互补的技能，而非与之竞争。
