## 正文

The transition from fossil fuels to renewable energy is no longer a distant aspiration — it is underway. In the past decade alone, the cost of solar power has fallen by nearly ninety percent, and wind energy has become the cheapest source of new electricity in most parts of the world. Electric vehicles, once a niche product for environmentally conscious consumers, are now mainstream. These developments suggest that the world may be approaching a tipping point in its response to climate change.

Yet the pace of the transition remains dangerously slow. Despite the rapid growth of renewables, fossil fuels still account for roughly eighty percent of global energy consumption. Carbon emissions continue to rise, and the window for limiting global warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels — the threshold beyond which scientists warn of catastrophic consequences — is closing rapidly. The International Energy Agency has estimated that achieving net-zero emissions by 2050 would require annual investment in clean energy to triple within the next decade.

Part of the difficulty lies in the sheer scale of the infrastructure that must be replaced. The global energy system — encompassing power plants, transmission lines, refineries, pipelines, and vehicles — represents trillions of dollars of sunk investment and decades of accumulated engineering. Replacing it is not simply a matter of building new wind farms and solar parks; it requires rewiring entire economies and retraining millions of workers whose livelihoods depend on the fossil fuel industry.

There is also a geopolitical dimension. The countries that dominate the production of fossil fuels — Russia, Saudi Arabia, the United States — are understandably reluctant to relinquish the economic and political power that comes with controlling the world's energy supply. Meanwhile, the transition has created new dependencies. China currently processes more than half of the world's lithium, cobalt, and rare earth minerals — the raw materials essential for batteries and renewable energy technologies. This concentration of supply creates vulnerabilities that could prove just as problematic as the oil dependencies of the twentieth century.

Perhaps the most contentious issue is the question of fairness. Developing nations, which have contributed least to historical carbon emissions, are bearing the brunt of climate change's effects — from rising sea levels to devastating droughts. They argue, with considerable justification, that they should not be expected to sacrifice economic growth in order to solve a problem created primarily by the industrialized world. At the same time, the communities that depend on coal mining and oil extraction — often poor, often rural — face the prospect of losing not just their jobs but their entire way of life.

The green energy transition, then, is not merely a technical challenge. It is a social, economic, and political one. Success will depend not only on the pace of technological innovation but on the willingness of governments to address the legitimate concerns of those who stand to lose from the transition — and to ensure that the benefits of a cleaner energy system are shared broadly enough to sustain public support. The clock is ticking.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> The window for limiting global warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels — the threshold beyond which scientists warn of catastrophic consequences — is closing rapidly.

**拆分**：
- **主句**: The window is closing rapidly（窗口正在迅速关闭）
- **定语**: for limiting global warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels（将全球变暖限制在比工业化前水平高1.5摄氏度的）
- **插入语（同位语）**: the threshold beyond which scientists warn of catastrophic consequences（科学家警告超越此阈值将产生灾难性后果）
  - beyond which 引导定语从句，修饰 threshold

**翻译**: 将全球变暖限制在比工业化前水平高1.5摄氏度的窗口——科学家警告超越此阈值将产生灾难性后果——正在迅速关闭。

### 句子2

> Developing nations, which have contributed least to historical carbon emissions, are bearing the brunt of climate change's effects, and they argue, with considerable justification, that they should not be expected to sacrifice economic growth in order to solve a problem created primarily by the industrialized world.

**拆分**：
- **主句1**: Developing nations are bearing the brunt of climate change's effects（发展中国家正在承受气候变化影响的主要冲击）
- **非限制性定语从句**: which have contributed least to historical carbon emissions（它们对历史碳排放贡献最小）
- **主句2**: they argue that they should not be expected to sacrifice economic growth（他们争辩说不应期望他们牺牲经济增长）
- **插入语**: with considerable justification（有相当充分的理由）
- **目的状语**: in order to solve a problem created primarily by the industrialized world（为了解决一个主要由工业化世界造成的问题）

**翻译**: 对历史碳排放贡献最小的发展中国家正在承受气候变化影响的主要冲击，他们有相当充分的理由争辩，不应期望他们牺牲经济增长来解决一个主要由工业化世界造成的问题。

## 文章大意

本文讨论了绿色能源转型的现状与挑战。尽管可再生能源成本大幅下降，转型速度仍然过慢。困难在于：现有能源基础设施规模庞大、地缘政治博弈、以及转型公平性问题——发展中国家承受气候变化最大冲击却被要求限制发展，化石燃料依赖社区面临生计威胁。作者认为绿色转型不仅是技术挑战，更是社会、经济和政治挑战，成功取决于能否回应利益受损者的关切并确保转型收益广泛共享。

## 思考题

1. What does the passage identify as a new geopolitical vulnerability created by the green energy transition?
   - A) Europe's dependence on Russian natural gas
   - B) China's dominance in processing minerals essential for renewable technologies
   - C) The United States' withdrawal from international climate agreements
   - D) India's refusal to invest in solar energy

2. Why do developing nations argue that the energy transition is unfair?
   - A) They produce the most renewable energy but receive no credit
   - B) They have contributed least to carbon emissions but bear the greatest impact of climate change
   - C) They are excluded from international climate negotiations
   - D) They cannot afford any form of energy

3. According to the author, what will determine the success of the green energy transition?
   - A) Only the pace of technological innovation
   - B) The ability to address concerns of those who lose from the transition and share benefits broadly
   - C) The complete elimination of all fossil fuel use within five years
   - D) Reducing energy consumption in developing countries

2. **B** — 文中指出发展中国家"have contributed least to historical carbon emissions"却"bearing the brunt of climate change's effects"，因此认为不应被期望为工业化世界造成的问题牺牲经济增长。

3. **B** — 作者在末段指出成功取决于"the willingness of governments to address the legitimate concerns of those who stand to lose from the transition — and to ensure that the benefits...are shared broadly enough to sustain public support"。
