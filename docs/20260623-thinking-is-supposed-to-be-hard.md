## 正文

In the mid-2000s, a girl sat at her family's bulky desktop, teaching herself to code. There was no tutorial video, no interactive prompt, and certainly no AI assistant to debug her errors. She spent painstaking hours tracing each missing semicolon and mistyped variable. The process was slow, frustrating, and — she now realises — irreplaceable. Those long sessions were not simply about fixing bugs; they were about building a way of thinking. Debugging taught her patience, logical precision, and a habit of questioning her own assumptions. The effort was the education.

Years later, the same person became a writer, and the pattern repeated itself. Writing, she discovered, is not the act of transferring pre-formed thoughts onto a page. It is through the act of writing that thoughts become clear at all. A sentence that feels vague forces you to ask: what do I actually mean? A paragraph that refuses to flow reveals a gap in your reasoning. Writing is how you discover what your values are — not by stating them, but by struggling to articulate them.

Both coding and writing now face a common challenge. Large language models can generate functional code and readable prose in seconds. "Vibe-coding" — the practice of prompting an AI and accepting whatever it produces — has already deskilled many aspiring developers. Meanwhile, writing has been overwhelmed by AI-generated content that floods platforms with polished but hollow text. The temptation is obvious: why struggle when a machine can produce a passable result instantly?

Yet the author avoids using AI whenever possible. She is wary of what psychologists call "cognitive offloading" — the tendency to transfer mental effort to external tools and, in doing so, weaken the very capacities that effort was meant to strengthen. A calculator saves time, but a student who never practices mental arithmetic loses number sense. A GPS is convenient, but a driver who relies on it exclusively loses spatial awareness. The pattern is consistent: the convenience that saves effort also erodes the skill that effort built.

Her deepest concern, however, is not for herself but for the generation coming of age in the AI boom. These young people are surrounded by technology presented as magical and opaque — black boxes managed by distant corporations. When everything can be generated in an instant, the idea that thinking should be difficult begins to seem outdated, even foolish. But if we accept that premise, we surrender something vital. The capacity to think carefully, to resist easy answers, and to sit with uncertainty — these are not relics of a pre-AI age. They are the foundation of every skill worth having, and no shortcut can substitute for the struggle that creates them.

### 1

> She is wary of what psychologists call "cognitive offloading" — the tendency to transfer mental effort to external tools and, in doing so, weaken the very capacities that effort was meant to strengthen.

**结构拆解**：

- **主干**：She is wary of what psychologists call "cognitive offloading"
  - what 引导宾语从句，作 of 的宾语
- **同位语**：— the tendency to transfer mental effort to external tools and weaken the very capacities...
  - 不定式并列：to transfer... and (to) weaken...（两个动作由 and 连接）

## 文章大意

作者以自身学编程和写作的经历为线索，强调艰辛的思考过程本身就是学习的核心——调试代码培养逻辑思维，写作模糊的句子倒逼思想清晰。如今LLM让代码和文字一键生成，"vibe-coding"使开发者技能退化，AI生成的空洞内容淹没写作平台。作者警惕"认知卸载"现象：正如依赖计算器削弱数感、依赖GPS削弱方向感，依赖AI会侵蚀努力所建构的能力。她尤其担忧在AI热潮中成长的年轻一代，在"一切可即时生成"的氛围下，"思考理应艰难"的观念被边缘化。但审慎思考、拒绝捷径、与不确定性共处——这些并非过时遗物，而是一切有价值技能的根基。
