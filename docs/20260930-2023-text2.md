## 正文

Is college still worth it? The question would have seemed absurd a generation ago, when a university degree was widely regarded as the surest path to a secure middle-class life. Today, with tuition fees soaring and student debt surpassing 1.7 trillion dollars in the United States alone, millions of families are asking whether the returns justify the investment. The answer, like so much in economics, depends on the details.

The case for college remains strong in aggregate. College graduates still earn substantially more than those without degrees — roughly 75 percent more over a lifetime, according to the Georgetown University Center on Education and the Workforce. The unemployment rate for bachelor's degree holders is consistently about half that of high school graduates. College graduates also report better health, higher life satisfaction, and greater civic engagement. On average, the numbers clearly favour getting a degree.

Yet averages conceal enormous variation. The return on a college investment depends heavily on the institution attended, the field of study, and whether the student completes the degree. A graduate of a prestigious engineering programme will see returns that dwarf the investment many times over. A student who borrows heavily to attend a low-ranked for-profit college and fails to graduate may face a lifetime of debt with no appreciable earnings premium. The gap between the best and worst outcomes has widened dramatically, making the decision riskier than ever.

The debt burden compounds the risk. The average borrower now graduates owing roughly 37,000 dollars, and many owe far more. Student loans are uniquely difficult to discharge, even in bankruptcy, and can follow borrowers for decades, delaying homeownership, marriage, and family formation. The psychological toll is significant: studies link student debt to higher rates of anxiety and depression among young adults. For first-generation college students from low-income families, the stakes are especially high — they have the least margin for error and the fewest resources to fall back on if things go wrong.

The debate has also been complicated by the growing availability of alternatives. Coding bootcamps, professional certificates, and apprenticeship programmes promise marketable skills in months rather than years, at a fraction of the cost. Some major employers, including Google and Apple, have dropped degree requirements for certain positions, signalling that skills may matter more than credentials. These alternatives are not without limitations — they tend to be narrow, lack the breadth of a liberal education, and offer fewer long-term career advantages — but they have undeniably eroded the monopoly that universities once held on post-secondary learning.

The challenge for policymakers is to preserve the genuine value of higher education while making it more affordable and accountable. This means holding institutions responsible for student outcomes, expanding income-based repayment plans, and investing in alternatives that serve students for whom a traditional four-year degree may not be the best fit. College remains one of the most powerful engines of social mobility — but only if it does not become a debt trap that entrenches the very inequality it was meant to overcome.

## 长难句解析

**1.** A student who borrows heavily to attend a low-ranked for-profit college and fails to graduate may face a lifetime of debt with no appreciable earnings premium.

- **拆分**：who 引导定语从句修饰 student，其中 borrows 和 fails 为并列谓语；主句谓语为 may face，with no appreciable earnings premium 为介词短语作定语修饰 debt。
- **翻译**：大量借贷就读低排名营利性大学却未能毕业的学生，可能面临终生的债务，却没有可观的收入溢价。

**2.** College remains one of the most powerful engines of social mobility — but only if it does not become a debt trap that entrenches the very inequality it was meant to overcome.

- **拆分**：but only if 引导条件状语从句；that 引导定语从句修饰 debt trap；it was meant to overcome 为省略了 that 的定语从句修饰 inequality；the very 中 very 表强调。
- **翻译**：大学仍然是最强大的社会流动引擎之一——但前提是它不会成为一个巩固其本应克服的不平等的债务陷阱。

## 文章大意

本文探讨了大学教育是否仍然值得投资。总体而言，大学毕业生终身收入比非毕业生高75%，失业率更低，健康和幸福感也更好。但平均值掩盖了巨大差异：名校工程专业的回报远超投资，而低排名营利性大学未毕业学生可能背负终身债务。学生贷款平均3.7万美元，难以破产免除，延迟购房和成家，带来心理负担。编程训练营等替代方案的兴起也在削弱大学的垄断地位。文章呼吁在保持高等教育价值的同时，提高其可负担性和问责性，确保大学不会成为巩固不平等的债务陷阱。

**2.** The phrase "margin for error" (Paragraph 4) refers to:

A. the space allowed for making mistakes without catastrophic consequences
B. the difference between tuition fees at different colleges
C. the extra credits students need to graduate
D. the interest rate on student loans

**3.** The author's overall position on the value of college is:

A. college is never worth the cost
B. college is always worth the cost regardless of circumstances
C. college remains valuable on average but outcomes vary greatly and reforms are needed
D. alternatives to college are always superior
