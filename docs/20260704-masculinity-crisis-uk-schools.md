# The Misogyny Crisis in Britain's Schools

> **Date**: 20260704 · **Source**: Adapted from The Guardian (Apr 2026) · **Difficulty**: ★★★★☆ · **Type**: 📰 同源外刊精选
>
> **Topic**: #社会 #教育 #性别

A damning survey released by the NASUWT, Britain's largest teaching union, has revealed that 23.4 percent of female teachers have been targeted by misogyny from pupils—a sharp increase from 17.4 percent just three years ago. The figures paint a disturbing picture of a profession under siege, with reports ranging from casual sexist remarks to AI-generated nude images of teachers circulated among students, and even rape jokes delivered openly in classrooms.

The survey, which collected responses from more than 6,000 teachers across England and Wales, found that the problem was not confined to secondary schools. Primary school teachers also reported incidents, though at lower rates. Most alarming was the nature of the abuse: several teachers described discovering deepfake pornographic images of themselves shared on pupils' social media groups, created using freely available AI tools. One teacher told the union that she had considered leaving the profession entirely after discovering that an image of her face had been superimposed onto an explicit photograph and circulated among Year 10 students.

Union leaders have been unequivocal in their condemnation. The NASUWT's general secretary described the situation as a "ticking timebomb," arguing that the proliferation of misogynistic content online—particularly from the so-called manosphere, a network of websites and influencers promoting anti-feminist and often violently sexist ideologies—has normalised hostile attitudes toward women among boys and young men. Social media algorithms, which prioritise engagement above all else, serve as accelerants, pushing impressionable teenagers from relatively mild content toward increasingly extreme material.

The relationship between online radicalisation and classroom behaviour is difficult to quantify precisely, but teachers report an unmistakable shift in tone. Pupils who once would have been corrected for a disrespectful comment now parrot talking points lifted directly from influencers, framing basic courtesy toward female teachers as a form of emasculation. The language has coarsened dramatically, with terms once confined to anonymous online forums now spoken openly in school corridors.

The government has responded by announcing a review of school behaviour policies and pledging additional funding for teacher training on digital safeguarding. However, critics argue that these measures address symptoms rather than causes. "You cannot tackle misogyny in schools without tackling it on the platforms that feed it," said one education policy researcher. Several MPs have called for stricter regulation of social media algorithms that promote extremist content to minors, though such proposals face significant political and legal obstacles.

For the teachers on the receiving end, the crisis is immediate and personal. Many describe a pervasive sense of vulnerability—being watched, evaluated, and degraded by the very students they are charged with educating. The toll is measurable: applications for early retirement and voluntary severance among female teachers have risen sharply. If the trend continues, the profession risks losing not just individual educators but the trust that makes classroom teaching possible at all.

## 文章大意

本文报道英国学校中针对女教师的厌女行为激增现象。NASUWT 调查显示 23.4% 的女教师遭受学生厌女行为，较 2023 年的 17.4% 大幅上升。文章具体描述了问题形式——从性别歧视言论到 AI 换脸色情图片和强奸笑话；指出根源在于"男性圈"网络文化和社交媒体算法的推波助澜，使厌女态度在青少年中正常化；政府虽承诺审查和资助，但被批评治标不治本；女教师的职业信心和安全感严重受损，若趋势持续将动摇课堂教学的基础。
