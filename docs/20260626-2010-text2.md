## 正文

In the summer of 2009, a major telecommunications company experienced what it later described as a "service disruption." For three days, millions of customers were unable to make calls, send messages, or access the internet. When service was finally restored, the company issued a brief statement attributing the outage to a technical malfunction and expressing regret for the inconvenience. Within a week, the company had lost more customers than it had gained in the previous year.

The episode illustrates a principle that crisis management experts have long emphasized: in a corporate crisis, the problem is rarely the problem itself. What destroys trust and drives customers away is not the original failure but the company's response to it. When organizations are perceived as evasive, dismissive, or slow to communicate, the damage to their reputation can far exceed the direct costs of the crisis.

The psychology behind this is straightforward. Trust, once established, creates a set of expectations. Customers expect that the companies they do business with will act competently and honestly. When a crisis occurs, those expectations are violated, and the natural response is to seek reassurance. If the company provides that reassurance — by acknowledging the problem quickly, explaining what went wrong, and outlining the steps being taken to prevent a recurrence — trust can often be restored. But if the company is seen as minimizing the issue, deflecting blame, or withholding information, the violation of expectations is compounded by a second violation: the failure to take responsibility.

Yet many companies continue to resist transparency during crises. The reasons are varied. Legal departments often advise against admitting fault, fearing that any statement could be used as evidence in subsequent lawsuits. Public relations teams may worry that too much detail will only amplify negative coverage. And executives, understandably, prefer to present their organizations in the best possible light. The cumulative effect of these concerns is a communications strategy that prioritizes self-protection over honesty — and that, paradoxically, often inflicts far greater damage than the truth would have.

The digital age has made this lesson more urgent. In an era of social media, information — and misinformation — spreads at extraordinary speed. A company that fails to communicate quickly and clearly will find that others fill the vacuum, often with narratives that are far less flattering than the truth. Within hours of the telecommunications outage, angry customers had taken to Twitter and online forums to share their frustrations, and media outlets were running stories that portrayed the company as indifferent and incompetent.

The irony is that the companies that handle crises most effectively are often those that emerge with their reputations not merely intact but enhanced. By demonstrating honesty, competence, and concern, they turn a moment of vulnerability into an opportunity to prove their character. The lesson is clear: in a crisis, silence and evasion are the real enemies. What customers want, above all, is to be heard, informed, and respected.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> What destroys trust and drives customers away is not the original failure but the company's response to it, and when organizations are perceived as evasive, dismissive, or slow to communicate, the damage to their reputation can far exceed the direct costs of the crisis.

**拆分**：
- **分句1**: What destroys trust and drives customers away is not the original failure but the company's response to it（摧毁信任、赶走客户的不是最初的失败而是公司的应对）
  - What 引导主语从句
- **分句2**: the damage to their reputation can far exceed the direct costs of the crisis（对声誉的损害可能远远超过危机的直接成本）
- **时间状语从句**: when organizations are perceived as evasive, dismissive, or slow to communicate（当组织被认为回避、轻蔑或沟通缓慢时）

**翻译**: 摧毁信任、赶走客户的不是最初的失败而是公司的应对方式，当组织被认为回避、轻蔑或沟通迟缓时，对声誉的损害可能远超危机的直接成本。

### 句子2

> The cumulative effect of these concerns is a communications strategy that prioritizes self-protection over honesty — and that, paradoxically, often inflicts far greater damage than the truth would have.

**拆分**：
- **主句**: The cumulative effect of these concerns is a communications strategy（这些担忧的累积效应是一种沟通策略）
- **定语从句1**: that prioritizes self-protection over honesty（将自我保护置于诚实之上）
- **定语从句2**: that often inflicts far greater damage than the truth would have（往往造成比真相更大的损害）
- **副词**: paradoxically（矛盾的是）

**翻译**: 这些担忧的累积效应是一种将自我保护置于诚实之上的沟通策略——矛盾的是，这往往造成比说出真相更大的损害。

## 文章大意

本文通过一家电信公司服务中断的案例，讨论了企业危机中的沟通问题。危机中真正损害信任的不是问题本身而是公司的应对方式——回避、轻蔑或沟通迟缓会严重损害声誉。许多公司因法律和公关顾虑而拒绝透明沟通，但这反而造成更大伤害。社交媒体时代信息传播极快，沉默只会让他人填补信息真空。成功处理危机的公司反而能提升声誉，关键在于诚实、迅速和尊重。

## 思考题

1. According to the passage, what is the primary cause of customer loss in a corporate crisis?
   - A) The financial cost of the crisis itself
   - B) The company's inadequate or evasive response rather than the original problem
   - C) Competitors offering better prices during the crisis
   - D) Government intervention in the company's operations

2. Why do many companies resist transparency during crises?
   - A) They believe customers prefer not to know the details
   - B) Legal, PR, and executive concerns lead to a strategy prioritizing self-protection
   - C) Transparency has been proven to cause more financial damage
   - D) They are required by law to remain silent

3. What does the passage suggest about companies that handle crises effectively?
   - A) They always suffer long-term reputational damage
   - B) They may actually emerge with enhanced reputations
   - C) They avoid communicating with customers entirely
   - D) They rely exclusively on legal advice

<details>
<summary>参考答案</summary>

2. **B** — 文中列举了法律部门（怕被用作诉讼证据）、公关团队（怕放大负面报道）和高管（想展示最好的一面）的顾虑，导致优先自我保护而非诚实。

3. **B** — 文中指出"the companies that handle crises most effectively are often those that emerge with their reputations not merely intact but enhanced"，有效处理危机的公司声誉反而提升。
