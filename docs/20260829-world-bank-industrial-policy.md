# When Even the World Bank Embraces Industrial Policy

| 项目 | 内容 |
|------|------|
| 日期 | 2026-08-29 |
| 类型 | 外刊精选 |
| 难度 | ★★★★☆ |
| 来源 | The Atlantic |

---

For decades, the World Bank was the cathedral of free-market orthodoxy. Its lending came with structural adjustment conditions: privatize state enterprises, lower trade barriers, and let markets allocate resources. Any government that mentioned "industrial policy" was politely but firmly told that picking winners was a fool's errand and that the state's role was to get out of the way. So when the World Bank recently published a report endorsing industrial policy as a legitimate tool for economic development, it was as if the Vatican had started ordaining women — a paradigm shift so profound that it signals the end of an era.

What changed? In a word: China. Beijing's state-directed industrial strategy transformed the country from a low-cost manufacturer into a technological superpower in roughly two decades. The Chinese government identified strategic sectors — semiconductors, electric vehicles, artificial intelligence — and poured resources into them through subsidies, preferential loans, and state-owned enterprises. Western economists initially dismissed this as inefficient and unsustainable. Then Chinese companies began dominating global markets in solar panels, batteries, and telecommunications equipment, and the dismissal turned to alarm.

The pandemic accelerated the shift. Supply chain disruptions revealed that decades of offshoring had created dangerous dependencies. When the world ran short of semiconductors, it became clear that market forces alone had not produced resilient supply chains — they had produced the cheapest ones. Governments discovered that they could not procure masks, chips, or pharmaceuticals without depending on adversarial nations, and the national security implications were too stark to ignore.

The United States responded with the CHIPS Act and the Inflation Reduction Act, both of which use subsidies and tax credits to steer private investment toward strategic sectors. The European Union unveiled its own industrial strategy. Even traditionally laissez-faire economies like Japan and South Korea expanded government intervention. The irony is rich: the very policies that the World Bank once condemned when practiced by developing nations are now being embraced by the developed world.

But this intellectual reversal carries risks. Industrial policy is easy to announce and difficult to execute well. Governments can indeed pick winners, but they can also pick losers, and the historical record is littered with expensive failures. The danger now is not that governments will do too little but that they will do too much — subsidizing politically connected industries rather than strategically important ones, and shielding domestic companies from competition that would make them stronger.

## 长难句解析

1. Any government that mentioned "industrial policy" was politely but firmly told that picking winners was a fool's errand and that the state's role was to get out of the way.
   - **解析**：主语 "Any government" 后跟 that 引导的定语从句，谓语 was told，两个 that 从句并列作 told 的宾语。"picking winners was a fool's errand" 和 "the state's role was to get out of the way" 构成平行结构，展现世行传统立场。

2. The irony is rich: the very policies that the World Bank once condemned when practiced by developing nations are now being embraced by the developed world.
   - **解析**：冒号后解释 irony 的具体内容。主语 "the very policies" 后跟 that 引导的定语从句（内含 when practiced 作状语），谓语 are now being embraced。核心讽刺：发达国家正在采纳世行曾谴责发展中国家使用的政策。

## 文章大意

世行从自由市场正统观念的捍卫者转变为产业政策的支持者，标志着经济学范式的深刻转变。中国的国家主导工业化战略、疫情暴露的供应链脆弱性，以及国家安全考量，共同推动了这一转变。美欧日韩纷纷出台产业政策，但历史表明产业政策既可能成功也可能造成巨大浪费，当前真正的风险不是政府做得太少，而是做得太多——补贴政治关联产业而非战略产业。
