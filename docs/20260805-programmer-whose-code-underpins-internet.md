## 正文

In the early 1960s, at the RAND Corporation's sleek offices in Santa Monica, a young mathematician named Sharla Perrine Boehm set out to answer a question that would shape the modern world: could a communications network survive a nuclear attack? The simulation she wrote to test this proposition became one of the foundational building blocks of the internet — yet her name remains virtually unknown outside a small circle of historians.

Boehm's work addressed a problem that obsessed Cold War strategists. Military commanders needed to know whether the United States could maintain communication after a Soviet first strike disabled key switching centres. The existing telephone network was centrally controlled and therefore highly vulnerable: destroy a few nodes, and entire regions would go dark. Paul Baran, a colleague at RAND, had proposed an alternative architecture — a distributed network with no central hub, where messages could route around damaged nodes like water flowing around a stone. The concept was elegant, but it was Boehm who proved it could actually work.

Working with limited computing resources, Boehm wrote a program that simulated thousands of network configurations, bombarding them with simulated attacks and measuring how quickly messages could still find their way from sender to receiver. Her results demonstrated convincingly that distributed networks were remarkably resilient: even when a significant fraction of nodes were destroyed, messages continued to reach their destinations through alternative paths. The implications were profound. If communications could survive the destruction of any single node — or many nodes — then a new kind of network was possible, one that was fundamentally robust rather than fundamentally fragile.

Yet history has not been kind to Boehm's contribution. When the story of the internet's origins is told, it is Baran who receives the lion's share of credit. His published papers are widely cited; his name appears in textbooks; his conceptual framework is celebrated as the intellectual foundation of packet-switching networks. Boehm's simulation, by contrast, is rarely mentioned. The reasons are familiar to anyone who studies the history of women in science: her work was classified as technical implementation rather than theoretical insight, a distinction that reflects biases about what constitutes meaningful intellectual contribution.

The "Lost Women of Science" initiative, which brought Boehm's story to light, aims to correct this imbalance. Her programming was not mere execution of someone else's vision — it was creative, rigorous, and essential. Without her proof that distributed routing actually functioned under stress, Baran's elegant theory would have remained precisely that: a theory.

The internet that connects billions of people today rests on the assumption that networks can survive damage and keep functioning. That assumption was first tested not by a famous theorist but by a meticulous programmer whose name we are only now learning to say.

## 长难句解析

**1.** Her results demonstrated convincingly that distributed networks were remarkably resilient: even when a significant fraction of nodes were destroyed, messages continued to reach their destinations through alternative paths.

- **主句**: Her results demonstrated convincingly that...
- **宾语从句**: distributed networks were remarkably resilient
- **冒号解释**: even when a significant fraction of nodes were destroyed, messages continued to reach their destinations through alternative paths
- **让步状语从句**: even when a significant fraction of nodes were destroyed
- **翻译**: 她的结果令人信服地证明了分布式网络具有非凡的韧性：即使大量节点被摧毁，信息仍能通过替代路径到达目的地。

**2.** Her work was classified as technical implementation rather than theoretical insight, a distinction that reflects biases about what constitutes meaningful intellectual contribution.

- **主句**: Her work was classified as technical implementation rather than theoretical insight
- **同位语**: a distinction = 上述分类
- **定语从句**: that reflects biases about what constitutes meaningful intellectual contribution（修饰 distinction）
- **宾语从句**: what constitutes meaningful intellectual contribution（about的宾语）
- **翻译**: 她的工作被归类为技术实现而非理论洞见，这一区分反映了对什么构成有意义的智力贡献的偏见。

## 文章大意（中文概括）

本文讲述了被遗忘的女性程序员Sharla Perrine Boehm的故事。1960年代，她在RAND公司编写了模拟程序，证明分布式通信网络在遭受攻击后仍能运作，这是互联网的理论基石。然而历史将她同事Paul Baran推上了功劳簿，而Boehm的贡献因被归类为"技术实现"而非"理论洞见"而被忽视。"失落的科学女性"项目正努力纠正这一不公：没有她的实证，Baran的理论永远只是理论。

## 思考题

**1.** What was the key question that Boehm's simulation sought to answer?

A. Whether the Soviet Union could intercept American communications.

B. Whether a communications network could continue to function after a nuclear attack.

C. Whether the telephone network could be expanded to cover rural areas.

D. Whether computers could simulate nuclear explosions accurately.

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>B</b>。文章开头明确提出 Boehm 要回答的问题是"could a communications network survive a nuclear attack?"。A是截获通信，与原文不符；C是电话网扩展，原文未提及；D是模拟核爆，非通信网络问题。
</details>

**2.** Why has Boehm received less credit than Baran according to the passage?

A. Because her simulation was flawed and produced incorrect results.

B. Because Baran's work was published first in peer-reviewed journals.

C. Because her work was dismissed as technical implementation rather than theoretical contribution.

D. Because she left RAND before the internet was actually built.

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>C</b>。文章明确指出"her work was classified as technical implementation rather than theoretical insight, a distinction that reflects biases about what constitutes meaningful intellectual contribution"。A与原文"convincingly"矛盾；B原文未提及发表顺序；D原文未提及她离职。
</details>

**3.** The phrase "the lion's share" in the fourth paragraph most nearly means ______.

A. the smallest portion

B. the largest or greatest part

C. the most controversial part

D. the most technical part

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>B</b>。"the lion's share"是习语，意为"最大的份额"。原文说Baran receives the lion's share of credit，即获得了最多的功劳。A是反义；C、D与习语含义无关。
</details>
