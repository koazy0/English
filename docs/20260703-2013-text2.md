## 正文

Few issues in modern politics are as contentious as immigration. In country after country, debates over border control, work permits, and citizenship have become defining features of the political landscape. The arguments on both sides are passionate and, to their respective proponents, compelling. Yet the debate is often conducted in terms that obscure more than they illuminate.

Those who favour stricter immigration controls typically argue that high levels of immigration depress wages, strain public services, and erode national identity. There is some evidence to support the first of these claims: studies have shown that in certain sectors, the influx of workers willing to accept lower pay can reduce wages for native-born workers, at least in the short term. The strain on public services — schools, hospitals, housing — is also real, particularly in communities that experience rapid demographic change. And the cultural anxiety that accompanies such change, while harder to quantify, is no less genuine for being so.

On the other side, advocates of more open borders point to the economic benefits of immigration. Immigrants, they note, are disproportionately likely to start businesses, file patents, and fill labour shortages in key industries. In aging societies such as Japan and much of Western Europe, immigrants provide a crucial source of younger workers whose tax contributions help sustain pension systems and social services. The evidence suggests that, over the long term, immigration tends to boost economic growth rather than hinder it.

There is also a moral dimension to the argument. Millions of people around the world are displaced by war, persecution, and environmental catastrophe. For them, the right to seek a better life in another country is not an abstract policy question but a matter of survival. International law recognizes the right to seek asylum, and many people argue that wealthy nations, which have contributed most to the global conditions that drive migration, have a corresponding obligation to offer refuge.

What is often missing from the debate, however, is a clear-eyed assessment of what immigration policy can and cannot achieve. Immigration is not, by itself, a solution to economic stagnation, nor is it the primary cause of social dislocation. It is a symptom of deeper forces — economic inequality, political instability, and demographic change — that no border policy alone can address. Framing the debate as a simple choice between "open" and "closed" borders encourages the belief that immigration policy alone can resolve problems whose roots lie elsewhere.

A more constructive approach would begin by acknowledging that both sides in the debate have legitimate concerns. The economic and cultural disruptions associated with rapid immigration are real, and so are the economic contributions and moral claims of immigrants. Policy that ignores either set of concerns is unlikely to succeed — or to command public confidence. The challenge is to design immigration systems that are both humane and effective, balancing the needs of host communities with the rights and aspirations of those who seek to join them. That is a difficult balance to strike, but it is the only one that can produce sustainable outcomes.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> Those who favour stricter immigration controls typically argue that high levels of immigration depress wages, strain public services, and erode national identity, and there is some evidence to support the first of these claims.

**拆分**：
- **分句1**: Those argue that...（那些人争辩说……）
  - who favour stricter immigration controls 是定语从句
  - that 引导宾语从句，包含三个并列谓语：depress wages, strain public services, erode national identity
- **分句2**: there is some evidence to support the first of these claims（有一些证据支持这些主张中的第一个）

**翻译**: 那些支持更严格移民管制的人通常认为，大量移民会压低工资、加重公共服务负担并削弱民族认同，而其中第一个主张确实有一些证据支持。

### 句子2

> Many people argue that wealthy nations, which have contributed most to the global conditions that drive migration, have a corresponding obligation to offer refuge.

**拆分**：
- **主句**: Many people argue that...（很多人认为……）
- **宾语从句主语**: wealthy nations（富裕国家）
- **非限制性定语从句**: which have contributed most to the global conditions（对推动移民的全球条件贡献最大的）
- **定语从句**: that drive migration（驱动移民的）
- **宾语从句核心**: have a corresponding obligation to offer refuge（有相应的义务提供庇护）

**翻译**: 很多人认为，对推动移民的全球条件贡献最大的富裕国家，有相应的义务提供庇护。

## 文章大意

本文讨论了移民政策的争议。支持管制者认为移民压低工资、加重公共服务负担、削弱民族认同；支持开放者指出移民促进经济增长、填补劳动力缺口，且存在道德维度——富裕国家有义务接纳难民。作者认为双方都有合理关切，但当前辩论忽视了移民政策本身不能解决深层问题。建设性的方法应承认双方关切的合理性，设计既人道又有效的移民体系，在本地社区需求与移民权利之间取得平衡。

## 思考题

1. According to the passage, which of the claims made by immigration control advocates has some supporting evidence?
   - A) The claim that immigration erodes national identity
   - B) The claim that immigration depresses wages in certain sectors
   - C) The claim that immigrants commit more crimes
   - D) The claim that immigration increases housing prices everywhere

2. What does the author identify as a key problem with the current immigration debate?
   - A) There is too much data available
   - B) Both sides have completely unreasonable arguments
   - C) The debate is framed as a simple choice between open and closed borders, ignoring deeper forces
   - D) Only economists should participate in the debate

3. What does the author recommend as a more constructive approach to immigration policy?
   - A) Completely closing all borders
   - B) Opening all borders without restrictions
   - C) Acknowledging both sides' legitimate concerns and designing humane, effective policies
   - D) Delegating all immigration decisions to international courts

<summary>参考答案</summary>

1. **B** — 文中指出"there is some evidence to support the first of these claims"，即移民压低工资（"depress wages"）这一主张有部分证据支持。

2. **C** — 作者指出"Framing the debate as a simple choice between 'open' and 'closed' borders encourages the belief that immigration policy alone can resolve problems whose roots lie elsewhere"。

3. **C** — 作者在末段建议"A more constructive approach would begin by acknowledging that both sides in the debate have legitimate concerns"，并主张平衡本地社区需求与移民权利。
