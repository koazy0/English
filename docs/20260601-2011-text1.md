## 正文

Walk into almost any clothing store in a Western shopping mall and you are likely to find garments inspired by military uniforms: camouflage jackets, cargo pants, and combat boots have all become fashion staples. But the growing popularity of military-style clothing has sparked a debate that goes far beyond questions of taste. At its heart lies a simple but uncomfortable question: does selling military-inspired fashion trivialize the sacrifices made by those who actually serve?

The commercialization of military imagery is not new. Fashion designers have drawn on military aesthetics for decades, adapting the clean lines and functional details of uniforms for civilian wardrobes. During the Iraq and Afghanistan wars, however, the trend accelerated. Retailers discovered that military-inspired clothing sold particularly well during times of conflict, when public sympathy for the armed forces was at its peak. Camouflage prints appeared on everything from baby strollers to bikinis.

Critics of the trend argue that this amounts to a form of disrespect. Military uniforms, they point out, are not merely decorative — they symbolize duty, sacrifice, and national service. When the same patterns are printed on throw pillows and beach towels, the gravity of what they represent is diluted. Some veterans' organizations have gone so far as to call for boycotts of retailers who treat military symbols as marketing tools.

Retailers and fashion houses respond that their intentions are entirely benign. Military-inspired clothing, they argue, is a form of tribute rather than mockery. By incorporating military elements into everyday fashion, they claim, the public is reminded of the armed forces and the work they do. Moreover, they note, no one objects to the civilian use of other institutional clothing styles — police-inspired jackets or firefighter-style boots — without assuming that these trivialize those professions.

The debate is complicated by the fact that the military itself has long been complicit in the commercialization of its image. Recruitment advertising routinely portrays military service as an adventure, using imagery that would not look out of place in a fashion magazine. The armed forces have even entered into licensing agreements that allow their logos and insignia to appear on consumer products. If the military is willing to commodify its own symbols, critics ask, can retailers fairly be blamed for doing the same?

Perhaps the most troubling aspect of this debate is what it reveals about the relationship between modern societies and their militaries. In countries where only a small percentage of the population serves, military life has become something most citizens experience only through media and merchandise. The risk is that, as the gap between the military and civilian worlds widens, the public's understanding of military service shrinks to a set of superficial images — stripped of their meaning and sold at a discount.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> When the same patterns are printed on throw pillows and beach towels, the gravity of what they represent is diluted.

**拆分**：
- **时间状语从句**: When the same patterns are printed on throw pillows and beach towels（当相同的图案被印在靠垫和沙滩巾上时）
- **主句**: the gravity of what they represent is diluted（它们所代表的庄严感被削弱了）
  - what they represent 是宾语从句，作 of 的宾语

**翻译**: 当相同的图案被印在靠垫和沙滩巾上时，它们所代表的庄严感就被削弱了。

### 句子2

> The risk is that, as the gap between the military and civilian worlds widens, the public's understanding of military service shrinks to a set of superficial images — stripped of their meaning and sold at a discount.

**拆分**：
- **主句**: The risk is that...（风险在于……）
- **表语从句**: as the gap between the military and civilian worlds widens（随着军队与平民世界之间的鸿沟扩大）
- **核心表达**: the public's understanding...shrinks to a set of superficial images（公众的理解缩小为一组肤浅的图像）
- **过去分词短语作定语**: stripped of their meaning and sold at a discount（被剥夺了意义并被折价出售）

**翻译**: 风险在于，随着军队与平民世界之间的鸿沟扩大，公众对军役的理解会缩小为一组肤浅的图像——被剥夺了意义并被折价出售。

## 文章大意

本文讨论了军装风格服装商业化的争议。军事风格服饰在时尚界大受欢迎，但批评者认为这轻视了军人的牺牲，将军事符号变成了营销工具；零售商则认为这是致敬而非嘲笑。文章指出军方自身也参与了形象商品化，而最令人担忧的是，随着军民差距扩大，公众对军役的理解正在缩减为一组被剥离了意义的肤浅图像。

## 思考题

1. According to the passage, why did military-inspired clothing become particularly popular during the Iraq and Afghanistan wars?
   - A) Military uniforms became more fashionable
   - B) Public sympathy for the armed forces was at its peak
   - C) The military began selling its own clothing lines
   - D) Fashion designers were recruited by the army

2. How do retailers defend their sale of military-style clothing?
   - A) They argue that it generates revenue for veterans
   - B) They claim it serves as a form of tribute to the armed forces
   - C) They deny that military symbols have any special meaning
   - D) They point out that only a few customers buy such items

3. What does the author suggest is the most concerning aspect of this debate?
   - A) Retailers are making large profits from military symbols
   - B) The military refuses to acknowledge its role in commercialization
   - C) The public's understanding of military service is becoming increasingly shallow
   - D) Veterans are boycotting all clothing stores

<details>
<summary>参考答案</summary>

1. **B** — 文中指出"military-inspired clothing sold particularly well during times of conflict, when public sympathy for the armed forces was at its peak"，即公众对军队的同情达到顶峰时销量最好。

2. **B** — 零售商辩称"their intentions are entirely benign"并认为这是一种致敬（"a form of tribute rather than mockery"）。

3. **C** — 作者在末段指出最令人担忧的是，随着军民差距扩大，公众对军役的理解缩减为"一组被剥离了意义的肤浅图像"（superficial images stripped of their meaning）。
