# 2023 Text 3 — 零工经济与劳动者权益

## 正文

The gig economy was supposed to set workers free. Flexible hours, no boss watching over your shoulder, the ability to earn money whenever and wherever you choose — the appeal was obvious. But for millions of platform workers around the world, the reality has been very different. Far from liberating them, the gig economy has created a vast class of workers who lack the most basic protections that employees have taken for granted for over a century.

The scale of the problem is enormous. An estimated 435 million people worldwide engage in some form of platform-based work, from driving for Uber to delivering meals for DoorDash to doing freelance coding on Upwork. In the United Kingdom, roughly one in ten workers is engaged in gig work. In the United States, the figure is similar, and growing. What unites these workers — despite the diversity of their tasks — is their classification as independent contractors rather than employees. This single legal distinction determines whether they receive minimum wage guarantees, paid sick leave, unemployment insurance, employer pension contributions, and protection against unfair dismissal. In almost every case, they do not.

The consequences are severe and predictable. Gig workers earn significantly less than employees doing comparable work, once all costs — vehicle maintenance, fuel, insurance, equipment — are factored in. A 2022 study of ride-hailing drivers in the United States found that, after expenses, they earned an average of 9.21 dollars per hour — well below the federal minimum wage. They have no paid holidays, no right to sick pay, and no compensation for workplace injuries. During the COVID-19 pandemic, when delivery drivers were classified as essential workers, many continued working while ill because they could not afford to stop. The irony was bitter: celebrated as heroes, treated as disposable.

The legal battles over worker classification have been fierce and contradictory. In California, a law known as AB5 sought to reclassify many gig workers as employees, but was effectively overturned by Proposition 22, a ballot measure funded by 200 million dollars from Uber, Lyft, and DoorDash. In the United Kingdom, the Supreme Court ruled in 2021 that Uber drivers are workers — a category between employee and contractor — entitled to minimum wage and holiday pay. The European Union has proposed a directive that would create a legal presumption of employment for platform workers. Yet in most jurisdictions, the default remains contractor status, and the burden of proving otherwise falls on the worker.

The technology platforms argue that reclassification would destroy the flexibility that gig workers value. They claim that the majority of their workers prefer the freedom to set their own schedules and that mandating employee status would force them to offer fewer hours and eliminate the on-demand model entirely. There is a kernel of truth in this — flexibility is genuinely valued by some workers, particularly those using gig work to supplement other income. But the argument is also self-serving. It obscures the fact that flexibility and basic protections are not mutually exclusive. Several European countries have demonstrated that it is possible to guarantee minimum standards while preserving scheduling freedom.

The gig economy is here to stay. The question is not whether platform work will exist, but on what terms. A system that extracts labour without providing security is not innovation — it is exploitation dressed in the language of disruption. Reforming it will require both legal innovation and political will. Workers, consumers, and regulators must insist that the rules of the digital economy are not written solely by those who profit from it.

## 长难句解析

**1.** What unites these workers — despite the diversity of their tasks — is their classification as independent contractors rather than employees.

- **拆分**：What unites these workers 为主语从句，is 为系动词，their classification 为表语；as independent contractors rather than employees 说明 classification 的具体内容；破折号间为让步状语。
- **翻译**：将这些工作者联系在一起的——尽管他们的任务多种多样——是他们被归类为独立承包商而非雇员。

**2.** A system that extracts labour without providing security is not innovation — it is exploitation dressed in the language of disruption.

- **拆分**：that 引导定语从句修饰 system；破折号后为对前句的否定性定义；dressed in 为过去分词短语作后置定语，比喻剥削被"颠覆"话语所包装。
- **翻译**：一个只索取劳动而不提供保障的制度不是创新——它是披着颠覆话语外衣的剥削。

## 文章大意

本文论述了零工经济中劳动者权益的严重缺失。全球约4.35亿人从事平台工作，但被归类为独立承包商而非雇员，因此没有最低工资保障、带薪病假、失业保险和工伤赔偿。美国网约车司机扣除费用后时薪仅9.21美元，远低于联邦最低工资。法律斗争此起彼伏：加州AB5法案被平台公司资助的公投提案推翻，英国最高法院裁定优步司机为"工作者"，欧盟也提出了相关指令。平台声称重新分类会损害灵活性，但灵活性与基本保障并不矛盾。文章指出，一个只索取劳动不提供保障的制度是披着颠覆话语的剥削。
