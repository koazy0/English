## 正文

When schools around the world shifted to remote learning during the pandemic, a familiar phrase returned to prominence: the digital divide. The gap between those with access to technology and those without was supposed to be closing. Instead, it has widened — and evolved. The original digital divide, defined by access to devices and internet connections, was always just the first layer. Beneath it lies a more insidious and consequential divide: the gap between those who can use digital technology to learn, earn, and participate in society, and those who are merely consumed by it.

The first divide — access — remains stubbornly persistent. Roughly 2.6 billion people worldwide still lack internet access, and the gap is widest in the poorest regions. But even in wealthy nations, significant disparities exist. In the United States, roughly one in four rural residents lacks reliable broadband. In the United Kingdom, children from low-income families are significantly less likely to have a dedicated device for schoolwork. During the pandemic, stories of children completing homework on parents' smartphones or sitting in car parks outside libraries to access Wi-Fi became painfully common. Access is a necessary condition for digital participation, but it is far from sufficient.

The second divide — skills — is where the real inequality deepens. Digital literacy is not a binary condition; it exists on a spectrum. At one end are those who can navigate interfaces, consume content, and use basic applications — what researchers call "functional literacy." At the other end are those who can critically evaluate online information, create digital content, understand how algorithms shape their experience, and use technology to solve problems. The gap between passive consumers and active creators mirrors the gap between the economically secure and the precarious. Research consistently shows that children from affluent families develop more sophisticated digital skills, not because they spend more time on screens, but because their parents guide their use, provide educational resources, and model productive technology habits.

The third divide — outcomes — is the most consequential and the least visible. Technology amplifies existing advantages. A student with strong digital skills can use the internet to access world-class educational resources, connect with mentors, and build a professional network. A student without those skills is more likely to be drawn into algorithmically curated feeds that prioritise engagement over enlightenment — infinite scrolling, targeted advertising, and misinformation. The same technology that empowers one student can entrap another. This is the cruel paradox of the digital age: the tools that promise democratisation can actually deepen inequality.

Schools are not equipped to close these divides alone. Many teachers lack the training to teach advanced digital skills, and curricula are slow to adapt. Community organisations, libraries, and after-school programmes play crucial roles but are chronically underfunded. Policy interventions must go beyond providing devices to addressing the deeper structural factors — income inequality, linguistic barriers, and the design of digital platforms themselves — that determine who benefits from technology and who is left behind.

The digital divide is not a technology problem; it is a social justice problem. Closing it will require not just better infrastructure but a fundamental commitment to ensuring that the benefits of the digital revolution are shared by all, not monopolised by the few who are already ahead.

## 长难句解析

**1.** Beneath it lies a more insidious and consequential divide: the gap between those who can use digital technology to learn, earn, and participate in society, and those who are merely consumed by it.

- **拆分**：Beneath it lies... 为倒装句，正常语序为 a more insidious divide lies beneath it；冒号后解释该鸿沟，between those who...and those who... 为两个并列的定语从句结构，形成鲜明对比。
- **翻译**：在其之下隐藏着一条更隐蔽、影响更深的鸿沟：那些能够利用数字技术学习、谋生和参与社会的人，与那些仅仅被数字技术所消费的人之间的差距。

**2.** The same technology that empowers one student can entrap another — infinite scrolling, targeted advertising, and misinformation.

- **拆分**：that 引导定语从句修饰 technology；empowers 与 entraps 形成鲜明对比；破折号后三个名词短语具体说明 entraps 的方式。
- **翻译**：赋能一个学生的同样技术也可能困住另一个学生——无限滚动、定向广告和虚假信息。

## 文章大意

本文分析了数字时代教育鸿沟的多层结构。第一层是接入鸿沟——全球26亿人仍无互联网，即使发达国家也存在城乡和贫富差距。第二层是技能鸿沟——数字素养不是二元的，被动消费者和主动创造者之间的差距映射着经济安全和不安定的差距。富裕家庭的孩子并非屏幕时间更多，而是有更好的引导和资源。第三层是结果鸿沟——技术放大现有优势，赋能一方却困住另一方，形成数字时代的残酷悖论。学校无力单独弥合这些鸿沟，政策干预必须超越提供设备，解决收入不平等、语言障碍和平台设计等深层结构性因素。数字鸿沟不是技术问题，而是社会正义问题。

A. technology companies make profits while users suffer
