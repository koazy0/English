## 正文

When we think of forests, most of us picture towering trees and wandering wildlife. We rarely think of balance sheets and profit margins. Yet forests are among the most economically valuable assets on Earth — and we are destroying them at a staggering rate, largely because their true worth is invisible in conventional accounting.

The timber value of forests is well understood. Global trade in wood products exceeds 600 billion dollars annually. But timber represents only a fraction of what forests contribute to the global economy. A landmark study by the World Bank estimated that forests provide ecosystem services worth roughly 16 trillion dollars per year — more than the GDP of the United States. These services include carbon storage, water purification, flood prevention, and soil conservation.

Carbon storage alone is immense. Trees absorb roughly 2.6 billion tonnes of carbon dioxide each year, roughly a third of the carbon dioxide released by fossil fuel burning. If forests were a country, they would be the second-largest carbon sink on the planet after the oceans. Yet when forests are cleared, not only is this storage capacity lost, but the carbon locked in trees is released back into the atmosphere, accelerating climate change.

Water purification is another largely unpriced service. Forests act as natural filters, trapping pollutants and sediments before they reach rivers and reservoirs. Cities that depend on forested watersheds — including New York, Tokyo, and Rio de Janeiro — save billions in water treatment costs. When forests are removed, municipalities must invest heavily in artificial filtration systems, costs that are ultimately borne by taxpayers.

Tourism and recreation add yet another layer of economic value. Forests attract hundreds of millions of visitors annually, generating income for rural communities that have few alternative livelihoods. In Costa Rica, eco-tourism driven by forest conservation has become one of the country's largest sources of revenue, proving that living trees can be more profitable than felled ones.

Despite all this, deforestation continues at an alarming pace. An area of forest the size of a football pitch is lost every single second, primarily in tropical regions. The driving force is agriculture — particularly cattle ranching and soybean farming in the Amazon, and palm oil plantations in Southeast Asia. These activities generate immediate, measurable profits, while the economic losses from deforestation are dispersed, delayed, and difficult to quantify.

The challenge is to make the hidden value of forests visible. Some progress is being made: carbon credit markets now pay landowners to keep trees standing, and governments are experimenting with payments for ecosystem services. But these mechanisms remain small compared to the scale of destruction. Until the economic case for preserving forests becomes as compelling as the case for cutting them down, the chainsaws will continue to roar.

## 长难句解析

**1.** "When forests are cleared, not only is this storage capacity lost, but the carbon locked in trees is released back into the atmosphere, accelerating climate change."

- **结构**: not only...but(also)...连接两个并列分句，not only置于句首引起部分倒装(is this storage capacity lost)；locked in trees为过去分词短语修饰carbon；accelerating为现在分词作结果状语
- **翻译**: 当森林被清除时，不仅这种储存能力会丧失，而且锁在树木中的碳也会释放回大气中，加速气候变化

**2.** "Until the economic case for preserving forests becomes as compelling as the case for cutting them down, the chainsaws will continue to roar."

- **结构**: Until引导时间状语从句，主句为the chainsaws will continue to roar；as compelling as为同级比较结构；chainsaws为隐喻，指代毁林行为
- **翻译**: 直到保护森林的经济理由变得和砍伐森林一样有说服力，电锯才会停止轰鸣

## 文章大意

本文论述森林的隐性经济价值。文章首先指出森林的经济价值远不止木材，其提供的生态系统服务（碳储存、水净化、防洪等）每年价值约16万亿美元；然后逐一分析碳储存、水净化和生态旅游三方面价值；接着指出毁林仍在加速，因为农业利润立即可见，而毁林损失分散且难以量化；最后呼吁让森林的隐性价值变得可见，建立保护森林的经济激励机制。

## 思考题

**1.** Why does the author mention New York, Tokyo, and Rio de Janeiro?

A. To illustrate that large cities are the main causes of deforestation
B. To show that cities with forested watersheds save money on water treatment
C. To compare different approaches to forest conservation
D. To argue that urban development threatens forest ecosystems

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章第四段提到这些城市依赖森林流域，节省了数十亿美元的水处理成本，用以证明森林水净化功能的经济价值。</details>

**2.** What is the primary reason that deforestation continues despite forests' enormous economic value?

A. Governments lack the legal authority to protect forests
B. The economic losses from deforestation are hard to measure and delayed
C. Timber companies have more political power than environmental groups
D. Local communities prefer agriculture to forest conservation

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章第六段指出农业活动产生立即可衡量的利润，而毁林造成的经济损失是"dispersed, delayed, and difficult to quantify"的。</details>

**3.** What can be inferred about carbon credit markets from the passage?

A. They have completely stopped deforestation in tropical regions
B. They represent a promising but currently insufficient solution

D. They have replaced traditional forestry industries worldwide

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章末段指出碳信贷市场等机制取得了进展，但"remain small compared to the scale of destruction"，说明有前景但尚不足够。</details>
