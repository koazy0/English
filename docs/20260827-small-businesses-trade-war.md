# Small Businesses Caught in the Crossfire of the Trade War

---

When the Supreme Court ruled that President Trump's sweeping tariffs were unconstitutional, small business owners across America allowed themselves a brief moment of hope. That hope lasted less than 48 hours. The White House immediately announced new 15 percent import charges levied under a different legal authority, and the relief that had flickered through the business community was extinguished as quickly as it had appeared.

For companies like WS Game Company, a family-owned board game manufacturer in Michigan, the whiplash has been devastating. The company paid $1.6 million in import charges over the past year on components sourced from China — circuit boards, plastic molds, and printed packaging that have no domestic equivalent. "We looked into moving production stateside," says founder Linda Chen. "The tooling costs alone would bankrupt us, and no American supplier can match the quality at our volume." The tariffs were supposed to incentivize reshoring, but the reality is that small businesses lack the capital to rebuild supply chains that took decades to establish.

The legal gymnastics are themselves a source of anxiety. The Supreme Court's decision hinged on the administration's use of the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) to impose tariffs without congressional approval. The new charges invoke a different statute, one that the White House argues falls within executive authority. Legal scholars are divided on whether this approach will survive judicial scrutiny, leaving businesses in a state of perpetual uncertainty. Should they absorb the costs and hope the tariffs are struck down? Should they raise prices and risk losing customers? Or should they restructure their entire operation for a regulatory environment that may change again next month?

The asymmetry of the trade war's impact is perhaps its most troubling feature. Large corporations can absorb tariff costs, negotiate bulk shipping discounts, or relocate production to tariff-exempt countries. Small businesses have none of these options. They are too small to absorb, too thin-margined to negotiate, and too undercapitalized to relocate. WS Game Company's $1.6 million in import charges represents a larger share of its revenue than the equivalent costs at Hasbro or Mattel, yet no one in Washington seems to be calculating the damage in those terms.

Meanwhile, there is no clear mechanism for recovering costs already paid under the struck-down tariffs. Businesses that paid duties under the now-invalidated IEEPA authority must file individual claims through a bureaucratic process that could take years, with no guarantee of full reimbursement. For small businesses operating on thin margins, the money is effectively gone.

## 长难句解析

1. The Supreme Court's decision hinged on the administration's use of the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) to impose tariffs without congressional approval.
   - **解析**：主语 "The Supreme Court's decision"，谓语 hinged on（取决于），宾语 "the administration's use of the IEEPA"，不定式 "to impose tariffs without congressional approval" 作目的状语，解释 IEEPA 的使用方式。

2. WS Game Company's $1.6 million in import charges represents a larger share of its revenue than the equivalent costs at Hasbro or Mattel, yet no one in Washington seems to be calculating the damage in those terms.
   - **解析**：yet 连接两个并列分句。前句主语 "$1.6 million in import charges"，谓语 represents，宾语 "a larger share of its revenue"，比较级 than 引出对比对象。后句表达讽刺：尽管比例更高，华盛顿无人以此角度衡量损失。

## 文章大意

美国最高法院裁定特朗普关税违宪后，白宫立即以不同法律依据征收新的15%进口费，让小企业再次陷入困境。WS Game公司一年支付了160万美元进口费，但找不到国内替代供应商，也无法承担回迁成本。大企业能吸收或规避关税成本，小企业却无此能力。此外，已缴纳的违宪关税没有明确的退还机制，对小企业而言这笔钱实际上已经打了水漂。
