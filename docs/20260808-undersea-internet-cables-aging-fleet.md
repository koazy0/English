# The Undersea Internet Cables and an Aging Fleet of Repair Ships

## 正文

When Iran's government threatened to cut undersea internet cables in the Strait of Hormuz, the warning exposed a vulnerability far greater than any single geopolitical flashpoint: the world's undersea cable network is maintained by a shrinking and aging fleet of repair vessels that is barely adequate for peacetime operations, let alone a crisis.

Submarine cables carry approximately 99 per cent of intercontinental data traffic, including the vast majority of international financial transactions, diplomatic communications, and cloud computing traffic. There are roughly 600 active cables spanning more than 1.4 million kilometres on the ocean floor. Despite this enormous infrastructure, the fleet dedicated to repairing them numbers only about 60 ships worldwide. Each vessel costs upwards of $100 million and takes years to build. When a cable breaks — and they break with surprising frequency — the nearest available repair ship may be weeks away.

The scale of the maintenance challenge is staggering. Between 150 and 200 cable faults occur annually, caused by fishing trawlers, anchor drags, undersea landslides, and occasional shark bites. Most individual faults are repaired within days or weeks, but simultaneous damage to multiple cables — whether from a natural disaster or deliberate sabotage — could overwhelm the repair fleet's limited capacity. The 2006 earthquake off Taiwan damaged eight cables simultaneously, disrupting internet service across East Asia for weeks. A similar event today, with far greater data volumes at stake, would be catastrophic.

The age profile of the repair fleet compounds the concern. Approximately half the global cable fleet is approaching its end-of-life designation by 2040, and at least 25 per cent of cable kilometres are due for retirement by 2030. Replacing ageing cables requires not only manufacturing new ones but also deploying them across ocean floors — a process that takes years and depends on the same limited fleet of installation vessels. The industry is caught in a vicious circle: cables need repair and replacement, but the ships that perform this work are themselves ageing and insufficient.

Some relief may come from emerging technologies. Autonomous underwater vehicles are being developed to inspect and potentially repair shallow-water cables, and new cable designs promise greater resilience against physical damage. But these innovations remain years from widespread deployment and do not address the fundamental shortfall in repair capacity.

The risk is not hypothetical. Undersea cables have been cut before — accidentally and deliberately. As dependence on digital infrastructure grows and geopolitical tensions rise, the question is not whether a major cable disruption will occur but when. Without significant investment in repair capacity and cable renewal, the internet's physical backbone is one crisis away from a systemic failure that no cloud can float above.

伊朗威胁切断海底光缆暴露了更大的脆弱性：全球约600条海底光缆承载99%洲际数据，但维修船仅约60艘。每年150-200次光缆故障，同时损坏多条光缆可能令维修能力崩溃。更严峻的是，半数船队2040年前接近退役，25%光缆公里数2030年前需更换，而新光缆铺设依赖同一支不足的船队——形成恶性循环。自动驾驶水下航行器等新技术尚需数年才能推广。问题不是大规模光缆中断是否会发生，而是何时发生。

## 思考题

**1.** What primary vulnerability did Iran's threat expose according to the passage?

A. That undersea cables are too easy for terrorists to access.

B. That the repair fleet for undersea cables is insufficient and aging.

C. That most undersea cables are owned by a single country.

D. That satellite internet can completely replace undersea cables.

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>B</b>。文章开头明确指出威胁暴露了"the world's undersea cable network is maintained by a shrinking and aging fleet of repair vessels that is barely adequate for peacetime operations"。A原文未提及恐怖分子；C原文未提及单一所有权；D原文未提及卫星替代。
</details>

**2.** Why does the author mention the 2006 earthquake off Taiwan?

A. To illustrate that simultaneous cable damage can overwhelm repair capacity.

B. To argue that earthquakes are the most common cause of cable faults.

C. To demonstrate that East Asia has the most vulnerable cable network.

D. To show that cable repair technology has greatly improved since 2006.

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>A</b>。作者引用2006年台湾地震案例来说明"simultaneous damage to multiple cables...could overwhelm the repair fleet's limited capacity"，即同时损坏多条光缆会压垮有限的维修能力。B原文未说地震是最常见原因（最常见是拖网渔船）；C原文未比较地区脆弱性；D原文未提及技术改善。
</details>

**3.** The phrase "vicious circle" in the fifth paragraph refers to the situation where ______.

A. cable companies make profits by charging high repair fees

B. newer cables are more fragile and need more frequent repairs

C. cables need repair but the repair ships themselves are aging and inadequate

D. more data traffic causes more cable faults, which cause more data loss

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>C</b>。原文解释"vicious circle"："cables need repair and replacement, but the ships that perform this work are themselves ageing and insufficient"，即光缆需要维修但维修船本身也在老化和不足。A原文未提及利润；B原文未说新光缆更脆弱；D因果链与原文不符。
</details>
