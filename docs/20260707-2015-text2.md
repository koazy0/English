## 正文

When Maria Gonzalez graduated from university in 2015, she received two job offers. The first, from a prestigious law firm, promised a generous salary, a clear path to partnership, and the kind of institutional prestige that would have made her parents proud. The second, from a small non-profit organization focused on environmental advocacy, paid barely enough to cover rent. She chose the non-profit. Her parents were bewildered.

Maria's decision reflects a broader generational shift in attitudes toward work. Surveys conducted across major economies consistently show that millennials and Generation Z workers rank purpose, flexibility, and work-life balance above salary and job security — a reversal of the priorities that defined their parents' and grandparents' careers. For older workers, who came of age during periods of economic instability and were socialized to value stability above all, this reordering of priorities can be difficult to understand.

The roots of this shift are complex. One important factor is economic circumstance. Many younger workers entered the job market during or after the 2008 financial crisis, only to find that the traditional promises — work hard, climb the ladder, achieve security — no longer seemed reliable. Fixed-term contracts replaced permanent positions, pensions gave way to defined-contribution plans, and the implicit contract between employer and employee was quietly rewritten. In such an environment, loyalty to a single employer came to seem less like a virtue and more like a gamble.

Technology has also played a role. The rise of remote work, freelance platforms, and the gig economy has made it possible — and, for many, preferable — to construct a career outside the traditional corporate framework. Younger workers, who are more comfortable with digital tools and less attached to the rituals of office life, have been quicker to embrace these alternatives. The pandemic accelerated this trend dramatically, normalizing remote work and demonstrating that many jobs could be performed from anywhere.

But perhaps the most significant factor is a different kind of expectation. Younger workers are more likely than their predecessors to expect that their work should be meaningful — that it should contribute to something beyond the bottom line. This is sometimes dismissed as idealism or entitlement, but it may also reflect a genuine shift in values. Growing up with access to unprecedented amounts of information about the world's problems — climate change, inequality, social injustice — many younger workers feel a sense of urgency that earlier generations did not.

Employers have been slow to adapt. Companies that continue to offer rigid hierarchies, inflexible schedules, and work that feels disconnected from any larger purpose are finding it increasingly difficult to attract and retain young talent. Those that have embraced flexibility, invested in professional development, and articulated a clear sense of mission are thriving.

The generational divide at work, then, is not simply a matter of younger employees demanding special treatment. It reflects a genuine transformation in the relationship between individuals and their work — one that employers ignore at their peril.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> For older workers, who came of age during periods of economic instability and were socialized to value stability above all, this reordering of priorities can be difficult to understand.

**拆分**：
- **介词短语（对象）**: For older workers（对年长的劳动者而言）
- **非限制性定语从句**: who came of age during periods of economic instability and were socialized to value stability above all（他们在经济不稳定时期长大，被社会化地看重稳定高于一切）
- **主句**: this reordering of priorities can be difficult to understand（这种优先级的重新排序可能难以理解）

**翻译**: 对于在经济不稳定时期成年、被社会化地看重稳定高于一切的年长劳动者来说，这种优先级的重新排序可能难以理解。

### 句子2

> Growing up with access to unprecedented amounts of information about the world's problems — climate change, inequality, social injustice — many younger workers feel a sense of urgency that earlier generations did not, and they expect their work to reflect that urgency.

**拆分**：
- **现在分词短语作状语**: Growing up with access to unprecedented amounts of information about the world's problems（在成长过程中接触到关于世界问题的空前信息量）
- **插入语**: climate change, inequality, social injustice
- **分句1**: many younger workers feel a sense of urgency that earlier generations did not（许多年轻工作者感受到了前几代人所没有的紧迫感）
- **分句2**: they expect their work to reflect that urgency（他们期望工作能反映这种紧迫感）

**翻译**: 在成长过程中接触到关于世界问题的空前信息量——气候变化、不平等、社会不公——许多年轻工作者感受到了前几代人所没有的紧迫感，并期望工作能反映这种紧迫感。

## 文章大意

本文讨论了代际工作态度的差异。千禧一代和Z世代将目的感、灵活性和工作生活平衡置于薪水和稳定之上，这与前辈的价值观截然不同。原因包括经济环境变化（传统承诺不再可靠）、技术发展（远程工作和零工经济）以及价值观转变（对工作意义感的期望）。适应灵活性和使命感的公司更能吸引年轻人才，而这种代际差异反映的是个人与工作关系的真正变革。

## 思考题

1. According to the passage, what is a key factor that weakened younger workers' trust in the traditional career path?
   - A) Their lack of education and skills
   - B) Experiencing economic instability, such as the 2008 financial crisis
   - C) Their preference for working alone rather than in teams
   - D) Government regulations preventing permanent employment

2. How do some critics characterize younger workers' expectations for meaningful work?
   - A) As a sign of strong work ethic
   - B) As idealism or a sense of entitlement
   - C) As a realistic assessment of the job market
   - D) As a temporary phase that will pass

3. What does the author conclude about the generational divide at work?
   - A) It is a trivial issue that will resolve itself naturally
   - B) It reflects a genuine transformation in the relationship between individuals and their work
   - C) It proves that older workers are always right
   - D) It can only be solved by raising salaries for younger workers

<summary>参考答案</summary>

1. **B** — 文中指出许多年轻工作者在2008年金融危机期间或之后进入就业市场，发现"the traditional promises...no longer seemed reliable"，传统承诺不再可靠。

2. **B** — 文中提到对工作意义感的期望"sometimes dismissed as idealism or entitlement"，有时被斥为理想主义或特权感。

3. **B** — 作者在末段指出代际差异"reflects a genuine transformation in the relationship between individuals and their work"，即个人与工作关系的真正变革。
