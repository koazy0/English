# Pope Leo Schooled the Tech Bros on Tolkien

---

When Pope Leo XIV released his first encyclical on artificial intelligence, the technology world expected the usual: vague appeals to human dignity, carefully hedged language about both promise and peril, and a conclusion that would offend no one and change nothing. What they got instead was a document that drew on J.R.R. Tolkien to deliver a pointed critique of Silicon Valley's most cherished assumptions — and it landed with a force that surprised even seasoned Vatican observers.

The encyclical, titled "Magnifica Humanitas," does not begin with technology. It begins with a meditation on Tolkien's concept of the "Gift of Men" — the idea, central to the legendarium of Middle-earth, that human mortality is not a curse but a divine gift, the very thing that gives human life its urgency, beauty, and meaning. The elves, Tolkien wrote, are bound to the world forever, and their immortality becomes a burden, a kind of endless stagnation. Humans, by contrast, are granted a finite span, and it is this finitude that makes their choices consequential and their love profound.

Pope Leo uses this framework to challenge the transhumanist aspirations that animate much of Silicon Valley. The desire to extend life indefinitely, to upload consciousness, to replace human judgment with algorithmic optimization — these are not, the Pope argues, acts of liberation but acts of evasion. They represent a refusal to accept the fundamental condition of human existence: that we are limited, that our limitations are the source of our dignity, and that to erase them is to erase something essential about what it means to be human.

The encyclical draws explicitly on Catholic social teaching, particularly the principle of subsidiarity — the idea that decisions should be made at the most local level possible, by the people most affected. Applied to AI, this principle suggests that algorithms should not override human judgment in matters that affect individuals and communities, and that the concentration of decision-making power in the hands of a few technology companies is contrary to human flourishing.

What made the encyclical resonate beyond the usual Vatican audience was its cultural fluency. By invoking Tolkien — a Catholic author whose works are beloved far beyond the Church — Pope Leo found a language that spoke to technologists in terms they could not easily dismiss. You do not have to be Catholic to understand why the Gift of Men is a powerful counterargument to the fantasy of digital immortality. You do not have to be religious to see that a society that treats human imperfection as a bug to be fixed rather than a feature to be cherished has lost something important.

The tech industry's response has been divided. Some leaders praised the encyclical as a necessary corrective. Others dismissed it as technologically naive. But the very fact that the Pope of the Catholic Church is engaged in substantive dialogue about AI ethics — and doing so with references that Silicon Valley actually understands — marks a shift in the conversation that neither side can afford to ignore.

## 长难句解析

1. The desire to extend life indefinitely, to upload consciousness, to replace human judgment with algorithmic optimization — these are not, the Pope argues, acts of liberation but acts of evasion.
   - **解析**：三个不定式短语并列作 "The desire" 的同位语，破折号后 these 指代这些欲望，"the Pope argues" 是插入语，"not... but..." 结构表明教皇的核心论点：这些不是解放而是逃避——逃避人类有限性这一基本条件。

2. You do not have to be religious to see that a society that treats human imperfection as a bug to be fixed rather than a feature to be cherished has lost something important.
   - **解析**：不定式 "to see" 表结果，that 引导宾语从句，内含 that 引导的定语从句修饰 society，"rather than a feature to be cherished" 与 "a bug to be fixed" 对比，用软件术语类比暗示将人类不完美视为需修复的缺陷是根本性错误。

## 文章大意

教皇利奥十四世的AI通谕出人意料地援引托尔金的"人类的礼物"概念——死亡是神圣恩赐，有限性赋予生命意义和紧迫性——来挑战硅谷的超人类主义理想。通谕将延长寿命、上传意识、用算法替代人类判断的欲望定义为"逃避"而非"解放"。通过天主教社会教导中的辅助性原则，教皇主张算法不应取代人类决策。引用托尔金使通谕超越了宗教语境，以科技界能理解的语言展开对话，引发业界分化回应。
