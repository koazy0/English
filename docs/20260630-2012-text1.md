## 正文

When we speak of gender inequality in the workplace, the conversation typically turns to the glass ceiling — the invisible barrier that prevents women from reaching the highest levels of corporate leadership. Yet this focus on the top of the occupational ladder obscures a more pervasive and in some ways more stubborn form of inequality: the persistent segregation of men and women into different types of work.

The phenomenon is known as occupational segregation, and it remains remarkably resistant to change. In most developed economies, women are concentrated in a narrow range of professions — nursing, teaching, social work, administrative support, and personal care — that are often described as "pink-collar" jobs. Men, meanwhile, dominate the better-paid sectors of engineering, construction, finance, and technology. The result is not merely a difference in the kind of work that men and women do; it is a difference in pay, prestige, and opportunity.

The reasons for this segregation are complex. Part of the explanation lies in socialization. From an early age, girls and boys are steered toward different interests and aspirations. Subtle messages from parents, teachers, and media — that girls are nurturing while boys are analytical, that women should prioritize family while men should prioritize career — shape educational choices that later become occupational realities. By the time young women enter the job market, many have already internalized a set of assumptions about what constitutes appropriate work for their gender.

But socialization alone cannot explain the persistence of occupational segregation. Institutional barriers also play a significant role. Many male-dominated professions have cultures that are actively hostile to women. Engineering firms, trading floors, and construction sites have all been documented as environments where women face harassment, exclusion, and a constant pressure to prove themselves. These conditions discourage women from entering such fields and drive many of those who do enter to leave.

There is also the question of how work itself is valued. Occupations that involve caring for others — the very kind of work that women have traditionally performed — are systematically undervalued in economic terms. Nursing, childcare, and eldercare are essential to the functioning of any society, yet they are among the lowest-paid and least prestigious professions. This is not a coincidence; it reflects a deeper cultural assumption that work associated with women is less skilled and therefore less worthy of compensation.

The consequences of occupational segregation extend beyond individual careers. When women are concentrated in low-paid sectors, the gender pay gap persists regardless of equal-pay legislation. When men dominate the highest-paying fields, economic inequality between the sexes is reinforced. And when entire professions are gendered, both men and women lose — men who might wish to enter caring professions face stigma, while women who aspire to traditionally male fields encounter resistance.

Addressing occupational segregation requires more than anti-discrimination laws. It demands a fundamental rethinking of how we value work, how we socialize our children, and how we structure our institutions. Until then, the pink-collar ghetto will remain one of the most durable — and most damaging — features of the modern economy.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> Yet this focus on the top of the occupational ladder obscures a more pervasive and in some ways more stubborn form of inequality: the persistent segregation of men and women into different types of work.

**拆分**：
- **主句**: this focus obscures a more pervasive and more stubborn form of inequality（这种关注遮蔽了一种更普遍、更顽固的不平等形式）
  - focus on the top of the occupational ladder 是主语
- **同位语**: the persistent segregation of men and women into different types of work（男女持续被分隔到不同类型的工作中）

**翻译**: 然而，这种对职业阶梯顶端的关注，遮蔽了一种更普遍、在某种程度上更顽固的不平等形式：男女持续被分隔到不同类型的工作中。

### 句子2

> Occupations that involve caring for others — the very kind of work that women have traditionally performed — are systematically undervalued in economic terms, and this reflects a deeper cultural assumption that work associated with women is less skilled and therefore less worthy of compensation.

**拆分**：
- **分句1**: Occupations are systematically undervalued in economic terms（职业在经济层面被系统性低估）
  - that involve caring for others 是定语从句
  - 破折号内同位语: the very kind of work that women have traditionally performed
- **分句2**: this reflects a deeper cultural assumption（这反映了一个更深层的文化假设）
  - that work associated with women is less skilled and therefore less worthy of compensation 是同位语从句，解释 assumption

**翻译**: 涉及照料他人的职业——正是女性传统上从事的那种工作——在经济层面被系统性地低估，这反映了一个更深层的文化假设：与女性相关的工作技能含量较低，因此不太值得补偿。

## 文章大意

本文讨论了职业性别隔离问题。人们常关注"玻璃天花板"，但更普遍且顽固的是男女被分隔到不同职业领域。女性集中在"粉领"低薪岗位，原因包括社会化、机构性障碍和文化对"女性工作"的低估。护理等传统女性职业在经济上被系统性低估，这反映了工作价值的文化偏见。职业隔离的后果远超个人层面，解决它需要重新审视我们如何评价工作、如何社会化儿童以及如何构建制度。

1. What does the author argue is a more stubborn form of gender inequality than the glass ceiling?
   - A) The gender pay gap in executive positions
   - B) The persistent segregation of men and women into different types of work
   - C) The lack of women in part-time employment
   - D) The unequal distribution of household chores

2. According to the passage, why are caring professions typically low-paid?
   - A) They require very little training or skill
   - B) They reflect a cultural assumption that work associated with women is less skilled
   - C) There is an oversupply of workers in these fields
   - D) The government sets low wage standards for them

3. What does the author suggest is necessary to address occupational segregation?
   - A) Simply passing more anti-discrimination laws
   - B) A fundamental rethinking of how work is valued, how children are socialized, and how institutions are structured
   - C) Encouraging all women to enter traditionally male professions
   - D) Eliminating all gender-specific job titles

2. **B** — 文中指出护理等职业被系统性低估，"reflects a deeper cultural assumption that work associated with women is less skilled and therefore less worthy of compensation"。

3. **B** — 作者在末段指出解决职业隔离需要"more than anti-discrimination laws"，需要"a fundamental rethinking of how we value work, how we socialize our children, and how we structure our institutions"。
