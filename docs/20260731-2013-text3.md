## 正文

We have all experienced it: the sudden flash of understanding that arrives without warning, solving a problem that has resisted hours of deliberate effort. Archimedes supposedly leapt from his bath shouting "Eureka!" The chemist August Kekulé claimed that the structure of the benzene ring came to him in a dream of a snake biting its own tail. These "eureka moments" feel magical — but science is revealing that they follow predictable patterns in the brain.

Neuroscientists have used brain-imaging technology to observe what happens during moments of creative insight. In a classic experimental setup, participants are given word puzzles that require them to find a single word that connects three seemingly unrelated words — for example, "cottage," "Swiss," and "cake," all linked by the word "cheese." When participants solved the puzzle through a sudden insight rather than step-by-step reasoning, scans showed a distinctive burst of activity in a region called the anterior superior temporal gyrus, located just above the right ear.

This region appears to play a crucial role in making remote associations — connecting ideas that are not obviously related. Importantly, this burst of activity was detectable up to eight seconds before the participant reported the answer, suggesting that the brain had already found the solution before the conscious mind became aware of it. The "aha moment" we experience is not the moment of discovery but the moment of awareness — the instant when an unconscious process surfaces into consciousness.

What triggers these moments? One key factor is what psychologists call "incubation" — a period of not thinking about the problem. Studies consistently show that stepping away from a difficult task improves the likelihood of a creative breakthrough. When we focus intensely on a problem, we tend to follow familiar mental pathways, which reinforces existing assumptions and makes it harder to see alternative solutions. Taking a break allows these well-worn paths to fade, freeing the brain to explore more distant connections.

Mood also matters. Research by Mark Beeman, one of the leading scholars of insight, found that participants who watched a short comedy clip before tackling a puzzle were significantly more likely to solve it through sudden insight. A positive mood appears to broaden attention, making it easier to notice peripheral connections that a narrower, more anxious focus would miss.

These findings have practical implications. They suggest that creativity is not a rare gift bestowed on a fortunate few but a capacity that can be cultivated. Taking regular breaks, cultivating a positive mood, and allowing time for ideas to incubate are not signs of laziness; they are strategies that work with — rather than against — the brain's natural creative processes. The light bulb moment, it turns out, needs time in the dark before it can shine.

## 长难句解析

## 文章大意

本文探讨"顿悟"的神经科学机制。文章先以阿基米德和凯库勒的经典案例引入"灵光一闪"现象；然后介绍神经科学研究，发现顿悟时大脑右耳上方的前上颞回会出现特征性活动，且大脑在意识觉察之前已完成解题；接着分析两个触发因素——"酝酿期"（暂时搁置问题有助于突破）和积极情绪（拓宽注意力范围）；最后指出创造力并非罕见天赋，而是可以通过策略培养的能力，顿悟需要在黑暗中酝酿才能闪亮。

## 思考题

**1.** What did brain scans reveal about the timing of creative insight?

A. The brain solves the problem at the exact moment of conscious awareness
B. The brain finds the solution before the person consciously realises it
C. Creative insight always occurs in the left hemisphere of the brain
D. Brain activity stops completely during moments of insight

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章第三段指出大脑活动爆发"up to eight seconds before the participant reported the answer"，说明大脑在意识觉察之前就已找到答案。</details>

**2.** Why does stepping away from a problem help creative breakthroughs?

A. It allows the brain to focus more intensely on the problem
B. It reduces stress that blocks creative thinking
C. It weakens familiar mental pathways, freeing the brain to explore new connections
D. It gives the brain time to forget the problem entirely

<details><summary>答案</summary>C。文章第四段解释暂时搁置问题时，"these well-worn paths to fade, freeing the brain to explore more distant connections"，即弱化既有思维路径以探索新联系。</details>

**3.** What can be inferred about creativity from the passage?

A. It is an innate talent that cannot be developed through practice
B. It relies primarily on intense and prolonged concentration
C. It can be enhanced by conditions that promote remote associations
D. It is only experienced by exceptional individuals like scientists

<details><summary>答案</summary>C。文章末段明确指出创造力"can be cultivated"，休息、积极情绪和酝酿期都是促进远距离联想(remote associations)的策略，因此C正确。</details>
