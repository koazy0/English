## 正文

A century ago, Americans worried about the power of Standard Oil and U.S. Steel. Today, the concern has shifted to a new set of giants. The five largest technology companies — Apple, Amazon, Google, Meta, and Microsoft — have a combined market value exceeding 10 trillion dollars, more than the GDP of Japan. Their products are used by billions of people every day. Their influence on the economy, politics, and culture is without precedent. The question of how to manage corporate power has returned to the centre of public debate.

The scale of these corporations is difficult to comprehend. Google processes over 8 billion searches per day and controls roughly 90 percent of the search market in many countries. Amazon accounts for more than 40 percent of all online retail sales in the United States. Apple captures over 80 percent of global smartphone industry profits despite selling fewer than a quarter of all devices. These are not merely large companies; they are market-dominating entities whose decisions shape the opportunities available to millions of smaller businesses.

Market dominance brings familiar problems. When a single platform controls access to customers, it can set terms that favour its own products over those of competitors. Amazon has been accused of using data from third-party sellers on its platform to launch competing products of its own. Google has faced billions in fines from European regulators for prioritising its own services in search results. App developers complain that Apple's App Store policies are arbitrary and punitive, yet they have no alternative platform to reach iPhone users.

The political influence of these companies compounds the problem. Technology giants spend hundreds of millions of dollars annually on lobbying. They fund research at leading universities and think tanks that shapes the policy conversation. Their employees cycle through government positions, creating what critics call a "revolving door" between Silicon Valley and Washington. This influence makes meaningful regulation difficult: the companies being regulated have significant power over the regulators.

New challenges arise from the control of data. The tech giants possess vast troves of information about consumers — their preferences, habits, social connections, and even physical movements. This data creates enormous barriers to entry for potential competitors, who cannot replicate the accumulated knowledge that years of user engagement have produced. It also raises concerns about surveillance and the potential for abuse of personal information.

What can be done? Traditional antitrust tools — breaking up monopolies, blocking mergers — are one option, but they may be poorly suited to the digital economy, where network effects make size beneficial to consumers in many ways. New approaches may be needed: data portability rules that reduce switching costs, interoperability requirements that prevent platforms from locking users in, and updated competition laws that account for the particular dynamics of digital markets.

The power of large corporations is not a new problem, but the scale and nature of today's tech giants demand a fresh response. Failing to act risks allowing a small number of unelected companies to govern ever larger aspects of our lives.

## 长难句解析

**1.** "These are not merely large companies; they are market-dominating entities whose decisions shape the opportunities available to millions of smaller businesses."

- **结构**: 分号连接两个并列分句；后句含whose引导的定语从句修饰entities；available to millions of smaller businesses为形容词短语后置修饰opportunities
- **翻译**: 这些不仅仅是大型公司；它们是主导市场的实体，其决策塑造了数百万小型企业可获得的机遇

**2.** "Traditional antitrust tools — breaking up monopolies, blocking mergers — are one option, but they may be poorly suited to the digital economy, where network effects make size beneficial to consumers in many ways."

- **结构**: but连接两个并列分句构成转折；破折号间为插入语解释传统工具；where引导定语从句修饰digital economy，其中make size beneficial为make + 宾语 + 宾补结构
- **翻译**: 传统的反垄断工具——拆分垄断企业、阻止合并——是一种选择，但它们可能不太适合数字经济，因为在数字经济中网络效应使规模在许多方面对消费者有利

## 文章大意

本文讨论大型企业尤其是科技巨头的过度集中权力。文章先描述五大科技公司的市值和影响力，指出其规模史无前例；然后列举市场主导地位带来的问题——利用平台优势偏袒自身产品、设置不公平条款；再分析政治游说和旋转门效应使监管困难；接着指出数据控制创造竞争壁垒和监控隐患；最后讨论传统反垄断工具的局限性和新方法的需求（数据可携带、互操作性要求等），强调不采取行动的风险。

## 思考题

**1.** What problem does the author identify with Amazon's use of third-party seller data?

A. It violates international trade agreements
B. Amazon uses the data to create competing products of its own
C. It leads to higher prices for consumers
D. Amazon sells the data to other retailers

**2.** Why does the author mention the "revolving door" between Silicon Valley and Washington?

A. To praise the cooperation between government and technology sectors
B. To illustrate how tech companies gain political influence that hinders regulation
C. To suggest that government employees are more competent than industry employees
D. To argue that technology companies should not be allowed to hire former officials

**3.** What does the passage suggest about traditional antitrust tools in the digital economy?

A. They are perfectly suited to address current monopoly problems
B. They may be inadequate because network effects make size beneficial in some ways
C. They should be applied more aggressively to all large corporations
D. They have already been successfully used to break up tech giants

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章第六段指出传统工具"may be poorly suited to the digital economy, where network effects make size beneficial to consumers in many ways"，暗示网络效应使规模在某些方面对消费者有利，传统工具可能不够适用。</details>
