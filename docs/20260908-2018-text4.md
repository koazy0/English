# 2018 Text 4 — 深度脱碳的挑战

## 正文

The world has made progress on climate change. Renewable energy is booming, electric vehicles are entering the mainstream, and global awareness has never been higher. Yet for all this momentum, one uncomfortable fact remains: carbon emissions continue to rise. After a brief plateau between 2014 and 2016, global CO₂ emissions climbed to record levels in 2023. The gap between what governments promise and what they deliver grows wider each year. Deep decarbonisation — the radical reduction of emissions needed to limit warming to 1.5 degrees — remains stubbornly out of reach.

Why is decarbonisation so difficult? The answer lies in the sheer scale of the transformation required. The global energy system — powered by fossil fuels for over two centuries — underpins virtually every aspect of modern life. Electricity, heating, transport, industry, and agriculture all depend on carbon-intensive processes. Replacing this infrastructure is not a matter of switching one fuel for another; it requires rebuilding the foundations of industrial civilisation. The International Energy Agency estimates that achieving net-zero emissions by 2050 will demand annual clean-energy investment of roughly 4.5 trillion dollars — more than triple the current level.

Technical barriers compound the challenge. The most stubborn emissions come from sectors that are exceptionally hard to decarbonise. Steel and cement production, which together account for roughly 15 percent of global emissions, rely on chemical processes that release CO₂ as a fundamental byproduct. Long-haul aviation and shipping lack viable low-carbon alternatives. Agriculture, which contributes roughly a quarter of all greenhouse gases, involves biological processes that cannot simply be electrified. For these so-called "hard-to-abate" sectors, solutions exist only at the experimental stage and remain far from commercial deployment.

Political obstacles are equally formidable. Fossil fuels remain central to the economies of many nations, providing not only energy but also revenue, employment, and geopolitical influence. Countries like Russia, Saudi Arabia, and Australia derive significant portions of their national income from fossil fuel exports. Transitioning away from coal, oil, and gas threatens powerful vested interests that resist change with considerable resources. Meanwhile, developing nations argue that they should not be expected to sacrifice economic growth for climate goals that were largely caused by the industrialised world. The principle of "common but differentiated responsibilities" — a cornerstone of international climate diplomacy — has proved easier to agree on than to implement.

Even within countries that champion climate action, public support wavers when costs become tangible. Carbon taxes have sparked protests from France's gilets jaunes to Australia's mining communities. When fuel prices rise or energy-intensive industries face closure, voters demand relief, and politicians often retreat. The tension between long-term necessity and short-term political survival is the central dilemma of climate policy.

None of this makes decarbonisation impossible — but it does make it the hardest collective action problem humanity has ever faced. Progress will require not just better technology but also smarter politics: international cooperation that distributes costs fairly, domestic policies that protect vulnerable communities, and honest communication about the scale of sacrifice involved. The window for limiting warming to 1.5 degrees is closing rapidly. Whether we can squeeze through it remains deeply uncertain.

## 长难句解析

**1.** The global energy system — powered by fossil fuels for over two centuries — underpins virtually every aspect of modern life.

- **拆分**：主语 the global energy system，谓语 underpins，宾语 virtually every aspect of modern life；破折号之间为过去分词短语作后置定语，补充说明能源系统的特征。
- **翻译**：由化石燃料驱动了两个多世纪的全球能源系统，支撑着现代生活几乎方方面面。

**2.** The principle of "common but differentiated responsibilities" — a cornerstone of international climate diplomacy — has proved easier to agree on than to implement.

- **拆分**：主语 the principle of...，谓语 has proved，easier to agree on than to implement 为比较结构；破折号之间为同位语，解释该原则的地位。
- **翻译**："共同但有区别的责任"原则——国际气候外交的基石——已被证明更容易达成共识而非付诸实施。

## 文章大意

本文分析了深度脱碳面临的重重困难。尽管可再生能源快速发展，全球碳排放仍在攀升，与1.5度目标的差距越来越大。脱碳之所以困难，首先因为所需转型规模巨大——整个能源系统需要重建，年清洁能源投资需增至4.5万亿美元。其次，钢铁、水泥、航空、农业等"难减排"领域缺乏可行方案。政治上，化石燃料出口国抵制转型，发展中国家要求公平分担责任。即使在国内，碳税也引发民众抗议。脱碳是人类面临的最艰难的集体行动问题，需要更好的技术、更聪明的政治和诚实的沟通。
