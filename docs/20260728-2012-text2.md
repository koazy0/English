## 正文

Something remarkable happened when you visited that website this morning. Before the page had even finished loading, dozens of invisible trackers had already noted your arrival. They recorded your IP address, identified your device, and noted how long you lingered on each section. By the time you closed the tab, a detailed profile of your interests, habits, and likely purchasing intentions had been assembled and auctioned to the highest bidder — all in a fraction of a second.

This is the hidden economy of the internet. Every day, billions of data points are collected from users around the world. Search engines know what questions we ask. Social media platforms know who our friends are and what we discuss. Online retailers know what we browse, what we buy, and what we almost bought but did not. Individually, each data point seems trivial. Aggregated, they paint an astonishingly detailed portrait of our lives.

The convenience this data enables is genuine. Personalised recommendations help us discover products we might enjoy. Navigation apps use location data to guide us through traffic. Email services filter out spam and surface the messages that matter most. Few users would willingly return to the pre-internet era of phone directories and paper maps. The trade-off between convenience and privacy has, for most people, seemed acceptable.

But the terms of this trade-off are shifting in ways that consumers rarely notice. Data collection has become far more extensive and sophisticated than most users realise. Facial recognition technology can identify individuals in crowded public spaces. Smart speakers in our homes listen continuously for voice commands, raising questions about what else they might be recording. Fitness trackers monitor our heart rates, sleep patterns, and physical locations, creating intimate health records that exist outside the protections of medical privacy law.

The economic incentives favour ever-greater intrusion. The global digital advertising market, fuelled by personal data, is worth over 500 billion dollars annually. Companies that collect more data can deliver more precisely targeted advertisements and thus charge higher prices. This creates a relentless pressure to gather ever more personal information, with few meaningful constraints. Privacy policies — those lengthy documents nobody reads — offer only the illusion of informed consent.

The consequences extend beyond unwanted advertisements. Personal data can be used to discriminate in employment, insurance, and lending. It can be stolen by criminals or demanded by authoritarian governments. It can be combined and analysed to predict behaviour in ways that feel less like convenience and more like surveillance.

Reclaiming privacy in the digital age will not be easy. It requires robust legal frameworks that give individuals genuine control over their data. It requires technology companies to adopt privacy-by-design principles. And it requires citizens to understand that the true cost of "free" services is paid in a currency more valuable than money: personal autonomy.

## 文章大意

本文探讨互联网时代个人隐私面临的挑战。文章首先揭示用户浏览网页时数据被实时收集和拍卖的现象；然后说明数据聚合能描绘极为详细的个人画像；接着承认数据带来的便利是真实的，但指出收集的规模和精密程度已远超用户认知（人脸识别、智能音箱、健身追踪器）；再分析经济激励驱使数据侵入不断升级，隐私政策形同虚设；最后指出数据滥用可能导致歧视、犯罪和监控，呼吁通过法律框架、隐私设计和公民意识重建数字时代的隐私保护。

## 思考题

**1.** What does the "hidden economy" of the internet refer to?

A. The trade of physical goods through online platforms
B. The collection and sale of personal data for targeted advertising
C. The subscription fees users pay for digital services
D. The underground market for stolen credit card information

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章前两段描述了用户数据被实时收集、汇总和拍卖的过程，这就是互联网"隐性经济"的核心——个人数据的采集与交易。</details>

**2.** Why do companies have a "relentless pressure" to collect more personal data?

A. Because they are required by law to maintain detailed user records
B. Because more data enables more targeted ads that command higher prices
C. Because users demand increasingly personalised services
D. Because they need to protect users from cybercrime

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章第五段指出收集更多数据的公司能提供更精准的定向广告，从而收取更高价格，这创造了收集更多个人信息的"relentless pressure"。</details>

**3.** What is the author's view on the current "trade-off" between convenience and privacy?

A. It is balanced and fair to most consumers
B. It has shifted in favour of data collectors without users' full awareness
C. It should be abandoned in favour of complete privacy
D. It has improved significantly due to new regulations

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章第四段开头指出"the terms of this trade-off are shifting in ways that consumers rarely notice"，说明交易条件正在向数据收集者倾斜而消费者并不知情。</details>
