# 2014 Text 4 — 住房问题与政府政策

## 正文

When we talk about the housing crisis, we usually mean one thing: ordinary people can no longer afford to buy a home. In major cities across the world, house prices have risen far faster than wages, creating a generation of renters who may never own property. The problem is not merely inconvenient — it is reshaping the social fabric of entire communities.

In London, the average house now costs more than fourteen times the median salary. In New York, a typical apartment consumes over two-thirds of a teacher's income. Similar patterns can be observed in Sydney, Vancouver, and Beijing. The causes are complex: limited land supply, restrictive zoning laws, foreign investment, and historically low interest rates have all contributed to the surge. But the result is straightforward — homeownership, once a reasonable expectation for the middle class, has become a distant dream for many.

The consequences extend beyond individual disappointment. When young professionals cannot afford to live near their workplaces, cities suffer from longer commutes, heavier traffic, and greater carbon emissions. When teachers and nurses are priced out of neighbourhoods, public services deteriorate. When housing costs absorb the bulk of household income, families have less money to spend on education, health, and savings. In short, the housing crisis feeds into a broader cycle of inequality.

What should governments do? The question has produced fierce debate. One camp argues for aggressive intervention: building more social housing, imposing rent controls, and taxing empty properties and second homes. They point to countries like Austria, where nearly a quarter of the population lives in high-quality public housing, and to Singapore, where the government supplies the majority of homes. These models, they argue, prove that the state can guarantee decent housing without destroying market incentives.

The opposing camp warns that heavy intervention distorts markets and ultimately reduces supply. Rent controls, they argue, discourage landlords from maintaining properties and discourage developers from building new ones. When Vienna expanded its social housing programme, private construction slowed. When Berlin froze rents, available apartments actually shrank in number. The solution, this side contends, lies not in controlling prices but in freeing supply — simplifying planning regulations, releasing public land, and encouraging denser development around transport hubs.

Both sides make valid points, and the truth likely lies somewhere in between. What is clear, however, is that doing nothing is not an option. The housing crisis is not a temporary fluctuation; it is a structural problem that demands structural solutions. As more young people find themselves locked out of homeownership, the political pressure on governments will only intensify. The question is no longer whether to act, but how boldly.

## 长难句解析

**1.** When young professionals cannot afford to live near their workplaces, cities suffer from longer commutes, heavier traffic, and greater carbon emissions.

- **拆分**：When 引导时间状语从句，主句为 cities suffer from...，后接三个并列宾语 longer commutes, heavier traffic, and greater carbon emissions。
- **翻译**：当年轻的专业人士负担不起在工作地点附近居住时，城市就会面临更长的通勤时间、更严重的交通拥堵以及更大的碳排放。

**2.** The solution, this side contends, lies not in controlling prices but in freeing supply — simplifying planning regulations, releasing public land, and encouraging denser development around transport hubs.

- **拆分**：this side contends 为插入语；lies not in...but in... 为平行结构；破折号后三个动名词短语解释 freeing supply 的具体措施。
- **翻译**：这一方认为，解决方案不在于控制价格，而在于释放供给——简化规划法规、释放公共土地，并鼓励在交通枢纽周围进行更高密度的开发。

## 文章大意

本文讨论了全球住房危机及其影响。房价涨幅远超工资水平，使得中产阶级拥有住房的梦想变得遥不可及。住房危机不仅影响个人，还加剧了不平等：通勤变长、公共服务恶化、家庭可支配收入减少。关于政府是否应干预住房市场，存在两派观点：一派主张积极干预（建公共住房、租金管制），另一派则认为干预会扭曲市场、减少供给。文章认为，无论采取哪种方式，政府都不能无所作为，这是一场需要结构性解决方案的结构性危机。

## 思考题

**1.** According to the passage, the housing crisis leads to all of the following EXCEPT:

A. longer commuting times
B. reduced quality of public services
C. increased household savings
D. greater carbon emissions

**2.** The author mentions Vienna and Berlin primarily to:

A. demonstrate the success of rent control policies
B. illustrate the potential drawbacks of housing intervention
C. compare European and Asian housing models
D. show that private construction always benefits from government action

**3.** The author's attitude toward solving the housing crisis is:

A. pessimistic about any government action
B. firmly supportive of rent controls
C. convinced that market forces alone will solve the problem
D. advocating for action while acknowledging the complexity of solutions

<details>
<summary>🔑 点击查看答案</summary>

1. **C** — 文章明确提到通勤变长(A)、公共服务恶化(B)、碳排放增加(D)，但指出家庭用于教育、健康和储蓄的钱减少，而非储蓄增加。
2. **B** — 维也纳的例子说明公共住房扩张导致私人建设放缓，柏林的例子说明租金冻结导致可用公寓减少，都是说明干预的潜在弊端。
3. **D** — 作者认为双方观点都有道理，但不作为不是选项，主张需要行动但承认方案的复杂性。
