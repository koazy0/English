## 正文

Food experts are warning that Britain is "sleepwalking into a food crisis," as extreme weather, persistent inflation, and the ripple effects of the Iran conflict converge to threaten the nation's food security. The phrase, once dismissed as alarmist, is gaining uncomfortable credibility.

The numbers paint a sobering picture. Food prices in the United Kingdom are on track to be 50 per cent higher than they were five years ago, a surge that has disproportionately affected low-income households. Fresh produce has been hit especially hard: unseasonal floods destroyed winter vegetable crops across East Anglia, while drought conditions in Southern Europe reduced imports of fruit and salad staples that Britain depends upon during the colder months. The Iran war has compounded the problem by disrupting shipping routes through the Strait of Hormuz, raising fuel costs and delaying shipments of grain and fertiliser from the Middle East and beyond.

Yet the crisis is not solely the product of external shocks. Decades of policy choices have left British agriculture unusually vulnerable. Since the 1980s, the country's self-sufficiency in food production has declined steadily, from nearly 80 per cent to roughly 60 per cent today. Successive governments have prioritised cheap imports over domestic resilience, treating food security as a problem that global markets would always solve. The result is a supply chain that is lean, efficient in normal times, and dangerously fragile when disruptions occur.

Some commentators argue that market forces will correct the imbalance. Higher prices, they contend, will incentivise domestic production, encourage innovation in agritech, and ultimately restore stability. This is not impossible, but it is slow. New farms do not appear overnight, and the skilled agricultural workforce has been shrinking for years. Moreover, climate change ensures that the "normal times" on which efficient supply chains depend are becoming increasingly rare.

The most urgent need, according to food policy researchers, is a national strategy that treats food security with the same seriousness given to energy security. This would mean investing in resilient domestic production — greenhouse technology, vertical farming, drought-resistant crop varieties — while also diversifying import sources and building strategic reserves. It would mean recognising that food is not just another commodity governed by market logic, but a fundamental requirement for social stability.

Britain is not facing famine. But the gap between "not famine" and "affordable, reliable access to nutritious food for all" is narrowing. The question is whether the government will act before that gap closes entirely.

## 长难句解析

**1.** The Iran war has compounded the problem by disrupting shipping routes through the Strait of Hormuz, raising fuel costs and delaying shipments of grain and fertiliser from the Middle East and beyond.

- **主句**: The Iran war has compounded the problem
- **方式状语**: by disrupting shipping routes through the Strait of Hormuz
- **并列结果状语**: raising fuel costs and delaying shipments of grain and fertiliser from the Middle East and beyond（三个现在分词并列）
- **翻译**: 伊朗战争通过扰乱霍尔木兹海峡的航运路线加剧了问题，推高了燃料成本，延误了来自中东及其他地区的谷物和化肥运输。

**2.** It would mean recognising that food is not just another commodity governed by market logic, but a fundamental requirement for social stability.

- **主句**: It would mean recognising that...
- **宾语从句**: food is not just another commodity governed by market logic, but a fundamental requirement for social stability
- **结构**: not just... but... / governed by market logic（过去分词作后置定语修饰 commodity）
- **翻译**: 这意味着要认识到，粮食不仅仅是受市场逻辑支配的又一种商品，而是社会稳定的基本需求。

## 文章大意（中文概括）

本文警告英国正"梦游般走入食品危机"。极端天气、通胀和伊朗战争冲击导致食品价格五年上涨50%。但根本原因在于数十年政策选择：自给率从80%降至60%，过度依赖进口使供应链脆弱。虽然有人认为市场会自我修正，但气候变化使"正常时期"愈发稀少。专家呼吁制定国家粮食安全战略，投资国内韧性生产、多元化进口来源，承认粮食不只是商品，而是社会稳定的基石。

## 思考题

**1.** According to the passage, why is Britain's food supply chain particularly vulnerable?

A. Because the country produces more food than it can consume.

B. Because decades of policy prioritised cheap imports over domestic resilience.

C. Because British consumers refuse to pay higher food prices.

D. Because the government has banned all food imports from the Middle East.

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>B</b>。第三段明确指出"Successive governments have prioritised cheap imports over domestic resilience"，导致自给率下降、供应链脆弱。A与原文相反（自给率在下降）；C原文未提及消费者拒付高价；D原文未提及禁止进口。
</details>

**2.** The phrase "sleepwalking into a food crisis" suggests that Britain is ______.

A. deliberately creating a food shortage

B. unaware of the approaching danger

C. unable to produce any food domestically

D. experiencing a temporary price fluctuation

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>B</b>。"sleepwalk into"比喻在不知不觉中陷入危机，强调缺乏警觉和主动应对。A"蓄意制造"曲解原意；C过于绝对（自给率60%非零）；D"临时价格波动"轻描淡写了危机的严重性。
</details>

**3.** What does the author suggest about market forces in addressing the food crisis?

A. They will solve the problem quickly and efficiently.

B. They are entirely irrelevant to food security.

C. They may help eventually but are too slow for the urgency of the situation.

D. They have already restored stability to food prices.

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>C</b>。第五段承认市场力量可能起作用（"This is not impossible"），但指出"it is slow"，新农场不会一夜出现，而且气候变化使正常时期愈发稀少。A与原文矛盾；B过于绝对；D与事实相反。
</details>
