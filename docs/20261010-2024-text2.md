## 正文

The narrative around artificial intelligence and work has long been dominated by a simple question: will AI take your job? But as AI tools become embedded in everyday work, a more interesting and consequential question is emerging: how will AI change your job? The evidence so far suggests that the most significant impact of AI will not be wholesale replacement of workers but a fundamental transformation of what work involves — and what skills workers need to thrive.

The pattern is already visible across industries. In healthcare, AI diagnostic tools do not replace doctors but enable them to screen patients faster and focus on complex cases. In law, AI document review does not eliminate lawyers but frees them from hours of tedious searching, allowing more time for strategy and counselling. In software engineering, AI coding assistants do not write programmes independently but accelerate development by suggesting code, identifying bugs, and automating routine tasks. In each case, the technology handles the routine and predictable, while humans are left with the creative, the ambiguous, and the interpersonal.

This division of labour between humans and AI is not simply about offloading unpleasant tasks. It is reshaping the skill profiles that employers value. Routine cognitive skills — data entry, basic analysis, formulaic writing — are declining in importance. In their place, employers increasingly seek what might be called "complementarity skills": the ability to frame problems that AI can solve, to interpret AI outputs with critical judgement, and to communicate AI-generated insights to human audiences. Emotional intelligence, ethical reasoning, and cross-disciplinary thinking — areas where AI remains weak — are becoming more valuable, not less.

The transition will not be smooth. Many workers, particularly those in mid-career, have built their expertise on skills that AI is now eroding. A financial analyst who spent years mastering spreadsheet models may find that AI can produce the same analysis in seconds. A marketing copywriter who honed a craft over decades may discover that AI generates serviceable text in moments. The psychological impact of this devaluation should not be underestimated. For workers whose identity is tied to their professional competence, the prospect of being outperformed by a machine is deeply unsettling.

Education and training systems are poorly prepared for this shift. Most curricula still emphasise the routine cognitive skills that AI handles best, while giving insufficient attention to critical thinking, collaboration, and adaptability. Corporate training programmes tend to focus on specific technical tools rather than the broader capabilities needed to work alongside AI. Lifelong learning is widely endorsed in principle but poorly supported in practice, with most adult workers lacking the time, money, and institutional support to reskill.

The AI transformation of work is not a future scenario — it is happening now. The workers and organisations that will fare best are those that treat AI not as a threat to be resisted but as a tool to be mastered, and that invest in the distinctly human capabilities that no algorithm can replicate. The future belongs not to humans alone or machines alone, but to the humans who learn to work intelligently with machines.

## 长难句解析

**1.** In each case, the technology handles the routine and predictable, while humans are left with the creative, the ambiguous, and the interpersonal.

- **拆分**：while 连接两个对比分句；the routine and predictable 与 the creative, the ambiguous, and the interpersonal 均为 the + 形容词表示一类事物，形成工整的对比结构。
- **翻译**：在每种情况下，技术处理常规和可预测的工作，而人类则面对创造性的、模糊的和人际互动的任务。

**2.** The workers and organisations that will fare best are those that treat AI not as a threat to be resisted but as a tool to be mastered, and that invest in the distinctly human capabilities that no algorithm can replicate.

- **拆分**：两个 that 分别引导定语从句修饰 the workers and organisations；not as a threat...but as a tool... 为平行对比结构；that no algorithm can replicate 为定语从句修饰 capabilities。
- **翻译**：表现最好的工作者和组织是那些不把AI视为应抵制的威胁而是应掌握的工具，并投资于任何算法都无法复制的独特人类能力的人。

## 文章大意

本文探讨了AI如何改变工作性质。AI并非大规模替代工人，而是从根本上转变工作内容。在医疗、法律、软件工程等领域，AI处理常规任务，人类则负责创造性、模糊性和人际互动的工作。这正在重塑雇主看重的技能组合：常规认知技能在贬值，而"互补性技能"——设定问题、批判性判断AI输出、沟通洞察——日益重要。情商、伦理推理和跨学科思维变得更加宝贵。但转型不会顺利：中年工人的专业技能被AI侵蚀，教育培训体系仍偏重AI擅长的常规技能。文章主张AI时代的未来属于学会与机器智能协作的人。

**1.** According to the passage, "complementarity skills" include the ability to:

A. write computer programmes faster than AI
B. frame problems for AI, interpret AI outputs critically, and communicate insights

**2.** The author suggests that current education systems are inadequate because they:

A. focus too much on critical thinking and adaptability
B. overemphasise routine cognitive skills that AI handles best
C. have already fully integrated AI into all curricula
D. teach too many interpersonal skills

A. AI will completely replace human workers
B. humans and AI are in direct competition for all tasks
C. the future belongs to humans who learn to work effectively with AI
D. AI should be banned from the workplace
