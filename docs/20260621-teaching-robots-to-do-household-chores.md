## 正文

For decades, robots have been a fixture of factory floors, welding car parts and assembling electronics with tireless precision. Now, they are knocking on the front door. Start-ups and tech giants alike are racing to build robots that can fold laundry, load dishwashers, and tidy up living rooms. The promise is appealing: imagine coming home to a clean house without lifting a finger. Yet turning that promise into reality has proved far harder than anyone expected.

The difficulty begins with the sheer variety of homes. Factory robots operate in environments designed around them — every object in a predictable position, every movement rehearsed a thousand times. A household, by contrast, is a mess of unpredictability. Toys scatter across floors, chairs shift between rooms, and no two kitchens arrange their cabinets the same way. A robot that has learned to open one drawer may be completely baffled by another that looks almost identical but latches differently. Where factory work demands repetition, home work demands adaptation — a fundamentally harder problem.

Then there is the challenge of handling soft, deformable objects. Industrial robots excel at moving rigid items — metal panels, glass bottles — because such objects hold their shape and respond predictably to force. Clothing is nothing like that. A T-shirt can be crumpled, stretched, or inside-out, and each state demands a different grasp. Tangled sheets, damp towels, and half-zipped jackets present endless variations that no fixed set of instructions can cover. Researchers estimate that folding a single garment can require more decision-making than assembling a smartphone component.

Safety adds yet another layer of complexity. Factory robots are typically caged off from human workers, but a home robot must share space with children, pets, and elderly residents without the luxury of barriers. It must react instantly when a toddler stumbles into its path or a cat jumps onto the counter it is wiping. A single misjudgement could cause real harm. This requires sensors and software far more sophisticated than those found in industrial settings.

The good news is that progress is accelerating. AI models trained in simulated environments can now practise millions of trials before ever touching a real kitchen, dramatically reducing the time needed to learn new tasks. Better sensors and more flexible grippers are slowly closing the gap between clumsy prototypes and dependable helpers. Still, the distance between a robot that performs flawlessly in a laboratory and one that works reliably in a stranger's cluttered flat remains considerable. Researchers are right to be optimistic — but honesty demands they acknowledge that household robotics still has a long way to go before it truly delivers on its domestic dream.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> A robot that has learned to open one drawer may be completely baffled by another that looks almost identical but latches differently.

**拆分**：
- **主句**: A robot may be completely baffled by another（一个机器人可能会被另一个[抽屉]完全难住）
- **定语从句1**: that has learned to open one drawer（修饰主语 A robot，"已经学会打开某个抽屉的[机器人]"）
- **定语从句2**: that looks almost identical but latches differently（修饰 another（drawer），"看起来几乎一样但门扣方式不同的[抽屉]"）
- **并列结构**: looks... but latches...（由 but 连接两个谓语，形成转折）

**翻译**: 一个已经学会打开某个抽屉的机器人，可能会被另一个看起来几乎一模一样但门扣方式不同的抽屉完全难住。

### 句子2

> The distance between a robot that performs flawlessly in a laboratory and one that works reliably in a stranger's cluttered flat remains considerable.

**拆分**：
- **主句**: The distance remains considerable（差距仍然很大）
- **介词短语**: between a robot... and one...（限定 distance 的范围，"在……与……之间"）
- **定语从句1**: that performs flawlessly in a laboratory（修饰 a robot，"在实验室里表现完美的[机器人]"）
- **定语从句2**: that works reliably in a stranger's cluttered flat（修饰 one，代指另一个 robot，"在陌生人杂乱公寓里可靠工作的[机器人]"）

**翻译**: 一个在实验室里表现完美的机器人，与一个在陌生人杂乱公寓里可靠工作的机器人之间，差距仍然很大。

## 文章大意

本文探讨了教机器人做家务为何如此困难。机器人在工厂中早已普及，但进入家庭却步履维艰，原因有三：一是家庭环境不可预测，与工厂的标准化环境截然不同；二是家务涉及大量柔软、可变形的物品（如衣物），比工业机器人处理的刚性物体难操控得多；三是家用机器人必须与人类共享空间，对安全性要求极高。尽管AI模拟训练和传感器技术正在加速进步，但实验室中的完美表现与真实家庭中的可靠运行之间仍有很大差距。作者对此持审慎乐观态度——前景可期，但任重道远。

## 思考题

**1.** According to the passage, the fundamental difference between factory work and household work for robots is that ______.

A. factory tasks are physically more demanding than household tasks

B. household tasks require more electrical power than factory robots can provide

C. factory tasks rely on repetition while household tasks demand adaptation

D. household robots are cheaper to build than factory robots

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>C</b>。原文明确指出"Where factory work demands repetition, home work demands adaptation"，即工厂工作依赖重复，而家务工作需要适应能力，这是两者的根本区别。A原文未比较体力需求大小；B"electrical power"原文未提及；D价格比较原文同样未提及，属于无中生有。
</details>

**2.** Why are soft objects like clothing particularly challenging for robots?

A. They are too heavy for most robotic arms to lift.

B. They can exist in many different states, each requiring a different approach.

C. They are usually stored in places that robots cannot reach.

D. They cause robots' sensors to malfunction more easily than rigid objects.

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>B</b>。原文指出衣物可以"crumpled, stretched, or inside-out"，且"each state demands a different grasp"，说明柔软物体的多状态特性使机器人难以用固定方法处理。A原文未提及衣物太重；C"存储位置不可达"原文未提及；D传感器故障原文未提及，原文说的是安全和适应性问题，而非传感器失灵。
</details>

**3.** The author's attitude toward the future of household robots can best be described as ______.

A. dismissive — the technology is fundamentally flawed

B. enthusiastic — widespread adoption is just around the corner

C. cautiously optimistic — progress is real but significant gaps remain

D. indifferent — the topic is of little practical importance

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>C</b>。文章结尾明确写道"Researchers are right to be optimistic — but honesty demands they acknowledge that household robotics still has a long way to go"，体现了审慎乐观的态度：承认进步，但也不回避现实差距。A"dismissive"与原文的乐观态度矛盾；B"enthusiastic"忽略了作者对差距的强调；D"indifferent"与全文的认真分析不符。
</details>
