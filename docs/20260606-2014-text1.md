## 正文

Most people believe, as the old saying goes, that money cannot buy happiness. Yet a growing body of research suggests that the relationship between wealth and well-being is more nuanced than this simple maxim implies. Money can, in fact, buy happiness — but only if you spend it wisely.

The idea that money fails to improve well-being traces back to a famous 1974 study by economist Richard Easterlin, who found that beyond a certain level of income, additional wealth does not correspond to additional happiness. This finding, known as the Easterlin Paradox, has been widely cited as evidence that money and happiness are largely unrelated. However, more recent research has challenged this view. Studies now show that higher income is indeed associated with greater life satisfaction, though the relationship is not linear. Each additional dollar brings less happiness than the one before it, and the benefits of wealth diminish as income rises.

If the amount of money matters less than we assume, then how we spend it may matter more. A series of experiments conducted by psychologists Elizabeth Dunn and Michael Norton has demonstrated that people derive significantly more satisfaction from spending money on experiences — such as travel, concerts, and meals with friends — than on material possessions like clothing, electronics, or furniture. The reasons are revealing. Experiences become part of our identity; they connect us to other people; and, crucially, they are resistant to the process of hedonic adaptation, whereby the pleasure we derive from material goods fades quickly as we become accustomed to them.

Another powerful finding concerns the relationship between money and time. People who spend money to save time — by hiring a cleaner, paying for a shorter commute, or having groceries delivered — report significantly higher life satisfaction than those who do not. Yet many people are reluctant to make such purchases, even when they can afford them. The discomfort arises from a deeply held cultural belief that paying for convenience is wasteful or indulgent.

Finally, research consistently shows that giving money away — whether to charity or to friends and family — produces greater happiness than spending it on oneself. In one experiment, participants who were given money and told to spend it on others reported feeling significantly happier than those told to spend it on themselves, regardless of the amount involved.

What emerges from this research is not a rejection of the idea that happiness depends on more than money. It is, rather, a reminder that money is a tool, and like any tool, its value depends on how it is used. Spending wisely — on experiences, on time, and on others — may not guarantee happiness, but it is a good place to start.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> The idea that money fails to improve well-being traces back to a famous 1974 study by economist Richard Easterlin, who found that beyond a certain level of income, additional wealth does not correspond to additional happiness.

**拆分**：
- **主句**: The idea traces back to a famous 1974 study（这一观点可追溯到1974年的一项著名研究）
- **同位语从句**: that money fails to improve well-being（金钱不能改善幸福感）
- **定语从句**: who found that...（他发现……）
  - 宾语从句: beyond a certain level of income, additional wealth does not correspond to additional happiness

**翻译**: 认为金钱不能改善幸福感的观点可追溯到经济学家理查德·伊斯特林1974年的一项著名研究，他发现超过一定收入水平后，额外财富不会带来额外的幸福感。

### 句子2

> Experiences become part of our identity; they connect us to other people; and, crucially, they are resistant to the process of hedonic adaptation, whereby the pleasure we derive from material goods fades quickly as we become accustomed to them.

**拆分**：
- **并列分句1**: Experiences become part of our identity（经历成为我们身份的一部分）
- **并列分句2**: they connect us to other people（它们将我们与他人联系起来）
- **并列分句3**: they are resistant to the process of hedonic adaptation（它们能抵抗享乐适应的过程）
- **定语从句**: whereby the pleasure we derive from material goods fades quickly（由此我们从物质商品中获得的快感迅速消退）
- **原因状语从句**: as we become accustomed to them（随着我们对它们的习惯）

**翻译**: 经历成为我们身份的一部分；它们将我们与他人联系起来；而且至关重要的是，它们能抵抗享乐适应的过程——即我们从物质商品中获得的快感会随着习惯而迅速消退。

## 文章大意

本文讨论了金钱与幸福的关系。研究显示金钱确实能买来幸福感，但方式比人们想象的更微妙。将钱花在体验而非物质上、花钱节省时间、以及将钱赠予他人，都能带来更大的满足感。文章指出金钱是工具，其价值取决于使用方式，明智地消费——花在体验、时间和他人身上——是获得幸福的良好起点。

## 思考题

1. What does the Easterlin Paradox suggest about money and happiness?
   - A) Money and happiness are completely unrelated at all income levels
   - B) Additional wealth beyond a certain income level does not increase happiness proportionally
   - C) Poor people are always happier than rich people
   - D) Money is the only factor that determines happiness

2. According to the passage, why do experiences bring more satisfaction than material possessions?
   - A) Experiences are always cheaper than material goods
   - B) Experiences resist hedonic adaptation and become part of our identity
   - C) People enjoy showing off their experiences to others
   - D) Material goods are impossible to become accustomed to

3. What is the author's main message about money and happiness?
   - A) Money has no effect on happiness whatsoever
   - B) Only large amounts of money can produce happiness
   - C) How money is spent matters more than how much one earns
   - D) People should avoid spending money on themselves entirely

<details>
<summary>参考答案</summary>

1. **B** — 伊斯特林悖论指出"beyond a certain level of income, additional wealth does not correspond to additional happiness"，即超过一定收入水平后，额外财富不会带来额外的幸福感。

2. **B** — 文中列出三个原因：经历成为身份的一部分、连接他人、能抵抗享乐适应（"resistant to the process of hedonic adaptation"）。

3. **C** — 文章核心论点是"how we spend it may matter more"以及"money is a tool...its value depends on how it is used"，即花钱的方式比赚钱的多少更重要。
