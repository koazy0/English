# AI Keeps Inventing Fake Cases — and People Keep Believing Them

## 正文

When the Alabama Supreme Court sanctioned an attorney for filing legal briefs loaded with inaccurate citations, the details were alarming enough. The lawyer had used an AI tool to generate references, and several of the cited cases simply did not exist. What made the incident even more striking was what happened next: after receiving an explicit warning about these errors, the attorney cited "nonexistent cases at the very next sentence." It was not ignorance but misplaced trust that produced this behaviour.

This episode is far from isolated. A database maintained by legal researchers now lists more than 1,400 cases in which courts have addressed AI-related errors over the past three years. The pattern is remarkably consistent: people keep trusting AI's answers even when they know the systems can be wrong. Misplaced trust has real consequences — dismissed appeals, attorney fines, and fired journalists are just the visible tip of a much larger problem.

Why does this happen? Part of the answer lies in what researchers call "automation bias" — the tendency to favour suggestions from automated systems over contradictory evidence from human sources. But recent research reveals something more troubling. In a controlled study, participants were told that advice came from an AI system. Those who held positive attitudes toward AI performed worse than those with less favourable views. Intriguingly, no such effect appeared when the same advice was attributed to a human. As the researchers concluded, "AI guidance has a quite specific ability to engender biases." In other words, it is not merely that we trust machines too easily; it is that our prior enthusiasm for AI actively distorts our judgment.

Perhaps the most disturbing evidence comes from experiments involving drone warfare simulations. Participants who had made accurate assessments on their own reversed their correct judgments when a robot disagreed with them. They did not simply weigh the machine's input alongside their own — they abandoned what they knew to be right. This finding suggests that AI's influence goes beyond casual reliance. It can override well-founded convictions, even in high-stakes situations where errors carry severe consequences.

What, then, should be done? The first step is to recognise that AI-generated content always requires independent verification. Courts are already beginning to mandate disclosure when AI tools have been used in legal filings. Newsrooms are adopting stricter review protocols. But rules alone will not solve the problem if people continue to place unwarranted faith in AI outputs. The deeper challenge is cultural: we must learn to treat AI as a useful but unreliable assistant, not as an authority whose word we accept without question. Trust is earned, and machines have not yet earned ours.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> Those who held positive attitudes toward AI performed worse than those with less favourable views.

**拆分**：
- **主句**: Those performed worse than those（前者比后者表现更差）
- **定语从句**: who held positive attitudes toward AI（修饰主语 Those，"对AI持正面态度的[人]"）
- **介词短语**: with less favourable views（修饰比较对象 those，"持不太有利看法的[人]"）

**翻译**: 那些对AI持正面态度的人，比那些持不太有利看法的人表现更差。

### 句子2

> They did not simply weigh the machine's input alongside their own — they abandoned what they knew to be right.

**拆分**：
- **第一分句**: They did not simply weigh the machine's input alongside their own（他们并非仅仅将机器的输入与自己的判断进行权衡）
  - not simply... 表示"不仅仅是"，暗示后面有更极端的行为
- **第二分句**: they abandoned what they knew to be right（他们放弃了自认为正确的东西）
  - what 引导宾语从句，作 abandoned 的宾语
  - they knew to be right 是插入的定语，修饰 what

**翻译**: 他们并非仅仅将机器的输入与自己的判断加以权衡——他们直接放弃了自认为正确的东西。

## 文章大意

本文以阿拉巴马州最高法院处罚律师为切入点，揭示AI"编造虚假案例"的普遍现象。数据库记录显示，近三年超过1,400起案件涉及AI错误引用。研究发现，人们对AI的过度信任并非简单的"偷懒"，而是源于"自动化偏见"——对AI持积极态度的人反而更容易被误导，这种效应在人类建议中并不存在。无人机模拟实验更显示，人们会因机器的反对而放弃自己原本正确的判断。作者呼吁，应将AI视为有用但不可靠的助手，而非不加质疑的权威。

## 思考题

**1.** What does the Alabama case illustrate about people's trust in AI?

A. Lawyers are more likely to make mistakes than other professionals.

B. People tend to ignore AI errors even after being warned about them.

C. AI tools are deliberately designed to produce false citations.

D. Courts have been too slow to respond to AI-related problems.

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>B</b>。文中指出该律师在被明确警告后，"在紧接着的下一句就引用了不存在的案例"，说明人们即使知道AI可能出错仍继续信任它。A原文未提及律师比其他职业更容易出错；C"deliberately designed"与原文矛盾，AI并非故意编造；D虽有一定道理，但不是该案例所说明的核心问题——该案例强调的是人们对AI的错位信任。
</details>

**2.** The study mentioned in Paragraph 3 suggests that ______.

A. people consistently distrust AI advice more than human advice

B. prior positive views of AI make people more vulnerable to its errors

C. AI generates more accurate advice than human experts

D. automation bias only affects people with negative attitudes toward AI

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>B</b>。研究显示，对AI持正面态度的参与者在获得AI建议时表现更差，而同样的建议被归因于人类时没有此效应。这说明对AI的积极态度反而使人更容易受其错误影响（"engender biases"）。A与原文相反，人们更信任而非怀疑AI；C原文未比较AI和人类的准确性；D恰好说反了，受影响的是持正面态度的人。
</details>

**3.** The drone warfare experiment is mentioned to show that ______.

A. AI systems are especially unreliable in military contexts

B. people tend to weigh AI input carefully before changing their minds

C. AI's influence can cause people to abandon correct judgments even in high-stakes situations

D. robots are gradually replacing human decision-makers in warfare

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>C</b>。实验中参与者"reversed their correct judgments when a robot disagreed with them"，且"abandoned what they knew to be right"，说明AI的影响足以让人在高风险情境下放弃正确判断。A原文并非讨论军事场景中AI更不可靠，而是用军事场景说明信任问题的严重性；B与原文"abandoned"而非"weighed carefully"矛盾；D偷换概念，实验讨论的是信任问题而非替代问题。
</details>
