## 正文

For generations of American workers, retirement meant a simple calculation: work for one company for thirty years, collect a pension, and enjoy your golden years. That bargain, once the cornerstone of the American middle-class dream, has largely collapsed. In its place has emerged a retirement system that shifts risk from employers to individuals — and leaves millions facing a savings gap that may never be closed.

The shift began in the 1980s, when employers started moving from defined-benefit pensions to defined-contribution plans such as the 401(k). Under a defined-benefit plan, the employer guarantees a specific monthly payment based on salary and years of service. The employer bears the investment risk and the responsibility for ensuring sufficient funds. Under a defined-contribution plan, the employer — if it contributes at all — matches a portion of the employee's own savings, and the employee bears all the investment risk. What seemed like a flexible, portable arrangement has proved, for many, to be a precarious gamble.

The results are stark. According to the Federal Reserve, the median retirement savings for American families headed by someone aged 55 to 64 is just 134,000 dollars — far below what most financial advisors consider adequate. Nearly a quarter of workers in that age group have no retirement savings at all. The gap between what people have saved and what they will need is estimated at 7.1 trillion dollars. For lower-income workers, women, and minorities, the shortfall is especially severe, reflecting the broader inequalities of the labour market.

Several factors explain this crisis. First, not all employers offer retirement plans — roughly half of private-sector workers lack access to any employer-sponsored plan. Second, even when plans are available, many workers do not participate or contribute enough, often because they are struggling with immediate financial pressures such as housing costs and student debt. Third, the shift to defined-contribution plans means that individuals, most of whom have no training in investment, must make complex financial decisions that will determine their security decades later. The average worker is not a financial expert, yet the system treats them as though they were.

The consequences are already visible. More Americans are working past the traditional retirement age — not by choice, but by necessity. Among workers aged 65 and older, labour force participation has risen steadily, from 12 percent in 1990 to nearly 20 percent today. Many retirees rely heavily on Social Security, which was designed as a safety net, not a primary income source. The average Social Security benefit replaces only about 40 percent of pre-retirement earnings, well below the 70 to 80 percent that most experts recommend.

Fixing the retirement crisis will require systemic reform. Options include expanding access to employer-sponsored plans through automatic enrolment, strengthening Social Security benefits, and creating new savings vehicles for gig economy and part-time workers. The lesson of the past four decades is clear: when individuals are left to navigate retirement alone, most will fall short. Retirement security is not an individual problem — it is a social contract that must be renewed.

## 长难句解析

**1.** Under a defined-contribution plan, the employer — if it contributes at all — matches a portion of the employee's own savings, and the employee bears all the investment risk.

- **拆分**：主句为并列结构 the employer matches...and the employee bears...；破折号间 if it contributes at all 为条件状语从句，暗示雇主可能根本不缴费。
- **翻译**：在固定缴款计划下，雇主——如果缴费的话——匹配员工自身储蓄的一部分，而员工承担全部投资风险。

**2.** Third, the shift to defined-contribution plans means that individuals, most of whom have no training in investment, must make complex financial decisions that will determine their security decades later.

- **拆分**：that 引导宾语从句；most of whom 为非限制性定语从句修饰 individuals；第二个 that 引导定语从句修饰 decisions。
- **翻译**：第三，向固定缴款计划的转变意味着，大多数没有投资训练的个人必须做出将决定他们几十年后安全感的复杂财务决策。

## 文章大意

本文分析了美国养老金体系从固定收益向固定缴款转变所导致的退休危机。在固定收益计划下，雇主承担投资风险；而在401(k)等固定缴款计划下，风险转移到个人身上。结果严峻：55至64岁家庭的退休储蓄中位数仅13.4万美元，约四分之一的人完全没有退休储蓄，缺口估计达7.1万亿美元。原因包括雇主不提供计划、员工参与不足、个人缺乏投资知识。越来越多美国人因经济需要而延迟退休。文章呼吁系统性改革，强调退休安全不是个人问题，而是必须更新的社会契约。
