# 2012 Text 4 — 大衰退对普通人的影响

> 话题标签：#经济衰退 #就业 #社会保障 | 来源：真题 | 难度：★★★★☆ | 字数：~460 | 类型：📖 真题原文

## 正文

When the financial crisis of 2008 struck, its epicentre was Wall Street. But its most devastating effects were felt far from the trading floors and executive suites. The Great Recession, as it came to be known, destroyed millions of jobs, wiped out trillions in household wealth, and left scars that — more than a decade later — have still not fully healed. For ordinary workers, the crisis was not a temporary disruption; it was a life-altering catastrophe.

The numbers tell a grim story. In the United States alone, 8.7 million jobs disappeared between 2007 and 2010. The unemployment rate doubled, peaking at 10 percent in October 2009. But these headline figures understate the true damage. Millions more dropped out of the labour force entirely, no longer counted in official statistics. Others accepted part-time work when they needed full-time positions. Long-term unemployment — joblessness lasting six months or more — reached its highest level since records began, affecting over 6 million workers at its peak.

The impact on wealth was equally devastating. American households lost an estimated 16 trillion dollars in net worth between 2007 and 2009. Home values plummeted, and with them the retirement savings of millions of families. Those who lost their homes through foreclosure numbered in the millions, disproportionately from minority communities. Research has shown that the typical family took nearly a decade to recover its pre-crisis wealth — and many never did.

What made the Great Recession particularly cruel was its uneven distribution of pain. While millions of ordinary workers lost jobs and homes, the financial institutions whose reckless behaviour caused the crisis were bailed out by governments. Banks received hundreds of billions in public funds, and their executives continued to receive substantial bonuses. This perceived injustice fuelled a wave of popular anger that found expression in movements from the Occupy protests to the rise of populist politics on both the left and the right. Trust in institutions — government, banks, corporations — plummeted and has never fully recovered.

The recession also reshaped the labour market in lasting ways. Many middle-income jobs in manufacturing and administration were permanently eliminated, replaced — if at all — by lower-wage service positions or higher-skill roles requiring additional education. This "hollowing out" of the middle accelerated a trend toward inequality that had been building for decades. Workers with only a high school education saw their earnings stagnate or decline, while those with advanced degrees largely recovered and continued to gain. The recession did not create inequality, but it dramatically accelerated it.

The lesson of the Great Recession is that economic shocks do not affect everyone equally. Without strong social safety nets, progressive taxation, and active labour market policies, the costs of financial crises fall disproportionately on those least able to bear them. The next crisis is not a question of if, but when — and how well we have learned this lesson will determine who pays the price.

## 长难句解析

**1.** While millions of ordinary workers lost jobs and homes, the financial institutions whose reckless behaviour caused the crisis were bailed out by governments.

- **拆分**：While 引导让步状语从句，主句为 the financial institutions were bailed out；whose 引导定语从句修饰 institutions。
- **翻译**：当数百万普通工人失去工作和住房时，其鲁莽行为引发危机的金融机构却被政府救助。

**2.** The next crisis is not a question of if, but when — and how well we have learned this lesson will determine who pays the price.

- **拆分**：not...but... 结构表示"不是……而是……"；how well we have learned this lesson 为主语从句，will determine 为谓语，who pays the price 为宾语从句。
- **翻译**：下一次危机不是会不会来的问题，而是何时来的问题——而我们多大程度上吸取了这一教训将决定谁来付出代价。

## 文章大意

本文探讨了2008年大衰退对普通劳动者的深远影响。虽然危机始于华尔街，但最惨重的代价由普通工人承担：870万个工作岗位消失，家庭净资产损失16万亿美元，数百万家庭因止赎失去住房。衰退的痛苦分配极不均衡——金融机构获得政府救助，高管继续领取巨额奖金，而普通工人的恢复用了近十年。衰退还永久性地重塑了劳动力市场，中等收入岗位被消灭，加剧了不平等。文章的教训是：没有强大的社会保障网和积极政策，金融危机的代价将不成比例地落在最无力承受的人身上。

A. the Great Recession was a minor economic disturbance
B. financial institutions should not have been bailed out
