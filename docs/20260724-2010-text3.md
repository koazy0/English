## 正文

Advertising is everywhere. From the moment we wake up and check our phones to the evening commute home past glowing billboards, we are bombarded with messages designed to make us want things we never knew we needed. The average person is exposed to between 4,000 and 10,000 advertisements per day. The question is not whether advertising affects us — it clearly does — but how deeply it shapes our desires without our conscious awareness.

Modern advertising has evolved far beyond simple product announcements. Today's marketers draw on decades of psychological research to craft messages that bypass rational thought and appeal directly to our emotions. Advertisements associate products with happiness, success, love, and social acceptance. A soft drink is not merely a beverage; it is a symbol of youthful vitality. A luxury car is not just transport; it is a statement of personal achievement. The product itself becomes secondary to the feelings it promises to deliver.

One of the most powerful techniques is targeted advertising. Using vast amounts of personal data harvested from online activity, companies can deliver tailored messages to specific individuals at moments of maximum vulnerability. A person who has recently searched for diet plans may find their social media feeds filled with weight-loss products. Someone browsing for flights to a distant city will see hotel deals within minutes. This precision targeting makes advertising more effective — and, critics argue, more manipulative.

Children are particularly susceptible. Research shows that young children cannot distinguish between advertising and entertainment until roughly the age of eight. Food companies have been heavily criticised for using cartoon characters, toys, and games to market sugary cereals and snacks directly to children, contributing to rising rates of childhood obesity. Several European countries have banned advertising unhealthy food during children's television programmes, but online advertising remains largely unregulated.

The ethical concerns are profound. If advertising can create desires we did not previously have, and can target us when we are most susceptible, to what extent are our purchasing decisions truly free? Defenders argue that advertising merely informs consumers about available choices, and that adults are capable of resisting messages they do not wish to receive. Yet the billions spent on advertising each year suggest that companies themselves do not share this confidence in consumer rationality.

The challenge for societies is to strike a balance between allowing commercial speech and protecting citizens from manipulation. This requires not only sensible regulation but also greater public awareness of how advertising works. Understanding the techniques of persuasion is the first step towards genuine freedom of choice — a freedom that begins, paradoxically, with recognising how unfree we often are.

## 文章大意

本文探讨广告对消费者行为的深远影响。文章先描述广告无处不在的现状，指出问题不在于广告是否影响我们，而在于它在多大程度上无意识地塑造我们的欲望；然后分析现代广告的三种手段——情感联想、精准定向和针对儿童的营销；接着提出核心伦理问题：广告创造的欲望和精准推送是否削弱了消费选择的自由；最后呼吁通过监管和提高公众意识来平衡商业言论自由与防止操纵。

## 思考题

**1.** According to the passage, what makes modern advertising fundamentally different from simple product announcements?

A. It uses much larger budgets than in the past
B. It bypasses rational thought and appeals to emotions
C. It is regulated more strictly by governments
D. It focuses on product quality rather than image

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章第二段指出当今营销者利用心理学研究，制作"bypass rational thought and appeal directly to our emotions"的信息。</details>

**2.** Why are children considered particularly vulnerable to advertising?

A. They spend more time online than adults do
B. They are specifically targeted by luxury brands
C. They cannot distinguish advertising from entertainment until about age eight
D. They are more influenced by billboard advertisements than by online ads

<details><summary>答案</summary>C。文章第四段明确指出"young children cannot distinguish between advertising and entertainment until roughly the age of eight"。</details>

**3.** What does the author imply about the argument that advertising merely informs consumers?

A. It is fully supported by research evidence
B. It is contradicted by the enormous sums companies spend on advertising
C. It accurately describes how most consumers make decisions
D. It should be the basis for all future advertising regulation

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章第五段末句指出企业每年在广告上花费数十亿，暗示企业自己对消费者的理性并不那么有信心，从而反驳了"广告只是传递信息"的论点。</details>
