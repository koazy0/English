# Ferrari Faces Backlash Over Its First Electric Car "Luce"

| 项目 | 内容 |
|------|------|
| 日期 | 2026-08-22 |
| 类型 | 外刊精选 |
| 难度 | ★★★☆☆ |
| 来源 | The Guardian |

---

Ferrari, the Italian automaker synonymous with roaring V12 engines and the intoxicating smell of gasoline, has unveiled its first fully electric vehicle, provocatively named "Luce" — Italian for "light." The announcement has sent shockwaves through the automotive world, but not the kind Ferrari typically welcomes.

Purists are horrified. Online forums dedicated to the Prancing Horse have erupted in dismay, with one longtime collector calling the vehicle "a silent betrayal of everything Enzo stood for." The tifosi — Ferrari's famously passionate fan base — have flooded social media with lamentations about the loss of the marque's sonic identity. After all, Ferrari's engine notes are so carefully engineered that the company has even filed trademarks for specific exhaust sounds.

Yet Ferrari insists it had no choice. European regulations mandate that all new cars sold in the EU be zero-emission by 2035, and even the most defiant internal combustion engine cannot outrun legislation. Chief executive Benedetto Vigna framed Luce not as a surrender but as an evolution, arguing that Ferrari's DNA lies in performance and exclusivity, not in how that performance is achieved.

The backlash, however, reveals something deeper than mere nostalgia. It exposes the cultural friction at the heart of the green transition. When luxury brands go electric, they are not merely changing a powertrain; they are renegotiating the emotional contract with their customers. A Ferrari without its engine note is, for many enthusiasts, like a concert hall without music — technically functional but spiritually hollow.

Ferrari is not alone in navigating this tension. Porsche faced similar skepticism before the Taycan proved that electric performance cars could sell. Lamborghini has opted for plug-in hybrids as a transitional compromise. But Ferrari's move carries outsized symbolic weight because the brand has always sold not just speed but a particular kind of romantic, visceral experience — one that electricity, for now, struggles to replicate.

Luce will likely succeed commercially; Ferrari's waiting lists are immune to ideological debates. Whether it succeeds culturally is another matter entirely.

## 长难句解析

1. When luxury brands go electric, they are not merely changing a powertrain; they are renegotiating the emotional contract with their customers.
   - **解析**：分号连接两个并列分句。前句是 when 引导的时间状语从句 + 主句；后句用 "not merely... but..." 的变体强调深层含义。核心意思是电动化不仅是技术变更，更是品牌与客户情感关系的重新定义。
