## 正文

For centuries, humans have regarded empathy — the ability to understand and share the feelings of another — as a uniquely human trait. The capacity to feel concern for the suffering of others, we have long assumed, requires a level of cognitive sophistication that only our species possesses. But a landmark study published in the journal Science has challenged that assumption in the most unexpected way: by demonstrating that rats, of all creatures, display behavior that closely resembles empathy.

In the experiment, researchers placed pairs of rats in a divided enclosure. One rat was free to roam; the other was trapped in a restraining device that could be opened only from the outside. The free rat, upon discovering its trapped companion, would initially circle the device anxiously. Then, in the majority of cases, it would learn to open the door, releasing the trapped rat. This behavior was consistent and striking: most free rats opened the door within a few days, and some did so on the very first trial.

The researchers took care to rule out alternative explanations. Perhaps the free rat was simply curious about the door mechanism, or perhaps it wanted access to the space occupied by the trapped rat. To test these possibilities, the experiment was repeated with additional conditions. When the restraining device was empty, free rats showed little interest in opening the door. When the trapped rat was released into a separate area that the free rat could not access, the free rat still opened the door — even though it gained nothing from doing so. Most tellingly, when the researchers placed chocolate chips in the enclosure as a distraction, the free rats often chose to free their companions before eating the chocolate, and in some cases shared the treats afterwards.

These findings are significant for several reasons. First, they suggest that the roots of empathy may extend far deeper into the animal kingdom than previously thought. If rats — animals not generally associated with intelligence or compassion — can display empathetic behavior, then the capacity for empathy may be shared by a wide range of species. This has implications for how we treat animals, particularly those used in laboratory research.

Second, the study challenges the view that empathy requires complex cognitive abilities such as theory of mind — the understanding that others have thoughts and feelings different from one's own. The rats' behavior suggests that empathy may rest on simpler, more primitive mechanisms: an innate aversion to the distress of others and a basic drive to alleviate it.

Naturally, the study has its skeptics. Some researchers argue that the rats may have been motivated not by empathy but by a desire to end the distressing vocalizations of their trapped companions — a form of self-interest rather than genuine concern. The distinction matters, but it is harder to sustain in light of the rats' willingness to share chocolate after freeing their companions.

What the study ultimately demonstrates is that the boundaries between humans and other animals are less clear than we like to believe. Empathy may not be a uniquely human gift but a deeply rooted biological response — one that connects us to creatures we have long underestimated.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> The capacity to feel concern for the suffering of others, we have long assumed, requires a level of cognitive sophistication that only our species possesses.

**拆分**：
- **主句**: The capacity requires a level of cognitive sophistication（这种能力需要一定程度的认知精密性）
- **插入语**: we have long assumed（我们长期以来假设）
- **不定式短语作定语**: to feel concern for the suffering of others（对他人苦难感到关切的）
- **定语从句**: that only our species possesses（只有我们物种才拥有的）

**翻译**: 我们长期以来假设，对他人苦难感到关切的能力需要一种只有人类才拥有的认知精密水平。

### 句子2

> Most tellingly, when the researchers placed chocolate chips in the enclosure as a distraction, the free rats often chose to free their companions before eating the chocolate, and in some cases shared the treats afterwards.

**拆分**：
- **副词**: Most tellingly（最能说明问题的是）
- **时间状语从句**: when the researchers placed chocolate chips in the enclosure as a distraction（当研究人员在围栏里放入巧克力碎片作为干扰时）
- **主句**: the free rats often chose to free their companions before eating the chocolate（自由的老鼠通常选择先解救同伴再吃巧克力）
- **并列分句**: and in some cases shared the treats afterwards（在某些情况下之后还分享了食物）

**翻译**: 最能说明问题的是，当研究人员在围栏里放入巧克力碎片作为干扰时，自由的老鼠通常选择先解救同伴再吃巧克力，在某些情况下之后还分享了食物。

## 文章大意

本文介绍了一项关于老鼠共情行为的研究。实验显示，自由的老鼠会主动打开装置解救被困的同伴，即使不能从中获益，甚至在有巧克力干扰时仍优先解救同伴并分享食物。这表明共情能力可能比之前认为的更广泛存在于动物界，且不一定需要复杂认知能力。虽然有人质疑老鼠的动机，但研究表明共情可能是一种根深蒂固的生物反应，而非人类独有的天赋。

## 思考题

1. How did the researchers rule out the possibility that free rats opened the door out of curiosity?
   - A) By removing the door mechanism entirely
   - B) By showing that free rats rarely opened empty restraining devices
   - C) By using different types of animals instead of rats
   - D) By training the rats to fear the door mechanism

2. What is the significance of the finding that rats freed companions before eating chocolate?
   - A) It proves that rats do not like chocolate
   - B) It suggests that the rats' motivation went beyond self-interest
   - C) It shows that rats are always hungry
   - D) It demonstrates that rats are smarter than humans

3. What do some skeptics argue about the rats' behavior?
   - A) That the experiment was not conducted properly
   - B) That the rats may have been motivated by a desire to end distressing sounds rather than genuine empathy
   - C) That rats are incapable of any social behavior
   - D) That the chocolate was contaminated

<details>
<summary>参考答案</summary>

1. **B** — 文中指出"When the restraining device was empty, free rats showed little interest in opening the door"，说明如果只是出于好奇，空装置也应引起兴趣。

2. **B** — 老鼠在巧克力面前仍优先解救同伴，这表明它们的动机超越了自身利益（"a form of self-interest rather than genuine concern"的质疑难以成立）。

3. **B** — 怀疑者认为老鼠可能是为了结束被困同伴令人不安的叫声（"a desire to end the distressing vocalizations"），而非出于真正的共情。
