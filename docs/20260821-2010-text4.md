## 正文

The jury system is one of the oldest and most distinctive features of the common law tradition. The idea that ordinary citizens, rather than professional judges, should determine questions of fact in criminal and civil trials reflects a deep commitment to democratic participation in the administration of justice. Yet the system that was designed to protect individuals from the power of the state has itself become a subject of intense scrutiny.

The principle behind the jury system is straightforward. Twelve citizens, selected at random from the community, hear the evidence presented by both sides and render a verdict. The requirement of unanimity in criminal cases — or near-unanimity, depending on the jurisdiction — is intended to ensure that no one is convicted unless the evidence is compelling. The jury serves as a safeguard against overzealous prosecutors and arbitrary government action. It is, in the words of the legal scholar Patrick Devlin, "the lamp that shows that freedom lives."

In practice, however, juries often fall short of this ideal. One persistent problem is the composition of juries. Despite legal requirements for representative selection, many juries are not truly representative of the communities from which they are drawn. Racial minorities are frequently underrepresented, partly because of discriminatory practices in jury selection and partly because socioeconomic factors make it harder for some groups to serve. A defendant's fate should not depend on the colour of the jurors who happen to be empanelled, but research suggests that it sometimes does.

Jurors are also expected to be impartial, setting aside their biases and deciding solely on the evidence. This is a demanding standard. All human beings carry implicit biases — attitudes that influence their judgement without their conscious awareness. Studies using implicit association tests have shown that most people hold unconscious racial and gender biases. In a courtroom, these biases can affect how jurors evaluate the credibility of witnesses, the reliability of confessions, and the plausibility of arguments.

The complexity of modern trials poses another challenge. Many cases involve technical evidence — DNA analysis, financial records, digital forensics — that laypeople may struggle to understand. Jurors are not experts, and they are not always equipped to evaluate competing expert testimonies. The result is that verdicts may sometimes be influenced more by which side presents its case more persuasively than by the actual weight of the evidence.

Despite these shortcomings, the jury system retains strong support. The alternatives — trial by judge alone, trial by expert panel — carry their own risks, including the danger that a small, insular group of legal professionals might develop a narrow and institutional perspective. The occasional failure of juries is, for many observers, the price of a system that remains fundamentally democratic in character.

The challenge, then, is not to abolish the jury but to improve it. This means reforming jury selection to ensure genuine representativeness, providing jurors with clearer guidance on complex evidence, and acknowledging the reality of implicit bias rather than pretending it does not exist. Justice by peers is a noble ideal. Making it a reliable reality requires honest recognition of its imperfections.

## 长难句解析

**1.** "The idea that ordinary citizens, rather than professional judges, should determine questions of fact in criminal and civil trials reflects a deep commitment to democratic participation in the administration of justice."

- **结构**: 主句The idea reflects a deep commitment；that引导同位语从句解释idea的内容，其中rather than连接对比概念；to democratic participation为介词短语修饰commitment；in the administration of justice为介词短语修饰participation
- **翻译**: 由普通公民而非专业法官来决定刑事和民事审判中事实问题的理念，反映了对司法管理中民主参与的深层承诺

**2.** "The result is that verdicts may sometimes be influenced more by which side presents its case more persuasively than by the actual weight of the evidence."

- **结构**: that引导表语从句；从句中more...than...构成比较结构，比较的两个对象为which side presents its case more persuasively和the actual weight of the evidence
- **翻译**: 结果是，裁决有时可能更多地受哪一方更有说服力地陈述案情的影响，而非受证据实际份量的影响

## 文章大意

本文讨论陪审团制度的优缺点。文章先介绍陪审团制度的基本原则——普通公民而非专业法官裁决事实，一致裁决要求确保公正；然后分析三大现实问题：陪审团代表性不足（少数族裔被低估代表）、陪审员难以真正做到公正无私（隐性偏见影响判断）、现代案件的复杂性超出了普通人的理解能力；接着指出替代方案也有风险（法官独审可能视角狭隘）；最后提出改进建议——改革遴选确保代表性、提供专业证据指引、承认隐性偏见的现实。

**1.** What does Patrick Devlin mean by calling the jury "the lamp that shows that freedom lives"?

A. Juries illuminate the courtroom during trials
B. The jury system demonstrates that democratic freedom exists
C. Juries should be replaced by more modern technology
D. The jury's primary function is to educate the public about law

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。Devlin把陪审团比作"显示自由存在的明灯"，意指陪审团制度是民主自由存在的象征。</details>

**2.** According to the passage, why are racial minorities often underrepresented on juries?

A. They are legally excluded from jury service in most jurisdictions
B. They tend to refuse jury service when called
C. Discriminatory selection practices and socioeconomic barriers make participation harder
D. They are more likely to be disqualified for bias during selection

<details><summary>答案</summary>C。文章第三段指出少数族裔代表不足，部分因为"discriminatory practices in jury selection"，部分因为"socioeconomic factors make it harder for some groups to serve"。</details>

**3.** What does the author ultimately advocate regarding the jury system?

A. Complete replacement with a professional judge system
B. Abandoning the jury system for expert panels
C. Reforming the system while preserving its democratic character
D. Reducing the number of jurors required for a valid verdict

<details><summary>答案</summary>C。文章末段明确指出"The challenge, then, is not to abolish the jury but to improve it"，随后列举了具体改革建议，在保持其民主特征的同时进行改进。</details>
