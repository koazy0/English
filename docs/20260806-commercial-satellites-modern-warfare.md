## 正文

A quiet revolution in orbital surveillance is reshaping the conduct of modern warfare. Commercial satellites now image the entire planet every few hours, making near-real-time global surveillance available not just to intelligence agencies but to anyone willing to pay for the data. The strategic implications are only beginning to be understood.

The resolution capabilities of these commercial platforms have reached levels once reserved for military-grade systems. Vantor, a leading satellite imagery provider, can resolve objects the size of a cutting board from an orbit of several hundred kilometres. This means that troop movements, ship deployments, and the construction of military installations can be tracked with a precision that was impossible a decade ago. During the Ukraine conflict, commercial satellite images revealed Russian force build-ups before the invasion began, and have since provided continuous documentation of battlefield developments.

But the very transparency that makes commercial satellites valuable also creates new vulnerabilities. The technology is inherently dual-use: the same imagery that helps journalists verify reports of atrocities can help adversaries locate friendly forces. Unlike classified military satellites, commercial imagery is sold on the open market. A hostile government, a non-state actor, or even a well-funded private individual can purchase images of virtually any location on Earth. The democratization of surveillance has, in effect, eliminated the concept of operational secrecy for any activity visible from space.

Militaries are adapting, but the adaptation is uneasy. Camouflage and concealment techniques designed to fool human observers are often ineffective against multispectral sensors that detect thermal signatures, disturbed soil, and synthetic materials. Some armed forces have begun deploying decoys and inflatable replicas of vehicles and equipment — inexpensive countermeasures that can saturate an adversary's analytical capacity by creating too many potential targets to investigate. Others are experimenting with electronic warfare techniques that jam satellite communications during critical operations, though such measures are temporary and risk escalating tensions in space.

The deeper problem is analytical rather than technical. Satellites produce vastly more imagery than human analysts can review. Artificial intelligence systems trained to identify military assets are improving rapidly, but they remain prone to errors — mistaking a civilian truck convoy for a military column, or failing to detect concealed weapons systems. The race between detection and deception is now being waged as much in algorithms as in physical countermeasures.

The era of exclusive space-based surveillance is over. What replaces it will be defined by how effectively militaries can operate in a world where they are always being watched — and by the norms and agreements that govern how this unprecedented visibility is used.

## 长难句解析

**1.** The same imagery that helps journalists verify reports of atrocities can help adversaries locate friendly forces.

- **主句**: The same imagery can help adversaries locate friendly forces
- **定语从句**: that helps journalists verify reports of atrocities（修饰 imagery）
- **结构**: The same...that...can also...（同一事物具有双重作用）
- **翻译**: 帮助记者验证暴行报道的同一种图像，也可能帮助对手定位己方部队。

**2.** Camouflage and concealment techniques designed to fool human observers are often ineffective against multispectral sensors that detect thermal signatures, disturbed soil, and synthetic materials.

- **主句**: Camouflage and concealment techniques are often ineffective against multispectral sensors
- **过去分词短语**: designed to fool human observers（修饰 techniques）
- **定语从句**: that detect thermal signatures, disturbed soil, and synthetic materials（修饰 sensors）
- **翻译**: 设计用来欺骗人类观察者的伪装和隐蔽技术，往往对能够探测热信号、翻动土壤和合成材料的多光谱传感器无效。

## 文章大意（中文概括）

商业卫星正在重塑现代战争。它们每隔数小时就对全球成像，Vantor等公司的分辨率已能从轨道上识别砧板大小的物体。这种透明度带来了双重影响：既可用于揭露暴行和追踪军事动态，也可被任何购买者用来定位己方部队，终结了太空可见活动的作战保密概念。军方正在适应——部署诱饵、干扰卫星通信，但更深层的问题是分析能力：卫星图像远超人力处理极限，AI检测仍易出错。监测与欺骗的竞赛如今也在算法领域展开。

## 思考题

**1.** What does the passage identify as a key vulnerability created by commercial satellite imagery?

A. Satellites are too expensive for most countries to operate.

B. The imagery is available to anyone who can pay, eliminating operational secrecy.

C. Commercial satellites cannot achieve military-grade resolution.

D. Satellite images take too long to process to be useful in real time.

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>B</b>。第三段指出"commercial imagery is sold on the open market"，"The democratization of surveillance has, in effect, eliminated the concept of operational secrecy"。A原文未提及成本问题；C与原文Vantor高分辨率矛盾；D与"near-real-time"矛盾。
</details>

**2.** Why are traditional camouflage techniques often ineffective against modern satellites?

A. Because satellites move too quickly to be deceived.

B. Because multispectral sensors can detect features invisible to human observers.

C. Because military forces have abandoned camouflage entirely.

D. Because commercial satellites use radar instead of optical imaging.

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>B</b>。第五段指出"Camouflage and concealment techniques designed to fool human observers are often ineffective against multispectral sensors that detect thermal signatures, disturbed soil, and synthetic materials"，即多光谱传感器能探测人眼不可见的特征。A原文未提及速度；C与"adapting"矛盾；D原文未提及雷达vs光学。
</details>

**3.** According to the passage, the deeper problem with satellite surveillance is ______.

A. the cost of launching enough satellites

B. the inability to collect imagery at night

C. the analytical challenge of processing vastly more imagery than humans can review

D. the lack of international agreements on satellite use

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>C</b>。倒数第二段指出"The deeper problem is analytical rather than technical. Satellites produce vastly more imagery than human analysts can review."A是成本问题，非"deeper problem"；B原文未提及夜间限制；D国际协议在末段提及但非"deeper problem"。
</details>
