# Professional Fact-Checker Says AI Is Wrong Roughly Half the Time

---

At a time when AI-powered search engines are rapidly becoming the default way people find information online, a sobering study from the Tow Center for Digital Journalism at Columbia University has quantified what many suspected but few had rigorously demonstrated: AI search systems get the facts wrong more often than they get them right. The study, which evaluated multiple AI search products across hundreds of queries, found that more than 60 percent of AI-generated search responses contained factual inaccuracies. Even the best-performing model, Claude, achieved only 73 percent accuracy — meaning it was wrong roughly one in four times.

The findings are particularly alarming because AI search tools do not merely present information; they present it with an air of authority that can be indistinguishable from verified fact. Traditional search engines return a list of links, allowing users to evaluate sources, cross-reference claims, and exercise critical judgment. AI search tools synthesize information into confident, fluent paragraphs that read as though they were written by an expert. There are no links to evaluate, no sources to cross-reference, and no visible indicators of uncertainty. The medium itself conceals the fallibility of the message.

Google's AI Overviews, the feature that now appears at the top of many Google search results, was wrong approximately one-third of the time, according to the study. This is a lower error rate than most competitors, but it is worth considering the scale: Google processes approximately 8.5 billion searches per day. Even a one-third error rate means that billions of AI-generated answers containing factual errors are being served to users daily, many of whom will take them at face value because they come from Google.

The study also revealed a troubling pattern in how AI systems handle uncertainty. When the models did not know the correct answer, they rarely admitted it. Instead, they produced what the researchers called "fluent fabrications" — responses that were grammatically perfect, logically structured, and entirely wrong. These fabrications are more dangerous than obvious errors because they are harder to detect. A user who recognizes a blatant mistake can discount it; a user who reads a plausible-sounding but fabricated answer may internalize it as fact.

The implications extend beyond individual queries. As AI-generated content proliferates across the web, future AI models will increasingly train on text produced by earlier AI models, creating a feedback loop that one researcher described as "digital inbreeding." Each generation of errors compounds the next, and the distinction between original human knowledge and AI-generated distortion becomes progressively blurrier. If the current generation of AI search is wrong 40 to 60 percent of the time, the next generation, trained on its output, may be worse.

## 文章大意

哥伦比亚大学Tow中心的研究发现，AI搜索系统的事实错误率超过60%，即使是表现最好的Claude也仅有73%的准确率。AI搜索工具将信息综合为自信流畅的段落，没有来源链接和不确定性标识，掩盖了信息的不可靠性。当AI不知道正确答案时，它不承认而是生成"流利的编造"——语法完美但完全错误的回答。更令人担忧的是，AI生成内容的激增可能导致"数字近亲繁殖"：未来模型在前代AI产出上训练，错误不断累积。
