## 正文

The world is running out of fresh water. Although water covers more than seventy percent of the Earth's surface, less than three percent of it is fresh, and the vast majority of that is locked away in glaciers and ice caps. The fraction that is readily accessible — in rivers, lakes, and underground aquifers — amounts to less than one percent of the total. As populations grow, climates shift, and industrial demand expands, that sliver of usable water is coming under unprecedented strain.

The consequences are already visible. In cities from Cape Town to Chennai, taps have run dry for days or weeks at a time. Agricultural regions that once relied on reliable rainfall now face chronic shortages. Conflicts over water resources are intensifying in parts of Africa, the Middle East, and Central Asia. The United Nations has warned that by 2025, nearly two billion people will live in countries facing absolute water scarcity.

One obvious solution is to make more fresh water by removing salt from seawater — a process known as desalination. The technology is not new; the first large-scale desalination plants were built in the 1960s. Today, countries such as Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, and Israel derive a significant proportion of their drinking water from desalinated seawater. Saudi Arabia alone produces roughly a billion cubic meters of desalinated water each year.

But desalination has serious drawbacks. The most common method, reverse osmosis, is extremely energy-intensive. Pumping seawater through semi-permeable membranes at high pressure requires enormous amounts of electricity, most of which currently comes from fossil fuels. This creates a painful paradox: the process of producing fresh water, which is needed partly because of climate change, contributes to the very carbon emissions that are driving climate change.

There are environmental costs beyond carbon. Desalination plants produce brine — a concentrated salt solution — as a by-product. When this brine is discharged back into the sea, it can raise the salinity of surrounding waters, damaging marine ecosystems and threatening coastal fisheries. The intake pipes that draw seawater into the plants also trap and kill small marine organisms, further disrupting local ecology.

Researchers are working on alternatives. New membrane technologies promise to reduce energy consumption by up to thirty percent. Solar-powered desalination units, though still small in scale, offer the possibility of carbon-neutral water production. And some engineers are exploring ways to extract valuable minerals from brine, turning a waste product into a resource.

Yet technology alone will not solve the water crisis. Conservation remains the cheapest and most effective strategy. Fixing leaking pipes, irrigating crops more efficiently, and pricing water to reflect its true scarcity would all yield immediate benefits. In many countries, water is so heavily subsidized that consumers have little incentive to use less.

The search for fresh water, then, is not merely a technological challenge. It is a test of our ability to manage a finite resource wisely — and to recognize that, in the case of water, there is no substitute.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> This creates a painful paradox: the process of producing fresh water, which is needed partly because of climate change, contributes to the very carbon emissions that are driving climate change.

**拆分**：
- **主句**: This creates a painful paradox（这造成了一个令人痛苦的悖论）
- **同位语从句（解释paradox）**: the process of producing fresh water contributes to the very carbon emissions（生产淡水的过程反而助长了那些碳排放）
- **非限制性定语从句**: which is needed partly because of climate change（这一过程部分因气候变化而需要）
- **定语从句**: that are driving climate change（正在推动气候变化的）

**翻译**: 这造成了一个令人痛苦的悖论：生产淡水的过程——部分因气候变化而需要——反而助长了正在推动气候变化的碳排放。

### 句子2

> Fixing leaking pipes, irrigating crops more efficiently, and pricing water to reflect its true scarcity would all yield immediate benefits, and in many countries, water is so heavily subsidized that consumers have little incentive to use less.

**拆分**：
- **分句1主语（三个动名词短语并列）**: Fixing leaking pipes, irrigating crops more efficiently, and pricing water to reflect its true scarcity
- **分句1**: would all yield immediate benefits（都会产生即时收益）
- **分句2**: water is so heavily subsidized that consumers have little incentive to use less（水的补贴如此之高，以至于消费者几乎没有少用的动力）
  - so...that... 结果状语从句

**翻译**: 修复漏水管道、更高效地灌溉农作物以及根据水的真正稀缺程度定价，都会产生即时收益，而在许多国家，水的补贴如此之高，以至于消费者几乎没有少用的动力。

## 文章大意

本文讨论了全球淡水危机及海水淡化技术的利弊。全球可用水不到1%，随着人口和工业需求增长，水资源压力空前。海水淡化是显而易见的解决方案，但反渗透法能耗巨大且依赖化石燃料，造成恶性循环；此外排放的高盐度浓盐水也会危害海洋生态。新技术如新型膜和太阳能淡化正在发展中，但节约用水仍是最廉价有效的策略。作者认为解决水危机不仅是技术挑战，更是对人类管理有限资源智慧的考验。

   - A) It produces too much fresh water
   - B) The process needed to address climate-related water scarcity contributes to the carbon emissions driving climate change
   - C) Desalination plants are too cheap to operate
   - D) It makes seawater saltier than before

2. What environmental problem does brine discharge cause?
   - A) It reduces the temperature of surrounding waters
   - B) It raises salinity levels and damages marine ecosystems
   - C) It attracts too many fish to the area
   - D) It makes drinking water taste salty

3. What does the author argue is the most effective strategy for addressing the water crisis?
   - A) Building more desalination plants
   - B) Importing water from other countries
   - C) Conservation and more efficient use of existing water resources
   - D) Waiting for new technology to solve the problem
