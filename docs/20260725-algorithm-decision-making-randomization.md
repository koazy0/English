## 正文

When Google engineer Max Hawkins built an app that randomly selected his daily destinations, restaurants, and even the music he listened to, he was not merely experimenting with novelty. He was mounting a quiet rebellion against algorithmic predictability. In an age where recommendation engines anticipate our every preference, Hawkins argues that deliberate randomness is a form of resistance.

The science supports his intuition more than one might expect. Researchers studying human mobility have discovered that our daily movements are astonishingly predictable — up to 93 per cent of the time, a person's location can be guessed based on their past patterns. Algorithms exploit this regularity to serve targeted advertisements, optimise delivery routes, and suggest what we should buy, watch, or eat next. The convenience is undeniable, but so is the cost: a narrowing of experience that confines us within corridors of our own historical behaviour.

Hawkins's randomization apps work by injecting uncertainty into routines that have become rigidly deterministic. One app selects a random destination within a chosen radius; another shuffles his music library with no regard for listening history; a third picks restaurants based on geography alone, ignoring ratings and reviews entirely. The result, he reports, is not chaos but discovery. By surrendering the illusion of optimal choice, he encounters experiences that no algorithm would ever suggest — a hidden park, an unfamiliar cuisine, a conversation with a stranger.

Critics counter that most people lack the time or resources to embrace randomness so freely. A parent on a tight schedule cannot afford to wander; a traveller in an unfamiliar city may reasonably prefer vetted recommendations over blind chance. There is also the question of privilege: the ability to treat life as an experiment presupposes a baseline of security that many do not enjoy.

Yet the broader insight remains valuable even for those who cannot adopt Hawkins's approach wholesale. The optimization culture that dominates digital life trains us to equate the best outcome with the most predictable one. But predictability and quality are not the same thing. A life optimised for efficiency may be a life impoverished by repetition. Sometimes the most meaningful experiences arrive not because we chose them, but because we allowed room for surprise.

The challenge, then, is not to abandon algorithms entirely but to recognise their limits. They are extraordinarily good at giving us more of what we already like. What they cannot do is show us what we never knew we might love.

## 文章大意（中文概括）

本文介绍Google工程师Max Hawkins通过随机化应用来抵抗算法对日常决策的控制。研究表明人类行为93%可预测，算法利用这种规律性推荐内容，但也缩小了体验范围。Hawkins的随机化应用帮助他跳出常规，发现意想不到的体验。尽管批评者指出这需要一定特权，作者认为核心启示在于：最优不等于最可预测，留出惊喜空间比追求效率更有意义。

## 思考题

**1.** According to the passage, why does Hawkins build randomization apps?

A. To improve the accuracy of recommendation algorithms.

B. To protest against Google's data collection practices.

C. To resist the narrowing of experience caused by algorithmic predictability.

D. To prove that human mobility is less predictable than researchers claim.

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>C</b>。文章明确指出Hawkins是在对算法可预测性进行"quiet rebellion"（默默反叛），因为他认为算法将人们困在"corridors of our own historical behaviour"（自身历史行为的走廊）中，导致体验变窄。A与原文相反；B未提及抗议数据收集；D与原文93%可预测的结论相悖。
</details>

**2.** The word "vetted" in the fourth paragraph is closest in meaning to ______.

A. random

B. examined and approved

C. expensive

D. popular

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>B</b>。"vetted recommendations"与"blind chance"形成对比，指经过审查和认可的推荐。vet意为"审查、审核"，vetted即"审查过的"。A是反义；C、D与上下文无关。
</details>

**3.** What does the author suggest about algorithms at the end of the passage?

A. They should be abandoned entirely in favour of random choices.

B. They are useful but cannot introduce people to entirely new experiences.

C. They are equally good at both prediction and discovery.

D. They will eventually learn to suggest unexpected experiences.

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>B</b>。作者末段指出，算法"extraordinarily good at giving us more of what we already like"，但"cannot show us what we never knew we might love"，即算法擅长推荐已知偏好，但无法引导全新发现。A过于极端，与"not to abandon algorithms entirely"矛盾；C与原文不符；D原文未提及。
</details>
