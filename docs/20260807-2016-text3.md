## 正文

Something has changed in the way we read. Not what we read — the volume of words consumed has, if anything, increased — but how we read. We skim and scroll, bounce between tabs, and glance at headlines without clicking through. The book that once held us rapt for hours now struggles to hold our attention for more than a few pages. We are reading more than ever, but we are reading less deeply — and that shift carries consequences we are only beginning to understand.

The statistics are telling. Surveys in the United States and the United Kingdom show that the proportion of adults who read at least one book per year has declined steadily over the past two decades. Among young adults aged 18 to 24, the decline has been particularly sharp. The average American now spends less than seven minutes per day reading for pleasure — less time than they spend on social media in a single hour. Book sales, though recently boosted by social media trends, have not returned to their pre-digital peaks.

The causes are not mysterious. We live in an attention economy where every app, website, and platform competes fiercely for our focus. Notifications interrupt us dozens of times per day. Social media feeds deliver an endless stream of bite-sized content designed to be consumed in seconds. The smartphone, that constant companion, offers an alternative to sustained attention at the slightest flicker of boredom. In this environment, the patience required to read a 400-page book becomes a scarce resource.

What is lost when we stop reading deeply? Neuroscience offers some answers. Deep reading — the kind that a demanding novel or complex work of non-fiction requires — activates regions of the brain associated with empathy, abstract reasoning, and critical thinking. When we immerse ourselves in a novel, we mentally simulate the experiences of characters, strengthening our capacity to understand others' perspectives. When we follow a sustained argument across chapters, we practise the kind of logical reasoning that underpins informed citizenship.

Shallow reading, by contrast, tends to reinforce existing beliefs. Online, we gravitate towards articles that confirm what we already think. We share headlines without reading the articles beneath them. The speed of digital consumption leaves little room for reflection or doubt — qualities that deep reading cultivates.

Some scholars resist the pessimism. They point out that previous generations worried that the telephone, the radio, and television would destroy reading. They did not. Digital technologies, they argue, are creating new forms of literacy — interactive, multimedia, collaborative — that may be as valuable in their own way as the book.

This may be partly true. But it does not follow that deep reading is obsolete. Different modes of reading serve different cognitive functions, and the capacity for sustained attention that books demand remains uniquely valuable. The question is not whether we will abandon books entirely — we will not — but whether we will preserve the habit of deep reading as one option among many, or whether it will become a niche practice for a declining minority. The answer to that question will shape not only the future of publishing but the quality of our collective thinking.

## 长难句解析

**1.** "When we immerse ourselves in a novel, we mentally simulate the experiences of characters, strengthening our capacity to understand others' perspectives."

- **结构**: When引导时间状语从句；主句we mentally simulate the experiences of characters；strengthening引导现在分词短语作结果状语；to understand others' perspectives为不定式短语修饰capacity
- **翻译**: 当我们沉浸于小说时，我们在心理上模拟角色的经历，从而增强我们理解他人视角的能力

**2.** "The question is not whether we will abandon books entirely — we will not — but whether we will preserve the habit of deep reading as one option among many, or whether it will become a niche practice for a declining minority."

- **结构**: 主句The question is not whether...but whether...or whether...构成三重选择结构；两个破折号间为插入语，强调不会完全放弃书籍；三个whether从句均为表语从句
- **翻译**: 问题不在于我们是否会完全放弃书籍——我们不会——而在于我们是将深度阅读的习惯保留为众多选择之一，还是让它成为越来越少人参与的小众实践

## 文章大意

本文讨论深度阅读的衰退及其认知影响。文章先描述阅读方式的变化——从沉浸式变为浏览式；然后用统计数据说明读书人数的下降；接着分析原因——注意力经济、数字干扰和智能手机的诱惑；再从神经科学角度阐述深度阅读激活共情、抽象推理和批判性思维，而浅阅读则强化既有偏见；虽然有人认为新技术创造了新的读写形式，但深度阅读培养的持续注意力仍然独特且不可替代，深度阅读能否存续将影响集体思考的质量。
