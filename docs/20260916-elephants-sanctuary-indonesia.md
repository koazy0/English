# Elephants' Deaths Spur Move for Sanctuary in Indonesia

| 项目 | 内容 |
|------|------|
| 日期 | 2026-09-16 |
| 类型 | 外刊精选 |
| 难度 | ★★★☆☆ |
| 来源 | The Guardian |

---

The images were impossible to ignore. A young Sumatran elephant, its body skeletal and its skin hanging loose, stumbled through a palm oil plantation in search of food. Days later, it was found dead. It was the fourth elephant death in the Riau province of Sumatra in as many weeks, and for conservationists, it was the breaking point. A coalition of environmental groups has now formally petitioned the Indonesian government to establish a dedicated elephant sanctuary in central Sumatra, arguing that without urgent intervention, the species faces extinction within decades.

The Sumatran elephant is classified as critically endangered, with fewer than 2,000 remaining in the wild. Its habitat has been devastated by the rapid expansion of palm oil plantations, which have replaced vast tracts of tropical rainforest. Between 1985 and 2020, Sumatra lost approximately 70 percent of its elephant habitat, and the destruction continues. When elephants wander onto plantations in search of the migratory routes their ancestors followed for millennia, they are treated as agricultural pests. Farmers poison them, set snares, and in some cases shoot them. The conflict is as old as agriculture itself, but the scale of habitat loss has made it existential.

The proposed sanctuary would encompass approximately 250,000 hectares of forest in the Bukit Tigapuluh landscape, one of the last expanses of lowland rainforest in Sumatra. The area is already home to a small population of reintroduced elephants, and conservationists argue that expanding protected territory would provide a crucial buffer between elephants and agricultural land. The plan also includes corridors connecting fragmented forest patches, allowing elephants to migrate seasonally without encountering human settlements.

But the proposal faces formidable obstacles. The proposed sanctuary land includes areas currently licensed for logging and palm oil production, and revoking these licenses would require compensating the companies that hold them — a cost the Indonesian government is reluctant to bear. Local communities, who depend on forest resources for their livelihoods, worry that a sanctuary would restrict their access to land they have used for generations. Without community support, conservation efforts have historically failed.

The deaths in Riau have given the sanctuary campaign new urgency. Each loss reduces the genetic diversity of an already tiny population, making the species more vulnerable to disease and inbreeding. "We are watching a species disappear in real time," said one conservationist. "Every week of delay costs us elephants we cannot afford to lose."

## 长难句解析

1. When elephants wander onto plantations in search of the migratory routes their ancestors followed for millennia, they are treated as agricultural pests.
   - **解析**：when 引导时间状语从句，"in search of the migratory routes" 表目的，"their ancestors followed for millennia" 是省略了 that 的定语从句修饰 routes。主句 "they are treated as agricultural pests" 揭示人象冲突的残酷现实。

2. The proposed sanctuary land includes areas currently licensed for logging and palm oil production, and revoking these licenses would require compensating the companies that hold them — a cost the Indonesian government is reluctant to bear.
   - **解析**：and 连接两个并列分句。前句说明土地现状，后句 "revoking these licenses" 是动名词短语作主语，would require compensating 是谓语+宾语，破折号后 "a cost" 是同位语，"the Indonesian government is reluctant to bear" 是省略 that 的定语从句。
