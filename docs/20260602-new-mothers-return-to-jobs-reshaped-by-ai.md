# New Mothers Return to Jobs Reshaped by AI

> **Date**: 20260602 · **Source**: Adapted from Wired (May 2026) by Joel Khalili · **Difficulty**: ★★☆☆☆ · **Type**: 📰 同源外刊精选
>
> **Topic**: #职场 #AI #性别

---

When Danielle returned to her software developer role at a car company in mid-2025 after a year of maternity leave, she barely recognized her own job. Before she left, few colleagues used AI to write code; by the time she came back, it had become the standard. "The skills I had learned—rote development skills—we are now expected to outsource to AI," she says. Her experience reflects a growing dilemma: new mothers returning from leave are finding that AI has reshaped their professions faster than anyone predicted.

The pace of change has been extraordinary. Over just 12 months, AI coding assistants went from a novelty to an expectation in many tech workplaces. For workers who remained on the job, this shift was gradual—they learned alongside their colleagues, experimented with new tools, and adapted step by step. The daily rhythm of work provided a natural learning curve. But for mothers on maternity leave, that same year felt like stepping off a moving train. When they climbed back on, the landscape had completely changed.

A UK-based project manager, still on leave, was told by her employer to brush up on AI skills before returning. "It made me feel very vulnerable," she admits. The message was clear: the job she left no longer existed in the form she knew. This vulnerability is not just emotional. When performance reviews and project assignments favor those already fluent in AI workflows, returning mothers start at a real disadvantage—through no fault of their own.

Yet the picture is not entirely bleak. Mary McCreary, a data engineer, notes that AI has actually improved some aspects of her work. "It helps me review others' code much faster," she says. Another engineer found that AI tools were a lifeline during the exhausting early months back, when postpartum fatigue made concentration difficult. Automating routine tasks gave her breathing room she otherwise would not have had.

Still, that same engineer worries about the longer term. If AI can handle much of what she does today, could her entire role eventually be automated? Her concern is widely shared. Job postings for software engineers now overwhelmingly list AI proficiency as a requirement, and the market has been reconfigured accordingly. Workers who cannot demonstrate fluency with these tools face fewer opportunities, lower salaries, and a shrinking sense of job security.

The tension is clear: AI transforms workplaces at a speed that leaves no room for the slow, essential work of recovering from childbirth and caring for a newborn. Without deliberate policies—updated training, flexible re-onboarding, or adjusted performance timelines—new mothers will continue returning to jobs that have moved on without them.

## 长难句解析

> **Without** deliberate policies—updated training, flexible re-onboarding, or adjusted performance timelines—new mothers **will continue returning to jobs that have moved on without them**.

- **主干**: new mothers will continue returning to jobs
- **that have moved on without them** 是定语从句，修饰 jobs，说明这些岗位已经在她们缺席时"向前走了"。
- **Without…** 介词短语做条件状语，表示"如果没有……"，破折号之间为 policies 的同位语举例。
- **翻译**: 如果没有刻意的政策——更新的培训、灵活的返岗入职或调整后的绩效时间线——新妈妈们将继续回到那些已经在她们缺席时向前走了的岗位。

## 文章大意

本文探讨人工智能快速普及对新晋母亲重返职场造成的冲击。文章以 Danielle 的经历开篇，指出一年产假期间 AI 已从新奇变为标配；随后分析在岗员工渐进适应与休假母亲突然落伍之间的落差；接着呈现两面性——AI 既让回归母亲感到脆弱和焦虑，又在疲劳时提供了实际帮助；最后点明核心矛盾：AI 变革速度不因生育而暂停，若无针对性政策，新母亲将持续处于劣势。

## 思考题

**1. According to the passage, why do returning mothers face a disadvantage compared to colleagues who stayed?**

A. They lack the basic programming skills required for their original roles.

B. They missed the gradual process of adapting to AI tools during their leave.

C. Their employers explicitly refuse to provide any retraining opportunities.

D. They are assigned to entirely different departments upon return.

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章指出在岗员工是"gradual—learned alongside their colleagues"渐进适应，而休假母亲则错过了这一过程。A与原文相反（原有技能仍在但被AI取代）；C过于绝对（文中提到"told to brush up on AI"说明有提醒而非拒绝培训）；D未提及。</details>

**2. What does the engineer's concern about automation suggest about the impact of AI on software engineering jobs?**

A. AI has already replaced most software engineers in the job market.

B. AI proficiency is only required for senior engineering positions.

C. The demand for traditional coding skills is declining in the job market.

D. AI tools are primarily used to supervise rather than assist engineers.

<details><summary>答案</summary>C。工程师担心角色被自动化，且文中提到"job postings…overwhelmingly list AI proficiency as a requirement"，说明传统编码技能需求在下降。A过于极端（"most"无依据）；B与"most postings"矛盾；D与"automating routine tasks"矛盾。</details>

**3. What is the author's main argument in the last paragraph?**

A. AI transformation should be slowed down to accommodate new mothers.

B. The speed of AI change conflicts with the necessary recovery time after childbirth.

C. Most companies have already implemented effective re-onboarding policies.

D. New mothers should delay their return to work until AI tools stabilize.

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。末段核心句："AI transforms workplaces at a speed that leaves no room for the slow, essential work of recovering from childbirth"。A未建议减速（而是呼吁政策适配）；C与"Without deliberate policies"矛盾；D未提及。</details>
