# The Dangers of AI Language Models That Sound Smart

> **论文**: On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots: Can Language Models Be Too Big? 🦜
> **作者**: Bender, Gebru et al.
> **会议**: FAccT 2021
> **日期**: 2026-06-18

---

## 正文

Large language models have become remarkably good at producing fluent text. Ask one a question, and it will generate a confident, well-structured response that appears intelligent. But does this fluency mean the model actually understands what it is saying? A team of researchers argues that it does not — and that mistaking fluency for comprehension carries serious risks.

In their paper "On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots," Bender, Gebru, and colleagues coin the term "stochastic parrot" to describe large language models. A parrot can mimic human speech without grasping its meaning; similarly, an LLM statistically combines patterns from its training data to produce text that sounds coherent while lacking any genuine understanding of the content. The model does not know what the words mean — it merely knows which words tend to appear together.

The first danger lies in the training data itself. LLMs are trained on vast collections of text drawn from the internet, which inevitably reflects the biases, prejudices, and stereotypes present in society. Rather than eliminating these biases, large models amplify them. When a model generates text about certain groups of people, it may reproduce harmful generalizations — and because the output sounds authoritative, readers are more likely to accept those generalizations as truth.

A second concern is the enormous environmental cost. Training a single large language model can emit as much carbon dioxide as five automobiles do over their entire lifetimes. As companies race to build ever-larger models, the energy consumption and carbon emissions grow proportionally, raising serious questions about whether the benefits justify the environmental harm.

Perhaps the most insidious risk is psychological. Humans are naturally inclined to trust language that sounds fluent and coherent. When a machine produces text that reads smoothly, people tend to assume it is accurate and reliable, even when it contains factual errors or logical flaws. This tendency creates a false sense of assurance. In high-stakes domains such as medicine, law, and education, relying on confidently worded but ungrounded outputs could lead to harmful decisions.

The researchers also challenge the widespread assumption that bigger models naturally become more capable of understanding. Scaling up the size of a model and its training data does improve fluency, but fluency and comprehension are fundamentally different things. A stochastic parrot with a billion more parameters is still a parrot — it just mimics more convincingly.

The controversy surrounding this paper was itself revealing. When one of the authors was forced out of her position at a major technology company, it drew public attention to the tensions between corporate interests and independent research. The episode underscored the paper's central argument: we must ask not only what these models can do, but what harms they might cause — and whether we are prepared to hold those who build them accountable.

## 长难句解析

**1.** "Rather than eliminating these biases, large models amplify them, and because the output sounds authoritative, readers are more likely to accept those generalizations as truth."

- **状语**: Rather than eliminating these biases (而不是消除这些偏见)
- **主句 1**: large models amplify them (大型模型反而放大了这些偏见)
- **并列连词**: and
- **原因状语从句**: because the output sounds authoritative (因为输出听起来具有权威性)
- **主句 2**: readers are more likely to accept those generalizations as truth (读者更可能将这些概括当作事实来接受)
- **翻译**: 大型模型并没有消除这些偏见，反而放大了它们，而且因为输出听起来具有权威性，读者更可能将这些概括当作事实来接受。

**2.** "A stochastic parrot with a billion more parameters is still a parrot — it just mimics more convincingly."

- **主干**: A stochastic parrot is still a parrot (一只随机鹦鹉仍然是鹦鹉)
- **定语**: with a billion more parameters (多出十亿参数的)
- **破折号补充**: it just mimics more convincingly (它只是模仿得更加令人信服而已)
- **翻译**: 一只多出十亿参数的随机鹦鹉仍然是鹦鹉——它只是模仿得更加令人信服而已。

## 文章大意

本文基于 Bender 和 Gebru 等人的论文"论随机鹦鹉的危险"，指出大型语言模型本质上是"随机鹦鹉"——它们通过统计模式组合生成文本，而非真正理解含义。文章阐述了四大风险：一是训练数据中的偏见会被模型放大；二是训练巨大模型带来严重的环境代价；三是人们容易将流利输出误认为准确可靠，尤其在医疗、法律等高风险领域后果严重；四是模型规模增大只提升流畅性，并不带来真正的理解力。论文引发的争议本身也印证了核心观点：我们不仅要关注模型能做什么，更要追问它可能造成什么伤害。
