# Waymo's New Ojai Robotaxi: Chinese-Made, American-Operated

| 项目 | 内容 |

| 类型 | 外刊精选 |

| 来源 | Wired |

---

Waymo has pulled back the curtain on its next-generation robotaxi, and the vehicle's origins tell a story about the state of American manufacturing that few in Silicon Valley care to dwell on. The Ojai, named after the small California city where Waymo conducted early testing, is manufactured by Zeekr, the electric vehicle brand owned by China's Geely. It is, in essence, a Chinese-built car running American self-driving software — a combination that encapsulates the uneasy marriage of technological ambition and industrial reality.

The Ojai bristles with sensors: 13 cameras, 6 radar units, and 4 lidar arrays provide a 360-degree perception system that Waymo claims is the most comprehensive ever fitted to a production vehicle. The interior is designed for rider comfort rather than driver control — no steering wheel, no pedals, just bench seats facing each other and a screen for route information. It is a vehicle built not to be driven but to be ridden in, and the design reflects Waymo's confidence that its autonomous driving system has matured past the need for human backup.

Yet that confidence sits awkwardly alongside the company's recent operational retreats. Waymo has shut down commercial robotaxi service in six cities, citing a combination of low ridership, regulatory hurdles, and the aftermath of severe flooding in several markets that damaged vehicles and eroded public trust. More significantly, the company has suspended highway driving — the very capability that would make robotaxis competitive with human-driven rides — after several incidents in which vehicles made unexpected lane changes at speed.

The decision to source vehicles from Zeekr reflects a pragmatic calculation. No American automaker currently produces a purpose-built robotaxi platform at scale. GM's Cruise division built its own vehicle, the Origin, but production delays and the division's near-collapse after a dragging incident in San Francisco left Waymo without a domestic alternative. Zeekr can deliver thousands of vehicles quickly and cheaply, with the sensor integration Waymo requires built into the chassis during manufacturing rather than retrofitted afterward.

The geopolitical implications are hard to ignore. A fleet of Chinese-manufactured vehicles collecting detailed spatial data on American streets raises questions that go far beyond trade policy. Waymo insists that all data is processed domestically and that Zeekr has no access to sensor data or driving algorithms. Whether such assurances will satisfy regulators in an era of escalating US-China tensions remains to be seen. For now, the Ojai represents both the promise and the paradox of autonomous driving: the technology works, but the ecosystem required to deploy it remains fragile.

1. The Ojai, named after the small California city where Waymo conducted early testing, is manufactured by Zeekr, the electric vehicle brand owned by China's Geely.
   - **解析**：主语 The Ojai，过去分词短语 "named after..." 作后置定语，其中 where 引导定语从句修饰 city。谓语 is manufactured by Zeekr，逗号后是 Zeekr 的同位语，进一步说明其中国背景。

## 文章大意

Waymo发布新一代无人出租车Ojai，由吉利旗下的极氪制造——中国产的车运行美国自动驾驶软件。Ojai配备13个摄像头、6个雷达和4个激光雷达，但Waymo同时在收缩业务：关闭6个城市服务、暂停高速行驶功能。选择极氪是因为美国没有量产的专用无人出租车平台。然而中国制造的车辆在美国街道采集空间数据引发了地缘政治担忧，Waymo声称所有数据在国内处理，但在中美紧张关系下这一保证能否满足监管要求仍是未知数。
