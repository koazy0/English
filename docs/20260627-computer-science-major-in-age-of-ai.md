# There's Never Been a Better Time to Study Computer Science

> **Date**: 20260627 · **Source**: Adapted from The Atlantic (May 2026) by Lila Shroff · **Difficulty**: ★★★☆☆ · **Type**: 📰 同源外刊精选
>
> **Topic**: #科技 #教育 #AI

---

Over the past two years, computer science departments across the United States have witnessed a striking reversal. After a decade of surging enrollment, undergraduate CS programs saw an 8 percent decline, while graduate enrollments dropped 14 percent. The culprit seems obvious: artificial intelligence. If AI coding bots can write software, debug programs, and even design systems, why bother learning to code? The anxiety is understandable. At Anthropic, one of the leading AI companies, insiders estimate that 90 percent of new code is now AI-generated. For students contemplating a CS degree, such statistics can feel like a death sentence for their career prospects.

Yet the data tells a more complicated story. Despite the enrollment dip, computer science graduates continue to enjoy remarkably low underemployment rates—lower than those of business, humanities, and even some engineering disciplines. CS majors consistently out-earn their peers across nearly every field. The reason is simple but often overlooked: the field of computer science is not static. What it means to "study computer science" has changed before, and it is changing again.

Historically, the discipline has undergone several transformations. In the 1960s, CS was largely about mathematical logic and circuit design. By the 1990s, it had shifted toward software engineering and systems architecture. Today, the rise of AI is prompting yet another evolution. Rather than eliminating the need for CS expertise, AI is raising the ceiling of what a computer scientist can accomplish. Entry-level coding tasks—writing boilerplate, debugging simple errors—are increasingly automated, but the demand for higher-order skills has only grown. Companies need people who can architect complex systems, evaluate AI-generated code for correctness and security, and design novel applications that no AI would conceive on its own.

Moreover, the areas where computer science intersects with other fields—healthcare, climate science, finance, education—are expanding rapidly. These domains require not just technical proficiency but domain understanding, ethical judgment, and creative problem-solving. AI tools, for all their capability, cannot replace the contextual reasoning that bridges computation and real-world impact.

The students who thrive in this new landscape will not be those who resist AI but those who learn to work alongside it. The discipline has always rewarded adaptability. As one Stanford professor put it, "We are not training students to be human compilers. We are training them to think computationally." That skill—the ability to formulate problems precisely, decompose them strategically, and verify solutions rigorously—remains as valuable as ever.

In truth, the most dangerous time to avoid studying computer science is precisely when everyone else is also avoiding it. The field is not shrinking; it is deepening. And those who master its evolving foundations will find themselves not obsolete, but indispensable.

## 长难句解析

> **Rather than** eliminating the need for CS expertise, AI **is raising the ceiling of what a computer scientist can accomplish**.

- **主干**: AI is raising the ceiling
- **of what a computer scientist can accomplish** 是介词宾语从句，修饰 ceiling，说明"上限是什么"。
- **Rather than eliminating…** 介词短语做状语，表示"与其消除……不如……"，对比两种结果。
- **翻译**: AI 与其说是消除了对计算机科学专业知识的需求，不如说是提高了计算机科学家能够实现的上限。

> **The areas where computer science intersects with other fields**—healthcare, climate science, finance, education—**are expanding rapidly**.

- **主干**: The areas are expanding rapidly
- **where computer science intersects with other fields** 是定语从句，修饰 areas。
- 破折号之间的名词短语是 other fields 的同位语，举例说明。
- **翻译**: 计算机科学与其他领域——医疗保健、气候科学、金融、教育——交叉的范围正在迅速扩大。

## 文章大意

本文讨论在 AI 编程工具普及的背景下，学习计算机科学是否仍然有价值。文章先指出 CS 专业入学率下降和 AI 自动编码的现实，然后以数据反驳焦虑：CS 毕业生的低不充分就业率和高收入证明该领域并未衰退。作者认为 CS 学科一直在演变，AI 的出现正在提高而非降低对高层次能力的需求，那些能将 AI 作为工具、具备跨学科思维和严格推理能力的学生将变得更加不可或缺。

## 思考题

**1. According to the passage, why has CS enrollment declined?**

A. The job market for CS graduates has become saturated.

B. Students fear that AI will make coding skills unnecessary.

C. Universities have reduced the number of CS programs available.

D. CS graduates are being replaced by AI in most industries.

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章第一段指出入学率下降的原因是 AI 编程工具的普及让学生质疑学习编码的必要性（"If AI coding bots can write software…why bother learning to code?"）。A与文中"low underemployment"矛盾；C未提及；D过于极端，原文说的是entry-level tasks而非most industries。</details>

**2. What does the author mean by "raising the ceiling" in the context of AI and computer science?**

A. AI is making computer science more difficult to learn.

B. AI is increasing the maximum level of achievement possible for computer scientists.

C. AI is raising the cost of computer science education.

D. AI is creating barriers to entry for new CS students.

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。"raising the ceiling"比喻提高了计算机科学家能够实现的上限，即AI不是取代CS专业人员，而是扩展了他们的能力边界。A曲解原意；C中的cost未提及；D与原文意思相反。</details>

**3. What can be inferred about the students who will succeed in the "new landscape" of computer science?**

A. They should focus exclusively on learning to write code manually.

B. They should avoid using AI tools to maintain their competitive edge.

C. They should develop the ability to integrate AI with domain knowledge and critical thinking.

D. They should switch to non-technical fields where AI cannot compete.

<details><summary>答案</summary>C。文章指出成功的学生是那些"learn to work alongside it"（学会与AI协作）并具备"domain understanding, ethical judgment, and creative problem-solving"的人。A和B与"not be those who resist AI"矛盾；D未提及。</details>
