## 正文

Most people consider themselves honest. They would never dream of stealing from a shop, lying to a friend, or cheating on a tax return. Yet dishonesty is far more common than these self-assessments would suggest. People cheat on diets, pad their expense accounts, exaggerate their accomplishments on résumés, and tell small lies to avoid social discomfort. The question that has long puzzled researchers is not whether people cheat — they clearly do — but why otherwise decent people engage in dishonest behaviour.

Psychologist Dan Ariely has spent years investigating this question, and his findings challenge the conventional view that dishonesty is primarily the result of a few bad actors. In a series of ingenious experiments, Ariely and his colleagues demonstrated that most people will cheat a little when given the opportunity — but only a little. Given a simple maths test with a monetary reward for correct answers, participants who were allowed to self-report their scores consistently claimed slightly more correct answers than those whose tests were verified. The cheating was modest — not enough to arouse suspicion, but enough to increase earnings meaningfully.

Ariely attributes this pattern to what he calls the "fudge factor" theory. Most people, he argues, want to benefit from dishonesty, but they also want to maintain a positive self-image. They resolve this conflict by cheating just enough to gain an advantage while still being able to rationalize their behaviour as essentially honest. A student who glances at a neighbour's answer during an exam can tell herself that she already knew the material; an employee who claims a slightly larger mileage reimbursement can convince himself that the difference is negligible. The dishonesty remains within the boundaries that allow the individual to feel like a basically good person.

Several factors can expand or shrink the fudge factor. When people are reminded of moral standards — by signing an honour code, for example, or by reading the Ten Commandments before taking a test — cheating drops significantly. Conversely, when the distance between the dishonest act and its consequences increases — when people are paid in tokens rather than cash, for instance — cheating rises. Conflicts of interest, group loyalty, and the observation of others cheating can also erode ethical boundaries.

What makes this research particularly troubling is its implications for the escalation of dishonesty. Small acts of cheating, Ariely's work suggests, can lead to larger ones. Once a person has crossed an ethical line, even slightly, the psychological barrier to crossing it again — and crossing it further — is diminished. The fudge factor expands. What begins as a trivial exaggeration can, over time, become a habit of dishonesty that the individual no longer recognizes as such.

The lesson is not that people are fundamentally dishonest. It is, rather, that the line between honesty and dishonesty is thinner and more permeable than most of us would like to admit. Understanding the psychology of cheating — the ways in which we rationalize, justify, and gradually expand our ethical boundaries — is the first step toward establishing environments and institutions that encourage honesty rather than inadvertently enabling its opposite.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> They resolve this conflict by cheating just enough to gain an advantage while still being able to rationalize their behaviour as essentially honest, and the dishonesty remains within the boundaries that allow the individual to feel like a basically good person.

**拆分**：
- **分句1**: They resolve this conflict by cheating just enough to gain an advantage（他们通过恰好足够的作弊来解决这种冲突，以获得优势）
  - while still being able to rationalize their behaviour as essentially honest（同时仍能将自己的行为合理化为基本诚实）
- **分句2**: the dishonesty remains within the boundaries that allow the individual to feel like a basically good person（不诚实仍保持在让个体感觉自己基本上是好人的边界之内）

**翻译**: 他们通过恰好够多的作弊来解决这种内心冲突，既获得了优势，又仍能将自己的行为合理化为基本诚实，而不诚实行为仍保持在让个体感觉自己是好人的边界之内。

### 句子2

> Understanding the psychology of cheating — the ways in which we rationalize, justify, and gradually expand our ethical boundaries — is the first step toward establishing environments and institutions that encourage honesty rather than inadvertently enabling its opposite.

**拆分**：
- **主语**: Understanding the psychology of cheating（理解作弊的心理学）
- **插入语（同位语）**: the ways in which we rationalize, justify, and gradually expand our ethical boundaries（即我们合理化、辩护并逐渐扩大伦理边界的方式）
- **谓语**: is the first step toward establishing environments and institutions（是建立环境和制度的第一步）
- **定语从句**: that encourage honesty rather than inadvertently enabling its opposite（鼓励诚实而非无意中助长其对立面）

**翻译**: 理解作弊的心理学——我们合理化、辩护并逐渐扩大伦理边界的方式——是建立鼓励诚实而非无意中助长其对立面的环境和制度的第一步。

## 文章大意

本文探讨了"好人为什么会作弊"的问题。心理学家阿瑞利的研究表明，大多数人会稍微作弊——既获益又能维持正面自我形象，这被称为"弹性因子"理论。道德提醒可以减少作弊，而增加行为与后果之间的距离则会增加作弊。更令人担忧的是，小的作弊行为可能导致不诚实逐步升级。文章指出，诚实与不诚实之间的界限比人们承认的更薄更可渗透，理解作弊心理学是建立鼓励诚实环境的第一步。

## 思考题

1. According to the "fudge factor" theory, why do most people cheat only a little?
   - A) They are afraid of getting caught and punished
   - B) They want to benefit from cheating while still maintaining a positive self-image
   - C) They lack the intelligence to cheat more effectively
   - D) They are influenced by their peers to be honest

2. What effect does reminding people of moral standards have on cheating?
   - A) It has no measurable effect
   - B) It significantly reduces cheating
   - C) It increases cheating by making people nervous
   - D) It only works on religious people

3. What does the passage suggest about the escalation of dishonesty?
   - A) People who cheat once never cheat again
   - B) Small acts of cheating can diminish the psychological barrier to larger ones
   - C) Escalation only occurs in financial contexts
   - D) Most people who cheat are aware of their escalating behavior

2. **B** — 文中指出"When people are reminded of moral standards...cheating drops significantly"。

3. **B** — 文中指出"Once a person has crossed an ethical line, even slightly, the psychological barrier to crossing it again — and crossing it further — is diminished"，小的作弊会削弱再次越界的心理障碍。
