## 正文

In the shadows of Europe's grand buildings and prosperous cities, a quiet crisis is unfolding. Across the European Union, roughly one in five young people is unemployed. In Spain and Greece, the figure exceeds 30 percent. In parts of southern Italy, it is closer to 50 percent. These numbers represent not just a temporary economic inconvenience but a generation at risk of permanent scarring — a generation that may never fully recover from the lost years of early adulthood.

The causes of Europe's youth unemployment crisis are multiple and deeply intertwined. The global financial crisis of 2008 and the subsequent European debt crisis destroyed millions of jobs, particularly in construction, retail, and hospitality — sectors that traditionally employed large numbers of young workers. But the roots of the problem extend well beyond the recent downturn. Structural features of European labour markets have made young people disproportionately vulnerable to joblessness.

Labour market dualism is a primary culprit. In many European countries, workers on permanent contracts enjoy strong protections against dismissal, generous severance terms, and extensive benefits. These protections, while valuable for those who have them, create a powerful incentive for employers to avoid hiring new workers — particularly young, untested ones — into permanent positions. Instead, employers offer short-term, temporary contracts that provide no job security and few benefits. When economic conditions worsen, these temporary workers are the first to be let go. Young people, who are predominantly employed on such contracts, bear the brunt of every downturn.

The mismatch between education and labour market needs compounds the problem. University enrolment across Europe has expanded dramatically over the past three decades, but the content of many degree programmes has not kept pace with the skills that employers actually demand. Too many graduates emerge with qualifications that do not translate into employability, while sectors such as healthcare, engineering, and information technology report persistent skills shortages. Vocational training, which in countries like Germany and Switzerland provides a well-established pathway from education to employment, remains underdeveloped in much of southern Europe.

The consequences of prolonged youth unemployment extend far beyond the individuals directly affected. Young people who spend their early working years in unemployment or underemployment suffer lasting damage to their earning potential, career trajectories, and mental health. Studies have shown that a period of unemployment before the age of 25 can reduce earnings by as much as 20 percent even two decades later. The social costs are equally severe: increased rates of depression, delayed family formation, and a pervasive sense of disillusionment with democratic institutions.

Solutions exist but require political courage. Labour market reform that reduces the gap between protected insiders and vulnerable outsiders is essential, though it faces fierce resistance from those who benefit from the current arrangement. Investment in vocational training and apprenticeships can narrow the skills mismatch. Short-term measures, such as wage subsidies for young hires, can provide immediate relief. But the deepest solution may be cultural: a willingness to accept that the promise of secure, lifelong employment — a promise that shaped the expectations of a generation — can no longer be fulfilled in a world of rapid economic change.

## 文章大意

本文分析欧洲青年失业危机。文章先列举触目惊心的失业数据，指出这可能造成一代人的永久创伤；然后分析多重原因：2008年金融危机和欧债危机的直接冲击、劳动力市场双轨制（长期合同保护既得利益者，短期合同使年轻人首当其冲）、教育与就业市场需求的错配（学位不等于就业能力，职业培训体系不完善）；再阐述长期后果——收入潜力受损、心理健康下降、家庭组建推迟、对民主体制幻灭；最后提出改革方向——劳动力市场改革缩小内外差距、投资职业培训、工资补贴等短期措施，以及更深层的观念转变。

## 思考题

**1.** What is "labour market dualism" and how does it affect young workers?

A. It means young and old workers are paid the same wages
B. It creates a divide between protected permanent workers and vulnerable temporary workers, with young people concentrated in the latter group
C. It requires all workers to have both academic and vocational qualifications
D. It guarantees equal job opportunities for all age groups

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章第三段解释劳动力市场双轨制：长期合同工人享有强保护，而雇主避免招新人入长期职位，转而提供短期合同，年轻人"predominantly employed on such contracts"。</details>

**2.** What long-term effect does youth unemployment have according to research mentioned in the passage?

A. It has no significant effect on earnings after the first year
B. It can reduce earnings by up to 20 percent even twenty years later
C. It mainly affects physical health rather than mental health
D. It increases the likelihood of starting a successful business

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章第五段指出研究表明25岁之前的失业经历"a period of unemployment before the age of 25 can reduce earnings by as much as 20 percent even two decades later"。</details>

**3.** What does the author suggest is the "deepest solution" to Europe's youth unemployment?

A. Providing universal basic income for all young people
B. Abolishing all labour market protections for older workers

<details><summary>答案</summary>C。文章末段指出最深刻的解决方案可能是文化层面的——愿意接受"the promise of secure, lifelong employment...can no longer be fulfilled in a world of rapid economic change"。</details>
