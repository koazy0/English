## 正文

Growing up between two cultures was once viewed as a disadvantage. Children of immigrants, it was commonly assumed, faced a difficult choice: assimilate fully into the dominant culture and lose touch with their heritage, or remain anchored in their parents' traditions and risk marginalization. The experience of straddling two worlds, the thinking went, produced confusion, identity crises, and a permanent sense of not quite belonging to either culture.

Research over the past two decades, however, has challenged this view in compelling ways. Far from being a liability, growing up with two cultural frameworks appears to confer significant cognitive and social advantages — advantages that are increasingly valuable in a globalized world.

The most striking of these advantages is enhanced creativity. In a series of experiments, researchers have found that individuals who have deeply internalized more than one culture consistently outperform their mono-cultural peers on tests of creative thinking. The effect is not small. Bi-cultural participants generate more novel ideas, find more unusual connections between concepts, and are more adept at solving problems that require thinking outside conventional frameworks.

The reasons for this creativity boost are revealing. When a person has internalized two distinct cultural systems, they have access to two sets of assumptions, two ways of categorizing experience, and two repertoires of behavioural responses. This dual perspective creates a kind of mental flexibility — the ability to switch between different interpretive frameworks depending on the context. Psychologists call this "cultural frame switching," and it is analogous to the cognitive flexibility that bilingual individuals display when moving between languages.

The benefits of bi-culturalism extend beyond creativity. Studies have shown that people who navigate between cultures develop stronger interpersonal skills, including greater empathy, more sophisticated communication strategies, and a heightened ability to detect and adapt to social cues. These are precisely the skills that are most in demand in diverse workplaces and international settings. It is no coincidence that a disproportionate number of successful entrepreneurs, artists, and innovators come from immigrant backgrounds.

There are, of course, challenges associated with growing up between cultures. The process of integrating two cultural identities can be stressful, particularly when the cultures in question hold conflicting values or when the surrounding society is hostile to diversity. Some bi-cultural individuals do experience feelings of alienation or pressure to choose one identity over the other. But research suggests that these difficulties are most acute during adolescence and tend to diminish as individuals develop more integrated identities.

The implications of this research are significant. In an era of increasing migration and cultural mixing, the number of people who grow up between cultures is growing rapidly. Rather than viewing this as a problem to be managed, societies would do well to recognize it as a resource to be cultivated. Schools, workplaces, and communities can support bi-cultural individuals by validating both of their cultural identities and by creating environments that value the unique perspectives they bring.

The old assumption that cultural purity is desirable and that mixing weakens identity has it exactly backwards. In a world that increasingly rewards adaptability, creativity, and cross-cultural understanding, growing up between cultures may be not a handicap but an advantage — one of the most valuable advantages a person can possess.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> Far from being a liability, growing up with two cultural frameworks appears to confer significant cognitive and social advantages — advantages that are increasingly valuable in a globalized world.

**拆分**：
- **状语（表否定）**: Far from being a liability（远非不利条件）
- **主句**: growing up with two cultural frameworks appears to confer significant cognitive and social advantages（在两种文化框架中成长似乎赋予了显著的认知和社会优势）
- **同位语**: advantages that are increasingly valuable in a globalized world（在全球化世界中越来越有价值的优势）

**翻译**: 在两种文化框架中成长远非不利条件，它似乎赋予了显著的认知和社会优势——这些优势在全球化世界中越来越有价值。

### 句子2

> When a person has internalized two distinct cultural systems, they have access to two sets of assumptions, two ways of categorizing experience, and two repertoires of behavioural responses, and this dual perspective creates a kind of mental flexibility — the ability to switch between different interpretive frameworks depending on the context.

**拆分**：
- **时间状语从句**: When a person has internalized two distinct cultural systems（当一个人内化了两种不同的文化体系时）
- **分句1**: they have access to two sets of assumptions, two ways of categorizing experience, and two repertoires of behavioural responses（他们可以获取两套假设、两种经验分类方式和两套行为反应库）
- **分句2**: this dual perspective creates a kind of mental flexibility（这种双重视角创造了一种心理灵活性）
- **同位语**: the ability to switch between different interpretive frameworks depending on the context（根据语境在不同解释框架之间切换的能力）

**翻译**: 当一个人内化了两种不同的文化体系时，他们可以调用两套假设、两种经验分类方式和两套行为反应库，这种双重视角创造了一种心理灵活性——根据语境在不同解释框架之间切换的能力。

## 文章大意

本文重新审视了在两种文化间成长的经历。过去被视为劣势的观点被研究推翻——双文化背景反而赋予显著的认知和社会优势，尤其是更强的创造力。拥有两种文化框架的人能够进行"文化框架切换"，产生心理灵活性。此外，跨文化经历还培养更强的同理心和社交技能。虽然过程存在挑战，但随年龄增长会减轻。作者认为，在日益全球化、奖励适应性和创造力的世界中，双文化背景不是障碍而是优势。

## 思考题

1. What was the traditional view of growing up between two cultures?
   - A) It was seen as an advantage for creative careers
   - B) It was considered a disadvantage that caused confusion and identity crises
   - C) It was viewed as irrelevant to personal development
   - D) It was thought to make people more successful

2. What is "cultural frame switching" according to the passage?
   - A) The inability to choose between two cultures
   - B) The ability to switch between different interpretive frameworks depending on the context
   - C) The process of abandoning one culture for another
   - D) A political strategy for multicultural communities

3. What does the author recommend that societies do regarding bi-cultural individuals?
   - A) Encourage them to choose one culture over the other
   - B) View their dual cultural identity as a resource to be cultivated rather than a problem
   - C) Prevent further cultural mixing to preserve cultural purity
   - D) Provide them with separate schools and communities

<details>
<summary>参考答案</summary>

1. **B** — 文中指出过去普遍认为"straddling two worlds...produced confusion, identity crises, and a permanent sense of not quite belonging"。

2. **B** — 文中定义文化框架切换为"the ability to switch between different interpretive frameworks depending on the context"。

3. **B** — 作者建议"Rather than viewing this as a problem to be managed, societies would do well to recognize it as a resource to be cultivated"。
