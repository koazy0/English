## 正文

The image of the lone wolf — strong, independent, and self-reliant — exerts a powerful hold on the popular imagination. From superhero films to business memoirs, we celebrate individuals who stand apart from the crowd and triumph through sheer personal willpower. The problem is that this image is not merely misleading; it is biologically wrong.

Wolves, as it happens, are among the most social creatures on the planet. They live in tightly knit packs, hunt cooperatively, and share food with injured or elderly members. A solitary wolf is not a symbol of strength but an animal in crisis — typically one that has been driven from its pack and faces a sharply reduced chance of survival. The metaphor that equates independence with success gets the natural world exactly backwards.

Humans are no different. Anthropologists have long argued that cooperation, not competition, was the driving force behind human evolution. Our ancestors survived on the African savanna not because they were the fastest or the strongest, but because they worked together. They shared food, coordinated hunts, and raised children collectively. The very traits we consider most distinctly human — language, culture, tool-making — are fundamentally collaborative achievements.

Modern research supports this view. Studies of hunter-gatherer societies consistently find that individuals who try to dominate others or hoard resources are quickly punished by the group, through ostracism or direct sanctions. In game theory experiments, groups that cooperate consistently outperform groups of selfish strategists. The "free rider" problem — individuals who benefit from group efforts without contributing — is real, but human societies have evolved sophisticated norms and enforcement mechanisms to keep it in check.

The business world, however, often seems determined to ignore these lessons. Corporate culture lionises the visionary founder, the bold CEO, the disruptor who single-handedly transforms an industry. The reality is that successful companies are built on networks of collaboration: research teams, supply chains, customer communities. Even the most celebrated entrepreneurs rely on vast ecosystems of partners, investors, and employees.

This celebration of the lone individual is not just inaccurate but harmful. It encourages young people to believe that asking for help is a sign of weakness, when in fact it is one of the most important skills anyone can develop. It leads organisations to reward star performers at the expense of team cohesion. And it blinds policymakers to the fact that the biggest challenges facing humanity — climate change, pandemics, inequality — cannot be solved by any single individual, however brilliant.

The lone wolf, then, is a myth. And believing in myths has consequences.

## 长难句解析

**1.** "A solitary wolf is not a symbol of strength but an animal in crisis — typically one that has been driven from its pack and faces a sharply reduced chance of survival."

- **结构**: not...but...结构连接两个表语(a symbol of strength / an animal in crisis)；破折号后one为an animal的同位语，后接that定语从句，从句中两个并列谓语has been driven和faces
- **翻译**: 孤狼不是力量的象征，而是身处危机的动物——通常是被驱赶出狼群、生存几率大幅降低的个体

**2.** "And it blinds policymakers to the fact that the biggest challenges facing humanity — climate change, pandemics, inequality — cannot be solved by any single individual, however brilliant."

- **结构**: it指代前文的celebration of the lone individual；blinds sb. to sth.使某人对……视而不见；that引导同位语从句解释fact的内容；facing humanity为现在分词修饰challenges；however brilliant为让步状语，相当于no matter how brilliant
- **翻译**: 它使决策者对这样一个事实视而不见：人类面临的最大挑战——气候变化、流行病、不平等——不可能由任何单一个体解决，无论其多么才华横溢

## 文章大意

本文驳斥"孤狼"神话，论证合作才是人类进化和成功的关键。文章先从狼的习性出发，指出独狼并非强者而是弱者；然后用人类学证据说明合作而非竞争推动了人类进化；接着引用现代研究（狩猎采集社会、博弈论实验）证明合作优于自私策略；再批评商界对个人英雄的崇拜忽视合作网络；最后指出这种错误观念的危害——削弱求助意愿、破坏团队凝聚力、使决策者忽视集体挑战。
