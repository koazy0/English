# 2020 Text 4 — 互联网与个人隐私

> 话题标签：#隐私 #互联网 #数据保护 | 来源：真题 | 难度：★★★★☆ | 字数：~460 | 类型：📖 真题原文

## 正文

The internet was supposed to liberate individuals. Instead, it has created the most extensive surveillance apparatus in human history. Every click, search, purchase, and location is recorded, analysed, and monetised by a handful of technology platforms whose business models depend on harvesting personal data at scale. The erosion of privacy is not an accidental side effect of the digital age — it is its central business proposition.

Consider the scale of data collection. Google processes over 8.5 billion searches per day, each one revealing something about a user's intentions, fears, and desires. Facebook tracks likes, shares, and even the time spent hovering over a post without clicking. Amazon monitors browsing and purchasing patterns to predict what customers will want next. Together, these companies hold more intimate information about billions of people than any government intelligence agency ever possessed. And unlike governments, they face minimal oversight in how they collect, store, and use this data.

The consequences of this data economy are far-reaching. Personal information is used to target advertising with surgical precision, influencing what people buy, read, and even how they vote. The Cambridge Analytica scandal revealed that data from 87 million Facebook users had been weaponised to manipulate political campaigns. Beyond politics, data breaches expose individuals to identity theft, financial fraud, and discrimination. Employers, insurers, and landlords increasingly use data profiles to make decisions about people's lives, often without their knowledge or consent.

Why has regulation lagged so far behind? Part of the explanation lies in the speed of technological change. Lawmakers struggle to understand systems that evolve faster than legislation can be drafted. But a more fundamental reason is the power imbalance between platforms and governments. Technology companies lobby aggressively against privacy regulations, arguing that restrictions will stifle innovation and harm consumers. They point to the convenience of personalised services and the economic value of data-driven advertising. Their argument has been remarkably effective, particularly in the United States, where comprehensive privacy legislation remains absent at the federal level.

Europe has taken a different path. The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which came into effect in 2018, imposes strict requirements on how organisations handle personal data. It grants individuals the right to know what data is collected about them, to request its deletion, and to object to its processing. Companies face fines of up to four percent of global revenue for violations. Early results suggest that GDPR has raised awareness and improved practices, though enforcement remains uneven and many websites simply bombard European users with consent pop-ups rather than genuinely reducing data collection.

The challenge ahead is to strike a balance that protects individuals without destroying the benefits of the digital economy. Privacy is not an absolute right — it must be weighed against legitimate interests in security, innovation, and public health. Yet the current balance is badly tilted in favour of platforms. Until individuals have meaningful control over their own data, and until the costs of data misuse are borne by those who profit from it, the internet will remain less a tool of liberation than an instrument of extraction.

## 长难句解析

**1.** Every click, search, purchase, and location is recorded, analysed, and monetised by a handful of technology platforms whose business models depend on harvesting personal data at scale.

- **拆分**：主语为四个并列名词 click, search, purchase, location，谓语为三个被动语态 recorded, analysed, monetised；whose 引导定语从句修饰 platforms。
- **翻译**：每一次点击、搜索、购买和定位都被少数几个科技平台记录、分析和变现，而这些平台的商业模式正依赖于大规模获取个人数据。

**2.** Until individuals have meaningful control over their own data, and until the costs of data misuse are borne by those who profit from it, the internet will remain less a tool of liberation than an instrument of extraction.

- **拆分**：两个 until 引导并列条件状语从句，主句为 the internet will remain...；less...than... 结构表示"与其说是……不如说是……"；who profit from it 为定语从句修饰 those。
- **翻译**：除非个人对自己的数据拥有有意义的控制权，除非数据滥用的成本由从中获利的人承担，否则互联网与其说是解放的工具，不如说是榨取的 instrument。

## 文章大意

本文论述了互联网时代个人隐私的严重侵蚀。科技平台通过大规模数据采集构建了人类历史上最庞大的监控体系，掌握着比任何情报机构都更私密的个人信息。数据被用于精准广告投放、政治操纵、身份盗窃和歧视性决策。监管严重滞后，部分原因在于技术变化速度快于立法，更深层原因在于科技公司的游说力量。欧洲GDPR是重要尝试，但执行不均匀。文章呼吁在保护个人与数字经济发展之间找到平衡，当前天平严重倾向于平台一方。
