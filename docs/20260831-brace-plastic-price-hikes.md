# Brace for Plastic Price Hikes: The Iran War's Hidden Supply Chain Shock

When most people think about the economic fallout of the Iran war, they picture oil prices spiking at the pump. They should be looking at their shampoo bottles, food packaging, and synthetic clothing. The conflict has triggered a supply chain crisis in the global plastics industry that is rapidly cascading through the consumer economy, and the worst is yet to come.

The mechanism is straightforward but underappreciated. Roughly 20 percent of the world's naphtha — the petroleum derivative that serves as the primary feedstock for plastic production — passes through the Strait of Hormuz. When the strait was effectively closed to commercial shipping, Asian petrochemical producers, who depend disproportionately on Middle Eastern naphtha, faced an immediate and severe shortage. Within days, major producers in South Korea and Singapore declared force majeure on their polyethylene contracts, a legal clause that frees parties from liability when extraordinary events make fulfillment impossible.

The consequences did not take long to reach American consumers. Dow Chemical announced three separate price increases for polyethylene in a single month. PET — the plastic used in beverage bottles — surged more than 30 percent in spot markets. Polypropylene, found in everything from automobile parts to medical devices, saw similar spikes. Industry analysts warn that the price increases are not temporary. "This isn't a blip," said one commodities strategist. "The feedstock is physically unavailable. You can't price your way out of a supply absence."

The ripple effects extend far beyond raw material costs. Packaging accounts for roughly 10 to 15 percent of the cost of most consumer goods. When packaging costs double, manufacturers face an unenviable choice: absorb the increase and destroy margins, or pass it on and risk demand destruction. Many are choosing a third option — redesigning products to use less plastic — but such transitions take months or years, not weeks.

Small and mid-sized manufacturers are especially vulnerable. Unlike large corporations, they cannot hedge against commodity price swings using futures contracts, nor can they negotiate volume discounts with suppliers who have nothing left to discount. Several small packaging companies have already suspended operations, and trade associations are lobbying for emergency relief.

Perhaps the most insidious long-term consequence is the potential derailment of recycling infrastructure. Recycled plastic, which requires collection, sorting, and processing, becomes economically uncompetitive when virgin plastic prices are volatile. Several major recycling facilities have paused operations, citing margin compression. A crisis that began with too little naphtha may end with too much unrecycled plastic in landfills — a bitter irony for an industry already struggling with its environmental reputation.

## 长难句解析

1. When the strait was effectively closed to commercial shipping, Asian petrochemical producers, who depend disproportionately on Middle Eastern naphtha, faced an immediate and severe shortage.
   - **解析**：when 引导时间状语从句，主句主语 "Asian petrochemical producers" 后跟 who 引导的非限制性定语从句，谓语 faced，宾语 "an immediate and severe shortage"。定语从句解释了为何亚洲生产商受创最重——它们对中东石脑油依赖度异常高。

2. A crisis that began with too little naphtha may end with too much unrecycled plastic in landfills — a bitter irony for an industry already struggling with its environmental reputation.
   - **解析**：主语 "A crisis" 后跟 that 引导的定语从句，谓语 may end with，宾语 "too much unrecycled plastic in landfills"，破折号后是同位语评价。"began with too little... end with too much" 构成对比反讽，凸显塑料危机的悖论。

## 文章大意

伊朗战争引发的霍尔木兹海峡封锁导致全球塑料供应链危机。约20%的塑料原料石脑油需经此海峡运输，封锁后亚洲生产商宣布不可抗力，聚乙烯和PET价格飙升30%以上。制造商面临两难选择：吸收成本还是转嫁涨价。小型企业尤其脆弱，无力对冲风险。更具讽刺意味的是，原生塑料价格波动使回收塑料失去经济竞争力，可能造成更多塑料进入垃圾填埋场。
