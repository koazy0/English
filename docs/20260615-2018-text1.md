## 正文

Across the developed world, university dropout rates have been rising steadily for the past decade. In the United States, only about sixty percent of students who enroll in a four-year degree program complete it within six years. In the United Kingdom, nearly one in ten undergraduates drops out before the end of their first year. The figures are troubling not only for the individuals concerned but for the societies that invest heavily in higher education and depend on it as an engine of social mobility.

Understanding why students leave requires looking beyond the most obvious explanations. Financial pressure is certainly a factor: tuition fees have risen dramatically in many countries, and students from lower-income families are disproportionately likely to abandon their studies. But money alone does not tell the whole story. Research consistently shows that students who receive full financial aid still drop out at significant rates, suggesting that other forces are at work.

One of the most powerful of these forces is a mismatch between students' expectations and the reality of university life. Many students arrive on campus with a romanticized vision of academic study, only to discover that the workload is heavier, the teaching less personal, and the intellectual demands more rigorous than they anticipated. This sense of disillusionment is especially acute among students who are the first in their families to attend university, as they lack the informal guidance that peers with university-educated parents often receive.

Mental health is another critical factor. Surveys of university students consistently reveal high levels of anxiety, depression, and stress. The transition from the structured environment of school to the relative freedom of university can be deeply unsettling, and many institutions have struggled to provide adequate support. Counselling services are frequently oversubscribed, and students may wait weeks or months for an appointment. In the absence of timely help, academic difficulties quickly compound emotional ones, creating a downward spiral from which recovery is difficult.

What can be done? Some universities have experimented with early-warning systems that identify students at risk of dropping out — those who miss lectures, submit assignments late, or show other signs of disengagement — and offer targeted support before it is too late. Others have invested in peer mentoring programmes, in which experienced students guide newcomers through the challenges of their first year. The evidence suggests that such interventions can be effective, but only if they are implemented early and consistently.

A more fundamental solution may lie in rethinking the purpose of university itself. If higher education is to serve as a genuine engine of opportunity rather than a sorting mechanism that rewards those who are already advantaged, then institutions must do more than simply admit students from diverse backgrounds. They must ensure that every student who enrolls has a realistic chance of completing their degree — and that means providing not just financial support, but the academic, emotional, and social scaffolding that makes success possible.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> This sense of disillusionment is especially acute among students who are the first in their families to attend university, as they lack the informal guidance that peers with university-educated parents often receive.

**拆分**：
- **主句**: This sense of disillusionment is especially acute among students（这种幻灭感在学生中尤为严重）
- **定语从句**: who are the first in their families to attend university（他们是家中第一代大学生）
- **原因状语从句**: as they lack the informal guidance（因为他们缺乏非正式的指导）
- **定语从句**: that peers with university-educated parents often receive（大学受教育父母的同龄人经常获得的）

**翻译**: 这种幻灭感在家中的第一代大学生中尤为严重，因为他们缺乏那些父母上过大学的同龄人经常获得的非正式指导。

### 句子2

> If higher education is to serve as a genuine engine of opportunity rather than a sorting mechanism that rewards those who are already advantaged, then institutions must do more than simply admit students from diverse backgrounds.

**拆分**：
- **条件状语从句**: If higher education is to serve as a genuine engine of opportunity（如果高等教育要成为真正的机会引擎）
  - rather than a sorting mechanism（而不是一个筛选机制）
  - that rewards those who are already advantaged（奖励那些已经有优势的人）
- **主句**: institutions must do more than simply admit students from diverse backgrounds（院校必须做的不仅仅是录取来自不同背景的学生）

**翻译**: 如果高等教育要成为真正的机会引擎而非奖励已有优势者的筛选机制，那么院校必须做的不仅仅是录取来自不同背景的学生。

## 文章大意

本文讨论了大学辍学率上升的问题。经济压力虽是重要因素，但不足以解释全部。学生对大学生活的期望与现实之间的落差、心理健康问题以及缺乏支持系统也是关键原因。一些大学采取了预警系统和同伴导师制等干预措施。作者认为，更根本的解决方案是重新审视大学的目的，不仅要录取多元背景的学生，更要提供学术、情感和社交方面的支撑，确保每个学生都有完成学业的现实可能。

## 思考题

1. According to the passage, why is financial pressure not the complete explanation for student dropout?
   - A) Tuition fees have been decreasing
   - B) Students who receive full financial aid still drop out at significant rates
   - C) Most dropouts come from wealthy families
   - D) Financial aid is not available at any university

2. What does the author identify as a major cause of student disillusionment?
   - A) The cost of university textbooks
   - B) A gap between students' expectations and the reality of university life
   - C) The lack of sports facilities on campus
   - D) Too much personal attention from professors

3. What is the author's main argument in the final paragraph?
   - A) Universities should focus exclusively on reducing tuition fees
   - B) Universities must provide comprehensive support beyond admission to ensure student success
   - C) Higher dropout rates are acceptable because university is not for everyone
   - D) Only students from privileged backgrounds should attend university

<details>
<summary>参考答案</summary>

1. **B** — 文中指出"students who receive full financial aid still drop out at significant rates, suggesting that other forces are at work"，即使获得全额资助仍有相当比例辍学。

2. **B** — 文中明确提到"a mismatch between students' expectations and the reality of university life"，即学生期望与大学生活现实之间的不匹配导致幻灭感。

3. **B** — 作者在末段指出院校必须做的不仅仅是录取多元背景的学生，还要提供"academic, emotional, and social scaffolding"，即全面的支撑系统。
