# When AI Gets Booed at Graduation

> **日期**：20260605 | **标题**：When AI Gets Booed at Graduation | **来源**：Adapted from MIT Technology Review (May 2026) by Caiwei Chen
>
> **标签**：#AI #社会 #教育 | **难度**：★★☆☆☆ | **类型**：📰 同源外刊精选

## 正文

When former Google CEO Eric Schmidt stood before University of Arizona graduates this May and declared that their mission was to "help shape AI," the response was swift and loud: a chorus of boos rolled across the stadium. "I can hear you," Schmidt replied, visibly caught off guard, before conceding that fears about disappearing jobs were "perfectly rational." He was not alone. Across American campuses this commencement season, students have been jeering at AI pep talks from tech executives who urge them to embrace the very technology many believe will steal their livelihoods.

The tension on display at these graduations reflects a deeper divide. On one side stand the architects of the AI revolution — company founders, venture capitalists, and industry lobbyists — who paint a picture of unprecedented opportunity. They point to soaring investment, landmark court victories, and a cascade of corporate partnerships as proof that the future belongs to those who adapt. OpenAI, for instance, continues to raise enormous sums and forge alliances with major publishers, seemingly untroubled by public skepticism. Even Hollywood has joined the chorus: actress Reese Witherspoon recently warned women to "embrace AI or be replaced by it," framing technological fluency as a matter of survival.

On the other side sit the graduates themselves, clutching newly printed diplomas and entering a job market that looks nothing like the one they prepared for. Entry-level positions in consulting, copywriting, and software testing — once reliable first rungs on the career ladder — are shrinking as AI tools absorb routine tasks. A recent survey found that nearly sixty percent of college seniors expect AI to reduce their job prospects within five years. Their anger at commencement speeches is not mere rudeness; it is a rational response to the experience of being told to celebrate a force that threatens their economic footing.

Who, then, is right? The truth likely lies between the extremes. History suggests that transformative technologies destroy some jobs while creating others, but the transition is seldom smooth or fair. Those who bear the heaviest burden — recent graduates with student loans and few savings — are rarely the same people who capture the greatest rewards. The graduates booing Schmidt are not anti-progress — they are demanding that progress come with accountability. If tech leaders want their optimistic vision to be taken seriously, they must do more than preach adaptability from a commencement podium. They need to support concrete policies: retraining programs, stronger labor protections, and transparent hiring practices that give young workers a genuine chance to compete alongside machines. Until then, the boos will keep coming — and they will be entirely justified.

## 长难句解析

### 1

> Entry-level positions in consulting, copywriting, and software testing — once reliable first rungs on the career ladder — are shrinking as AI tools absorb routine tasks.

**结构拆解**：

- **主干**：Entry-level positions are shrinking
- **介词短语**：in consulting, copywriting, and software testing（修饰 positions）
- **插入语**：— once reliable first rungs on the career ladder —（补充说明 positions，rungs 意为"阶梯的横档"，比喻职业起点）
- **状语从句**：as AI tools absorb routine tasks（as = 随着，表原因/时间）

**翻译**：咨询、文案和软件测试领域的入门级岗位——曾经是职业阶梯上可靠的第一步——正在随着AI工具接管常规任务而萎缩。

### 2

> If tech leaders want their optimistic vision to be taken seriously, they must do more than preach adaptability from a commencement podium.

**结构拆解**：

- **条件状语从句**：If tech leaders want their optimistic vision to be taken seriously
  - want sb./sth. to do 结构：want their optimistic vision to be taken seriously（被动不定式）
- **主干**：they must do more than preach adaptability from a commencement podium
  - do more than + 动词原形：不仅仅要做……
  - from a commencement podium：介词短语作状语，表示"从毕业典礼讲台上"

**翻译**：如果科技领袖想让他们的乐观愿景被认真对待，他们就必须做得更多，而不能只在毕业典礼的讲台上空谈适应能力。

## 文章大意

本文以Eric Schmidt在亚利桑那大学毕业典礼上遭嘘声的事件为切入点，揭示了AI乐观派与年轻毕业生之间的深层矛盾。科技领袖强调AI带来的机遇与投资热潮，甚至好莱坞名人也加入鼓吹行列；然而，面临就业市场萎缩的毕业生们对此感到愤怒与焦虑，认为这是对他们经济处境的无视。作者指出，双方各有道理，但历史表明技术转型的代价往往不公。毕业生并非反对进步，而是要求进步伴随问责；科技领袖若想获得信任，就应推动切实的政策保障，而非空谈适应。
