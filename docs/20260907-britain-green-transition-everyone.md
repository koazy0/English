# Britain's Green Transition Leaves Everyone Behind

| 项目 | 内容 |
|------|------|
| 日期 | 2026-09-07 |
| 类型 | 外刊精选 |
| 难度 | ★★★★★ |
| 来源 | The Guardian |

---

The Labour government's approach to the green transition has acquired a paradoxical quality: it manages to be simultaneously authoritarian and ineffectual. It curtails the public's right to challenge energy projects while failing to build the popular consent without which no transition can succeed. The result is a policy framework that alienates the very communities it needs to carry along, all in the name of urgency that somehow never translates into actual deployment at the scale required.

The Planning and Infrastructure Bill, currently making its way through Parliament, is the clearest expression of this contradiction. The legislation streamlines the approval process for major energy projects — wind farms, solar arrays, transmission lines — by limiting the grounds on which local communities and environmental groups can object. The government argues that the current planning system allows a small number of opponents to delay critical infrastructure for years, and that climate urgency justifies removing these obstacles. There is some truth to this: a single wind farm in Norfolk was delayed for seven years by a handful of objectors, and Britain's grid connection queue now stretches into the 2030s.

But stripping away democratic safeguards to accelerate a transition that most people have not been persuaded to support is a recipe for backlash, not progress. Germany's Energiewende succeeded not because it bypassed public opposition but because it built genuine consensus through community ownership models, local benefit funds, and transparent planning processes. Denmark achieved similar results by making wind farm co-ownership available to local residents, who became beneficiaries rather than victims of the energy transformation.

In Britain, by contrast, the government offers communities nothing but imposition. When a solar farm is proposed in a rural area, residents are told that it is necessary for national decarbonization and that their objections are merely NIMBYism. They are not offered shares in the project. They are not guaranteed lower energy bills. They are not even promised that the electricity generated will flow to their community rather than to a data center in London. Is it any wonder they object?

The deeper problem is that this authoritarian approach undermines its own objectives. When communities feel excluded from decision-making, they resist not just the specific project but the entire enterprise of decarbonization. Support for renewable energy in Britain, while still majority-positive, has been declining in rural areas where projects are concentrated. The government's heavy-handed tactics are creating the very opposition they were designed to circumvent.

Meanwhile, the urgency that justifies curtailing democratic rights has produced remarkably little actual urgency in deployment. Britain's offshore wind capacity additions have consistently missed targets. The hydrogen strategy has been delayed repeatedly. Home insulation programs have been cut. The government that insists it cannot afford the luxury of democratic consultation also cannot seem to build things at the pace its own climate targets require.

A green transition built on coercion rather than consent will not endure. It will be reversed the moment political winds shift, as similar top-down environmental mandates have been reversed before. The only sustainable path is one that brings the public along — not as subjects to be managed but as partners to be convinced.

## 长难句解析

1. It curtails the public's right to challenge energy projects while failing to build the popular consent without which no transition can succeed.
   - **解析**：主句谓语 curtails，宾语 the public's right to challenge，while 引导对比状语，现在分词 failing 表主动，"without which no transition can succeed" 是介词 + 关系代词引导的定语从句修饰 consent。

2. A green transition built on coercion rather than consent will not endure; it will be reversed the moment political winds shift, as similar top-down environmental mandates have been reversed before.
   - **解析**：分号连接两个分句。前句主语 "A green transition" 后跟过去分词短语作定语，"rather than" 表示而非。后句含 the moment 引导的时间状语从句，as 引导方式状语从句，用历史先例论证强制推动的转型不可持续。

## 文章大意

英国工党政府的绿色转型策略既威权又低效：一方面限制公众挑战能源项目的权利，另一方面却未能建立公众支持。德国和丹麦通过社区共有和本地收益成功推进绿色转型，而英国只给社区强加项目不给予回报，反而加剧了反对。更讽刺的是，以紧迫为由削减民主权利的政府，实际部署速度却屡屡落后于目标。只有建立在共识而非强制基础上的绿色转型才能持久。
