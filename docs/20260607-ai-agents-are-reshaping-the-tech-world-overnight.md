## 正文

In August 2025, a London meetup called Claude Code Anonymous drew dozens of devoted programmers. Peter Steinberger, a seasoned software developer, stood before the crowd and made a striking confession: "I dedicate pretty much all my waking time to this." The "this" he referred to was AI agents — software programs that can perform complex tasks on their own, without step-by-step human instructions.

Just weeks later, Anthropic released Opus 4.5, a model capable of handling far more complicated programming tasks than its predecessors. More impressively, it could manage a team of AI subagents — smaller programs that work together under a central controller. Anthropic claimed that Opus 4.5 "scored higher than any human candidate ever" on their internal hiring exam. The claim sparked heated debate, but countless coders spent their holidays trying out the new tool anyway.

Steinberger was among the first to act. He launched OpenClaw, an open-source tool that lets users create personal AI agents capable of running autonomously on their computers. The response was overwhelming: OpenClaw attracted 366,000 stars on GitHub within weeks, making it one of the fastest-growing projects in the platform's history.

Industry veterans were quick to recognise the significance. Thomas Reardon, a veteran technologist, called it "the most underrated, massive release I've experienced in technology." Marc Andreessen, the prominent investor, was even more direct: "It's almost inevitable that this is the way people are going to use computers."

What was left unsaid, however, is perhaps more important than any of these enthusiastic endorsements. The shift toward AI agents will not be a matter of personal preference — it will be a necessity. As companies adopt agent-based workflows, workers who refuse to use them will find themselves unable to keep pace. Just as email replaced memos and smartphones replaced landlines, AI agents are likely to become the standard interface through which we interact with digital systems. The question is no longer whether you will use an AI agent, but how soon.

This reality raises uncomfortable questions. If using AI agents becomes unavoidable, what happens to those who cannot adapt quickly enough? And if a handful of companies control the most powerful agents, how much leverage will they have over the rest of us? For now, the technology is still young, and the excitement feels genuine. But history suggests that when a tool becomes this widespread this fast, the consequences — both positive and negative — arrive sooner than anyone expects.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> As companies adopt agent-based workflows, workers who refuse to use them will find themselves unable to keep pace.

**拆分**：
- **状语从句**: As companies adopt agent-based workflows（随着公司采用基于智能体的工作流程）
  - as 在此表示"随着"，引导时间状语从句
- **主句**: workers will find themselves unable to keep pace
- **定语从句**: who refuse to use them 修饰 workers，"拒绝使用它们的[员工]"

**翻译**: 随着公司采用基于智能体的工作流程，拒绝使用它们的员工会发现自己跟不上步伐。

### 句子2

> But history suggests that when a tool becomes this widespread this fast, the consequences — both positive and negative — arrive sooner than anyone expects.

**拆分**：
- **主句**: history suggests that...（历史表明……）
- **宾语从句**: when a tool becomes this widespread this fast, the consequences arrive sooner than anyone expects
  - when 引导时间状语从句，this widespread this fast 中 this 为副词，"如此广泛、如此之快"
  - both positive and negative 为插入语，补充说明 consequences
  - sooner than anyone expects 为比较结构，"比任何人预料的都要快"

**翻译**: 但历史表明，当一个工具以如此快的速度变得如此普及，其后果——无论正面还是负面——会比任何人预料的来得更早。

## 文章大意

本文讲述AI智能体（AI agents）如何以超出预期的速度改变科技行业。从Anthropic发布Opus 4.5到OpenClaw的爆火，行业重量级人物纷纷预言AI智能体将成为人们使用电脑的标准方式。作者指出，关键问题不在于你是否会选择使用AI智能体，而在于你多快必须适应它——这不是偏好，而是必然。

## 思考题

1. According to the passage, what made Opus 4.5 different from earlier AI models?
   - A) It was the first AI model ever released
   - B) It could manage a team of AI subagents and handle more complex tasks
   - C) It replaced all human programmers immediately
   - D) It was designed specifically for hiring exams

2. The word "inevitable" in Marc Andreessen's quote is closest in meaning to:
   - A) desirable
   - B) unavoidable
   - C) unlikely
   - D) optional

3. What does the author imply in the last paragraph about the adoption of AI agents?
   - A) People should resist using AI agents as long as possible
   - B) Only positive consequences will result from AI agents
   - C) The rapid spread of AI agents will bring consequences faster than people anticipate
   - D) Companies that control AI agents will voluntarily share their power

<details>
<summary>参考答案</summary>

1. **B** — 文中指出 Opus 4.5 "capable of handling far more complicated programming tasks than its predecessors" 且 "could manage a team of AI subagents"，即能处理更复杂的任务并管理子智能体团队。

2. **B** — inevitable 意为"不可避免的"，与 unavoidable 同义。安德森认为人们以这种方式使用电脑是不可避免的。

3. **C** — 文章最后一句指出 "the consequences — both positive and negative — arrive sooner than anyone expects"，即AI智能体普及的后果会比任何人预料的来得更快，与C选项一致。
