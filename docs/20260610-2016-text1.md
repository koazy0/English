## 正文

In September 2014, England became the first country in the world to make computer programming a compulsory subject for all children from the age of five. The move was hailed as visionary by some and dismissed as gimmickry by others, but it reflected a growing consensus among policymakers that understanding code is no longer a niche skill but a form of basic literacy.

The argument for early coding education is straightforward. In a world increasingly shaped by software, the ability to understand how programs are constructed — and, crucially, how they construct the world around us — is becoming as fundamental as reading and writing. Proponents point out that coding teaches logical thinking, problem decomposition, and persistence in the face of failure. Children who learn to program, they argue, are not merely acquiring a technical skill; they are learning a new way of thinking.

There is also an economic rationale. The technology sector is one of the fastest-growing sources of employment in most developed economies, and the demand for skilled programmers consistently outstrips supply. By introducing coding early, governments hope to enlarge the pipeline of future talent and, ideally, to broaden participation in the tech industry beyond its current demographic profile, which remains disproportionately male and affluent.

Yet the rollout has not been without difficulties. A significant shortage of qualified teachers has forced many schools to rely on untrained staff or online resources of variable quality. In some cases, coding lessons have been reduced to little more than sessions in which children follow on-screen instructions to assemble pre-built components — a process that critics argue bears little resemblance to actual programming and does little to develop genuine computational thinking.

There is also a broader philosophical objection. Not everyone agrees that coding belongs in the same category as reading and mathematics. Unlike literacy and numeracy, which are relatively stable skills, programming languages evolve rapidly and the specific tools children learn today may well be obsolete within a decade. Some educators argue that the time devoted to coding would be better spent on subjects that develop deeper analytical capacities — such as philosophy or pure mathematics — which are less vulnerable to technological change.

Supporters of coding education counter that the specific language matters less than the underlying concepts. The point of teaching children to code, they insist, is not to train them in a particular programming language but to help them understand the logic of algorithms, the structure of data, and the relationship between input and output. These are enduring principles that transcend any single technology.

The truth may lie somewhere between the enthusiasts and the skeptics. Coding education can be valuable, but only if it is taught well, by teachers who understand both the subject and how children learn. Without that foundation, the ambition to create a generation of digital thinkers risks becoming merely another educational fashion — well intentioned, but ultimately hollow.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> By introducing coding early, governments hope to enlarge the pipeline of future talent and, ideally, to broaden participation in the tech industry beyond its current demographic profile, which remains disproportionately male and affluent.

**拆分**：
- **方式状语**: By introducing coding early（通过尽早引入编程）
- **主句**: governments hope to enlarge...and to broaden...（政府希望扩大……并拓宽……）
- **定语从句**: which remains disproportionately male and affluent（其仍然不成比例地以男性和富裕人群为主）

**翻译**: 通过尽早引入编程，政府希望扩大未来人才储备，并理想地将科技行业的参与范围拓宽到现有的人口特征之外——目前该行业仍不成比例地以男性和富裕人群为主。

### 句子2

> Some educators argue that the time devoted to coding would be better spent on subjects that develop deeper analytical capacities — such as philosophy or pure mathematics — which are less vulnerable to technological change.

**拆分**：
- **宾语从句**: the time devoted to coding would be better spent on subjects（投入编程的时间最好花在其他学科上）
- **定语从句1**: that develop deeper analytical capacities（培养更深层次分析能力的）
- **定语从句2**: which are less vulnerable to technological change（这些学科不太容易受技术变革影响的）

**翻译**: 一些教育者认为，投入编程的时间最好花在那些培养更深层次分析能力的学科上——如哲学或纯数学——这些学科不太容易受到技术变革的影响。

## 文章大意

本文讨论了儿童编程教育的推广及其争议。2014年英国率先将编程列为必修课，支持者认为编程是新时代的基本素养，能培养逻辑思维并满足经济需求；批评者则指出师资不足、教学内容浅表化，以及编程语言快速过时等问题。支持者反驳称重要的是底层计算思维而非特定语言。作者认为编程教育有价值，但前提是教学得当，否则只会成为又一场空洞的教育风潮。

## 思考题

1. Why did England make coding a compulsory subject for young children?
   - A) To reduce unemployment rates immediately
   - B) Because understanding code is considered a form of basic literacy in the digital age
   - C) To replace traditional subjects like mathematics and reading
   - D) Because technology companies demanded it

2. What is one major problem with the current implementation of coding education?
   - A) Children are not interested in learning to code
   - B) Parents refuse to allow their children to study programming
   - C) There is a significant shortage of qualified teachers
   - D) Programming languages are too difficult for young children

3. How do supporters respond to the objection that programming languages will become obsolete?
   - A) They agree that coding education should be delayed until children are older
   - B) They argue that the underlying concepts are more important than specific languages
   - C) They claim that programming languages have not changed in decades
   - D) They suggest teaching only the newest programming languages

<details>
<summary>参考答案</summary>

1. **B** — 文中提到"understanding code is no longer a niche skill but a form of basic literacy"，即理解代码已成为一种基本素养。

2. **C** — 文中明确指出"A significant shortage of qualified teachers has forced many schools to rely on untrained staff or online resources of variable quality"。

3. **B** — 支持者反驳称"the specific language matters less than the underlying concepts"，重要的是理解算法逻辑、数据结构等超越具体技术的底层原理。
