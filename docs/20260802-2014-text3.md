## 正文

Of all the factors of production that economists study — labour, capital, and entrepreneurship — land is the most peculiar. Unlike machinery, which can be manufactured, or workers, who can be trained, land is fixed in supply. As the old saying goes: they are not making it anymore. This simple fact has profound consequences for how economies function and how wealth is distributed.

The classical economists understood this well. David Ricardo, writing in the early 19th century, argued that as populations grew and demand for land increased, the owners of the most productive land would reap ever-larger rents. The value of land, he observed, derives not from what the owner has done but from what society has built around it. A farmer whose land happens to lie beside a newly constructed railway sees its value soar — not through any effort of his own, but because the railway has made the land more useful to others.

This insight has lost none of its relevance. In modern cities, the pattern Ricardo described operates on an enormous scale. A modest apartment in central London or Manhattan may cost more than a grand house in a rural village, even though the physical structure is identical. The difference is entirely in the location — and location value is created by the community, not by the individual landowner. Public transport, schools, parks, and the density of economic activity all contribute to making a particular piece of land desirable.

Urbanisation has intensified the economics of land in striking ways. As more people move to cities — and the United Nations estimates that 68 percent of the world's population will live in urban areas by 2050 — the competition for well-located land becomes fiercer. The result is soaring property prices that outpace wage growth, making homeownership increasingly unattainable for young families. In cities like Hong Kong, Vancouver, and Sydney, the ratio of median house prices to median incomes exceeds 10 to 1, a level once considered extraordinary.

The economic implications are significant. When a large share of national income flows to landowners rather than to workers or entrepreneurs, inequality widens. Productive investment is displaced by speculative property dealing. Young professionals in expensive cities spend such a large share of their income on rent that they have little left for consumption or saving, dampening overall economic growth.

Some economists advocate a land value tax — a levy on the unimproved value of land — as a solution. By capturing for public benefit the value created by society rather than by individual owners, such a tax could reduce speculation, fund public services, and make housing more affordable. Yet implementation has been slow, hampered by political resistance from those who benefit most from the current arrangement.

Land, the oldest factor of production, remains at the centre of some of the most pressing economic challenges of our time. Ignoring its peculiar economics comes at a cost that falls disproportionately on those who can least afford it.

## 长难句解析

**1.** "A farmer whose land happens to lie beside a newly constructed railway sees its value soar — not through any effort of his own, but because the railway has made the land more useful to others."

- **结构**: 主句主干A farmer sees its value soar；whose引导定语从句修饰farmer；破折号后not through...but because...形成对比结构，解释地价上涨的原因
- **翻译**: 一个恰好其土地位于新建铁路旁的农夫会看到土地价值飙升——不是因为他自身的努力，而是因为铁路使这块土地对他人更有用

**2.** "By capturing for public benefit the value created by society rather than by individual owners, such a tax could reduce speculation, fund public services, and make housing more affordable."

- **结构**: By capturing...介词短语作方式状语，其中created by society rather than by individual owners为过去分词短语修饰value；主句含三个并列谓语could reduce, fund, and make
- **翻译**: 通过将社会而非个人所有者创造的价值收归公共利益，这种税收可以减少投机、资助公共服务，并使住房更加可负担

## 文章大意

本文探讨土地作为经济要素的特殊性质。文章先指出土地供应固定这一根本特征；然后回顾李嘉图的地租理论——土地价值源于社会建设而非个人努力；接着分析现代城市中地价的巨大差异和城市化对地价的推动；再讨论高房价的经济影响——加剧不平等、挤出生产性投资、抑制消费和增长；最后介绍土地价值税方案及其面临的政治阻力，强调忽视土地特殊经济学的代价。

**1.** According to Ricardo's theory, what primarily determines the value of land?

A. The improvements made by the individual landowner
B. The natural fertility and resources of the land
C. The social development and infrastructure built around it
D. The global market prices for agricultural products

<details><summary>答案</summary>C。文章第二段指出Ricardo认为土地价值"derives not from what the owner has done but from what society has built around it"，即社会建设而非个人努力决定地价。</details>

**2.** What economic problem does rising land prices cause for young professionals?

A. They are forced to work longer hours to pay rent
B. They spend so much on rent that they cannot save or consume adequately
C. They cannot find employment in cities with high property prices
D. They are compelled to move to rural areas for cheaper housing

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章第五段指出年轻专业人士在昂贵城市中将如此大份额的收入花在房租上，"they have little left for consumption or saving, dampening overall economic growth"。</details>

**3.** Why has the land value tax been slow to implement?

A. It is technically too complex to calculate land values accurately
B. It has been ruled unconstitutional in most countries

D. It would not generate enough revenue to fund public services

<details><summary>答案</summary>C。文章末段指出土地价值税的实施进展缓慢，"hampered by political resistance from those who benefit most from the current arrangement"。</details>
