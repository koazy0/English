# Witnessing the Disappearance of Indonesia's Eternity Glaciers

---

On the highest peaks of Papua, in the remote mountains of what was once called Dutch New Guinea, glaciers have existed for approximately 5,000 years. The indigenous Papuans call them "the eternal ice" — a name that carried the weight of permanence, of something that outlasted civilizations and would outlast us all. Today, that name has become a cruel misnomer. Indonesia's glaciers are disappearing, and within a few years, they will be gone entirely.

The Puncak Jaya glacier, perched at nearly 4,900 meters on the Carstensz Pyramid, once covered several square kilometers of alpine terrain. Satellite imagery from the 1970s shows a substantial ice cap blanketing the summit. By 2020, it had fragmented into isolated patches barely visible from space. Researchers from Ohio State University, who have monitored the glacier since 2010, estimate that the remaining ice has lost more than 90 percent of its mass over the past century. At current rates, the last fragments will vanish before 2030.

What makes tropical glaciers particularly significant is their role as early warning systems. Unlike polar ice, which responds to climate change over centuries, tropical glaciers react within decades, making them sensitive barometers of global warming. The loss of Indonesia's glaciers is not merely a local tragedy; it is a signal that the planet's climate system is changing faster than many models predicted.

For the Papuan communities who live in the shadow of these mountains, the loss is cultural as well as environmental. The eternal ice features prominently in local mythology and spiritual practices. Oral traditions describe the glaciers as the tears of ancestral spirits, frozen in grief and preserved as a reminder of the sacred bond between the people and the land. As the ice melts, so too does a tangible connection to these traditions — not because the beliefs themselves are melting, but because the physical anchor of those beliefs is dissolving before their eyes.

Scientists emphasize that what is happening in Papua is happening everywhere, just more slowly and less visibly. The glaciers of the Andes, the Alps, and the Himalayas are all in retreat, but their decline is measured in percentage points over decades, while Papua's glaciers are measured in years. The difference is one of degree, not of kind.

When the last patch of ice disappears from Puncak Jaya, Indonesia will become the first tropical nation to lose all its glaciers. It will not be the last.

## 长难句解析

1. Unlike polar ice, which responds to climate change over centuries, tropical glaciers react within decades, making them sensitive barometers of global warming.
   - **解析**：介词短语 "Unlike polar ice" 引出对比，which 引导非限制性定语从句修饰 polar ice，主句主语 tropical glaciers，谓语 react，现在分词 making 引导结果状语，说明热带冰川对全球变暖的指示作用。

2. As the ice melts, so too does a tangible connection to these traditions — not because the beliefs themselves are melting, but because the physical anchor of those beliefs is dissolving before their eyes.
   - **解析**：as 引导时间状语从句，"so too does" 是倒装结构表示"同样消失"，破折号后用 "not because... but because..." 解释原因——不是信仰消失，而是信仰的物质载体正在眼前消融。
