# DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs Through Reinforcement Learning

---

In January 2025, the Chinese AI lab DeepSeek released a paper that sent shockwaves through the global AI community. DeepSeek-R1 demonstrated that a language model could develop sophisticated reasoning capabilities — chain-of-thought problem solving, mathematical proof construction, and multi-step logical inference — without being explicitly trained to do so. The key was reinforcement learning applied directly to a base model, and the results challenged fundamental assumptions about how reasoning emerges in artificial systems.

The paper's central insight is deceptively simple: if you reward a language model for producing correct final answers, it will spontaneously develop chain-of-thought reasoning as a strategy for reaching those answers. DeepSeek-R1-Zero, the paper's most striking variant, begins with a pretrained base model (DeepSeek-V3) and applies reinforcement learning with a rule-based reward function that evaluates only the correctness of the final answer. No supervised reasoning data is provided. No human demonstrations of chain-of-thought thinking are shown to the model. The model discovers reasoning on its own.

The emergence of reasoning is not gradual but phase-like. In early training, the model's outputs are short and often incorrect. Then, at a certain point in training, something shifts: the model begins producing longer outputs that include intermediate steps. It starts verifying its own work, catching errors, and revising its approach mid-solution. The researchers observed the spontaneous appearance of behaviors they never explicitly trained: self-reflection, backtracking, and the generation of alternative solution paths when the initial approach failed.

However, DeepSeek-R1-Zero exhibited significant drawbacks. Its reasoning chains were sometimes chaotic — interleaving different languages, producing redundant steps, or failing to format outputs consistently. To address these issues while preserving the emergent reasoning capability, the researchers developed DeepSeek-R1 through a multi-stage training pipeline. First, they performed a small amount of supervised fine-tuning on high-quality reasoning examples to establish a template for well-structured chain-of-thought output. Then, they applied the same reinforcement learning process used for R1-Zero, but with additional rewards for format compliance and language consistency alongside answer correctness. Finally, they performed a second round of rejection sampling and supervised fine-tuning to polish the model's outputs.

The paper also introduces reasoning distillation, the process of using DeepSeek-R1's outputs to train smaller models. The researchers demonstrated that a much smaller model fine-tuned on DeepSeek-R1's reasoning traces could achieve performance competitive with models many times its size. This finding has profound implications: if reasoning can be distilled from a large model to a small one, the cost of deploying reasoning-capable AI systems can be dramatically reduced.

The broader significance of DeepSeek-R1 lies in what it reveals about the nature of reasoning in language models. The fact that reasoning emerges through reinforcement learning, rather than requiring explicit training on reasoning data, suggests that the capacity for logical inference may be latent in sufficiently large pretrained models, needing only the right incentive structure to activate it. This challenges the prevailing assumption that reasoning must be taught and opens new research directions focused on discovering what other latent capabilities might be activated through similar techniques.

## 文章大意

DeepSeek-R1证明语言模型可通过强化学习自发发展推理能力。R1-Zero仅用规则化奖励函数评估最终答案正确性，不经任何推理示范训练，模型自发涌现出链式思考、自我反思和回溯等行为。但R1-Zero存在输出混乱问题，因此通过多阶段训练（少量推理示范微调→强化学习+格式奖励→拒绝采样再微调）开发DeepSeek-R1。推理蒸馏技术可将R1的推理能力转移到更小模型上。核心发现是推理能力可能潜在于大型预训练模型中，只需正确激励即可激活，挑战了推理必须被教授的传统假设。
