## 正文

Artificial intelligence is coming for your job — or so the headlines would have you believe. From self-driving trucks to algorithmic journalists, AI seems poised to automate an ever-wider range of human occupations. The anxiety is understandable: previous waves of automation eliminated millions of manufacturing and clerical jobs, and AI threatens to do the same to knowledge work. But the reality is more complicated than either the alarmists or the enthusiasts suggest.

The first thing to understand is that AI does not replace entire occupations; it replaces specific tasks. A radiologist, for example, does far more than read medical images — they consult with patients, coordinate treatment plans, and make judgement calls under uncertainty. AI can assist with image analysis, but it cannot replicate the full scope of a doctor's role. Similarly, legal AI tools can scan thousands of contracts for unusual clauses, but they cannot negotiate a settlement or argue before a judge. The occupations most at risk are those dominated by routine, predictable tasks: data entry, basic accounting, telemarketing, and assembly-line work.

Estimates of how many jobs will be displaced vary widely. A widely cited study by the Oxford Martin School suggested that 47 percent of American jobs were vulnerable to automation. More recent analyses, however, have been considerably more conservative. The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development estimates that only about 14 percent of jobs across its member countries are highly automatable. The difference arises because the earlier study classified entire occupations based on their most automatable tasks, while later research examined the specific tasks within each role. The truth likely lies between these extremes.

Yet even if displacement is more modest than feared, the transition will be painful. Workers who lose their jobs to automation rarely move smoothly into new positions. Older workers, those with fewer qualifications, and residents of regions dependent on a single industry face the steepest obstacles. Research on previous technological transitions — from the decline of coal mining to the automation of telephone operators — consistently shows that the costs are concentrated, while the benefits are diffuse. The workers who gain new, better-paying jobs are rarely the same as those who lost the old ones.

There is also the question of whether new jobs will appear fast enough. Historically, technology has created more jobs than it destroyed, but there is no guarantee this pattern will continue. The jobs most likely to emerge — AI engineers, data scientists, robotics technicians — require skills that displaced workers do not possess. Without massive investment in retraining and education, the mismatch between the jobs available and the skills workers have could widen dramatically.

The appropriate response is not to halt technological progress but to manage the transition wisely. This means strengthening social safety nets, investing in lifelong learning programmes, and redesigning education systems to emphasise adaptability over rote knowledge. It also means ensuring that the gains from AI are shared broadly — through progressive taxation, worker retraining funds, and perhaps even a universal basic income. The question is not whether AI will transform work, but whether society will shape that transformation or simply be shaped by it.

## 长难句解析

**1.** The difference arises because the earlier study classified entire occupations based on their most automatable tasks, while later research examined the specific tasks within each role.

- **拆分**：because 引导原因状语从句，其中包含 while 连接的对比结构；earlier study 与 later research 形成对比，classified...based on 与 examined...within 为两种不同研究方法。
- **翻译**：差异的出现是因为早期研究根据最可自动化的任务对整个职业进行分类，而后来研究则审视每个角色中的具体任务。

**2.** Research on previous technological transitions — from the decline of coal mining to the automation of telephone operators — consistently shows that the costs are concentrated, while the benefits are diffuse.

- **拆分**：主语 research on...transitions，谓语 shows，that 引导宾语从句；从句中 while 连接对比：costs are concentrated 与 benefits are diffuse；破折号间为举例说明。
- **翻译**：对以往技术转型的研究——从煤矿衰退到电话接线员自动化——一致表明，代价是集中的，而收益是分散的。

## 文章大意

本文分析了人工智能对就业的影响。AI并非取代整个职业，而是替代特定任务，因此放射科医生和律师等综合性角色不会轻易被取代。最危险的是以常规、可预测任务为主的工作。关于失业规模的估计差异很大，从47%到14%不等。即使替代规模较温和，转型也将是痛苦的——代价集中于特定群体，而收益分散。新创造的工作岗位可能要求被替代工人不具备的技能。文章主张不是阻止技术进步，而是通过加强社会保障、终身学习和共享AI收益来智慧地管理转型。
