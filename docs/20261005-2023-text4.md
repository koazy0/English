# 2023 Text 4 — 气候适应与减缓的博弈

## 正文

For decades, the fight against climate change has been dominated by one word: mitigation. Reducing greenhouse gas emissions, transitioning to renewable energy, and decarbonising industry have been the primary focus of international agreements, national policies, and activist campaigns. But as global temperatures continue to rise and the impacts of climate change become increasingly severe, a second imperative is gaining urgency: adaptation. The problem is that adaptation and mitigation are competing for the same limited resources — and the balance between them may determine whether communities survive the coming decades.

The case for adaptation is no longer debatable. Extreme weather events — floods, droughts, heatwaves, and storms — are occurring with greater frequency and intensity than climate models predicted just a decade ago. Sea levels are rising, threatening coastal cities where roughly one billion people live. Agricultural yields in tropical regions are declining, imperilling food security for the world's poorest populations. No matter how aggressively emissions are reduced, a significant degree of warming is already locked in. Adapting to these changes is not an admission of defeat — it is a practical necessity.

Yet adaptation receives far less attention and funding than mitigation. Climate finance — the money directed toward addressing climate change — is overwhelmingly concentrated on emissions reduction. Of the roughly 632 billion dollars in global climate finance in 2020, only about 7 percent went to adaptation. In developing countries, where adaptation needs are greatest, the gap is even wider. The UN Environment Programme estimates that adaptation costs in developing nations will reach 140 to 300 billion dollars per year by 2030, but current flows are barely a tenth of that amount. This chronic underfunding leaves vulnerable communities exposed to risks that are both predictable and preventable.

The tension between mitigation and adaptation is real. Every dollar spent on building seawalls or drought-resistant crops is a dollar not spent on replacing coal plants or subsidising electric vehicles. Some climate advocates fear that emphasising adaptation sends the wrong signal — that it implies acceptance of warming rather than resistance to it. They worry that adaptation could become an excuse for delay, allowing wealthy nations and corporations to continue emitting while paying for protection from the consequences. This concern is not unfounded: some of the biggest corporate investors in adaptation infrastructure are also among the largest emitters of greenhouse gases.

But the dichotomy between adaptation and mitigation is false. The two strategies are not alternatives; they are complements. Effective adaptation reduces the damage from climate change, buying time for mitigation efforts to take effect. Mitigation, in turn, limits the scale of adaptation that will ultimately be needed — every fraction of a degree of avoided warming reduces future adaptation costs exponentially. The most intelligent policies pursue both simultaneously: restoring mangroves, for instance, both sequesters carbon and protects coastlines from storm surges.

The challenge is to fund both adequately. This will require reforming international climate finance so that adaptation receives a fair share, expanding innovative financing mechanisms such as green bonds and resilience credits, and ensuring that the communities most affected by climate change have a voice in how resources are allocated. The climate crisis does not afford us the luxury of choosing between prevention and protection. We must do both — or we will achieve neither.

## 长难句解析

**1.** Every dollar spent on building seawalls or drought-resistant crops is a dollar not spent on replacing coal plants or subsidising electric vehicles.

- **拆分**：主句为 every dollar is a dollar not spent on...；spent on building... 为过去分词短语作后置定语修饰 dollar；not spent on replacing... 修饰第二个 dollar，体现资源分配的机会成本。
- **翻译**：花在建海堤或抗干旱作物上的每一美元，就是没有花在替换煤电厂或补贴电动汽车上的一美元。

**2.** Mitigation, in turn, limits the scale of adaptation that will ultimately be needed — every fraction of a degree of avoided warming reduces future adaptation costs exponentially.

- **拆分**：that 引导定语从句修饰 the scale of adaptation；破折号后进一步解释：every fraction of a degree 为主语，reduces 为谓语，exponentially 为副词强调效果的非线性关系。
- **翻译**：反过来，减缓限制了最终所需的适应规模——每避免零点几度的升温，就会指数级地降低未来的适应成本。

## 文章大意

本文论述了气候变化中适应与减缓之间的资源竞争。长期以来，气候政策以减缓（减少排放）为主导，但随着极端天气加剧，适应（应对已发生的变化）的紧迫性日益增加。然而适应资金严重不足——2020年全球气候资金中仅7%用于适应。二者之间的张力是真实的：用于建海堤的钱就不能用于替换煤电厂。但适应与减缓并非对立而是互补：有效适应争取时间让减缓发挥作用，减缓则降低未来适应需求。最聪明的政策是同时推进两者，如恢复红树林既固碳又防风暴。文章呼吁改革气候融资，确保两者都得到充分资金。
