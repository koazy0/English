# Manchester University to Offer Work Placements to All Undergraduates

> **Date**: 20260629 · **Source**: Adapted from The Guardian (May 2026) · **Difficulty**: ★★☆☆☆ · **Type**: 📰 同源外刊精选
>
> **Topic**: #教育 #就业

---

The University of Manchester has announced an ambitious plan to offer work placements to every one of its 32,000 undergraduate students, making it the first Russell Group institution to guarantee such an opportunity at scale. The initiative, set to launch in the 2026-27 academic year, will integrate placements ranging from four-week projects to full-year industry stints into every degree program, from philosophy to physics.

The university's vice-chancellor described the move as a "moral obligation" in an era when employers increasingly demand practical experience alongside academic credentials. "A degree alone is no longer enough," she said. "Students deserve not just knowledge but the chance to apply it in real-world settings." The program will partner with over 2,000 employers across the north of England, spanning sectors from advanced manufacturing to creative industries.

Supporters argue that work placements help bridge the persistent gap between university education and graduate employment. Research by the High Fliers group has consistently shown that graduates with placement experience are more likely to secure professional-level jobs within six months. For students from less privileged backgrounds, who often lack family connections to open workplace doors, guaranteed placements could be transformative. A third-year sociology student at Manchester noted that without her summer placement at a local council, she would have had "no idea" how to translate her degree into a career.

However, the plan has drawn significant skepticism. Critics question whether the university can realistically secure 32,000 quality placements, particularly in fields with limited employer bases such as classics or linguistics. There are concerns that some placements may amount to little more than clerical work, offering minimal genuine learning. A professor of medieval history, speaking anonymously, warned that "packing students off to offices for a few weeks does not constitute an education." Others worry about the logistical burden on already-stretched academic staff, who will need to coordinate placements alongside their teaching and research duties.

Employer commitment also remains uncertain. While many companies have signed on enthusiastically, sustaining engagement across thousands of small and medium-sized enterprises will require significant ongoing effort. Some industry leaders have privately expressed doubt about the capacity of smaller firms to provide meaningful supervision for students.

Despite these concerns, the initiative represents a significant shift in how British universities conceive of their role. If Manchester succeeds, other institutions are likely to follow. The question is not whether work experience matters—few dispute that—but whether universities can deliver it at scale without sacrificing quality. For Manchester's 32,000 undergraduates, the answer will soon become very real.

## 长难句解析

> **For students from less privileged backgrounds, who often lack family connections to open workplace doors**, guaranteed placements **could be transformative**.

- **主干**: guaranteed placements could be transformative
- **For students from less privileged backgrounds** 介词短语做状语，表示"对于……来说"。
- **who often lack family connections to open workplace doors** 是非限制性定语从句，补充说明这类学生的处境。
- **翻译**: 对于那些往往缺乏家庭关系来打开职场大门的弱势背景学生来说，有保障的实习岗位可能是改变命运的。

> **The question is not whether work experience matters**—few dispute that—**but whether universities can deliver it at scale without sacrificing quality**.

- **主干**: The question is not…but…（不是……而是……）
- 两个 whether 引导的从句分别作 is not 和 but 的表语。
- 破折号之间的 few dispute that 是插入语，表示"几乎没有人否认这一点"。
- **翻译**: 问题不在于工作经验是否重要——几乎没有人否认这一点——而在于大学能否在不牺牲质量的前提下大规模地提供实习机会。

## 文章大意

本文报道曼彻斯特大学宣布为全部 32,000 名本科生提供实习岗位，成为首个实施此计划的罗素集团大学。支持者认为这有助于弥补教育与就业之间的差距，尤其对缺乏社会资源的弱势学生意义重大。批评者则质疑学校能否为所有专业找到高质量的实习岗位，担心部分实习流于形式，以及行政和师资的负担。文章最后指出，关键不在于实习是否有价值，而在于大学能否在不牺牲质量的前提下实现规模化。
