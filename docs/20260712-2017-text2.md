## 正文

The scene has become familiar enough to be unremarkable: a family sits down for dinner, and within minutes, each member is gazing at a separate screen. The parents check work emails; the teenagers scroll through social media; the youngest child watches a video on a tablet. The meal is consumed, but the conversation, such as it is, is fragmented and distracted. This is family life in the age of the smartphone — connected to the world, but increasingly disconnected from one another.

The penetration of digital devices into family life has been swift and comprehensive. Surveys in developed countries show that the average household now contains more screens than people, and that the average adult spends over four hours a day on a smartphone. Children are not far behind: by the age of twelve, most have their own phone and spend several hours daily on it. The devices have colonized every room, every meal, and every spare moment.

The consequences of this digital saturation are only beginning to be understood, but early research paints a troubling picture. Studies have found that the mere presence of a smartphone on the table — even if it is turned face down — reduces the quality of conversation and the depth of connection between people who are physically present. Psychologists call this "technoference": the everyday intrusions and interruptions of devices that erode the quality of human interaction. Parents who are frequently distracted by their phones, for example, are more likely to respond impatiently to their children's requests, and their children, in turn, are more likely to exhibit behavioural problems.

The impact on children may be the most concerning aspect of this trend. Research has consistently shown that language development, emotional regulation, and the capacity for sustained attention all depend heavily on responsive, face-to-face interaction with adults. When parents are physically present but mentally absent — their attention fragmented by the ping of a notification — children receive a diluted version of the engagement they need. Pediatricians have warned that the rise in screen time among very young children may be contributing to delays in language acquisition and social development.

Yet it would be a mistake to view digital devices as purely destructive to family life. They also enable new forms of connection. Grandparents who live hundreds of miles away can video-call their grandchildren; working parents can stay in touch with their families throughout the day; and families separated by migration can maintain bonds that would once have been impossible. For many families, technology is not a barrier but a bridge.

The challenge, then, is not to eliminate screens from family life — an unrealistic goal in any case — but to manage them wisely. This requires establishing clear boundaries: device-free meals, screen-free bedrooms, and designated times when the whole family unplugs. It also requires parents to model the behaviour they wish to see. Children are keen observers of adult habits, and exhortations to put down the phone carry little weight when the adults themselves cannot do so.

In the end, the question is not whether digital devices belong in family life — they plainly do — but whether we can find a balance that preserves the depth and quality of human connection that no screen can replace.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> Studies have found that the mere presence of a smartphone on the table — even if it is turned face down — reduces the quality of conversation and the depth of connection between people who are physically present, and parents who are frequently distracted by their phones are more likely to respond impatiently to their children's requests.

**拆分**：
- **分句1**: Studies have found that the mere presence of a smartphone reduces the quality of conversation and the depth of connection（研究发现智能手机的单纯存在会降低对话质量和联系深度）
  - 破折号内让步状语: even if it is turned face down（即使屏幕朝下放着）
  - who are physically present 是定语从句，修饰 people
- **分句2**: parents who are frequently distracted by their phones are more likely to respond impatiently to their children's requests（经常被手机分心的父母更可能不耐烦地回应孩子的请求）

**翻译**: 研究发现，桌上仅有一部智能手机的存在——即使屏幕朝下——也会降低在场人士之间的对话质量和联系深度，而经常被手机分心的父母更可能不耐烦地回应孩子的请求。

### 句子2

> When parents are physically present but mentally absent — their attention fragmented by the ping of a notification — children receive a diluted version of the engagement they need, and pediatricians have warned that the rise in screen time among very young children may be contributing to delays in language acquisition and social development.

**拆分**：
- **时间状语从句**: When parents are physically present but mentally absent（当父母身在场而心不在时）
- **插入语（独立主格）**: their attention fragmented by the ping of a notification（他们的注意力被通知的提示音碎片化）
- **分句1**: children receive a diluted version of the engagement they need（孩子获得的是他们所需互动的稀释版本）
- **分句2**: pediatricians have warned that the rise in screen time...may be contributing to delays in language acquisition and social development（儿科医生警告幼童屏幕时间的增加可能导致语言习得和社会发展的延迟）

**翻译**: 当父母身在场而心不在——注意力被通知提示音碎片化——孩子们获得的只是他们所需互动的稀释版本，而儿科医生已警告幼童屏幕时间的增加可能导致语言习得和社会发展的延迟。

## 文章大意

本文讨论了数字设备对家庭生活的影响。智能手机已全面渗透家庭生活，造成"技术干扰"——即使手机只是放在桌上也会降低互动质量，父母分心会导致孩子行为问题。对儿童而言最令人担忧：父母"身在场心不在"会削弱孩子所需的深度互动，可能影响语言和社会发展。但数字设备也有积极面——让远隔千里的家人保持联系。作者认为挑战不是消除屏幕而是智慧管理，需要建立清晰边界和父母以身作则，找到保护人际连接深度的平衡。

   - B) Children are only interested in their parents' phones
   - C) Parents spend more on devices than on children's needs
   - D) Children cannot learn to use technology without parental guidance

   - B) Finding a balance that preserves the quality of human connection
   - C) Ensuring every family member has their own device
