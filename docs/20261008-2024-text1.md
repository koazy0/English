## 正文

In March 2024, the European Union passed the Artificial Intelligence Act — the world's first comprehensive law regulating AI. The legislation classifies AI systems by risk level, from minimal to unacceptable, and imposes obligations on developers and deployers accordingly. High-risk systems — those used in healthcare, law enforcement, and critical infrastructure — must meet strict requirements for transparency, data quality, and human oversight. Systems deemed an unacceptable risk, such as social credit scoring, are banned outright. The EU's approach represents a bold attempt to govern a technology that is evolving faster than the rules meant to contain it.

The United States has taken a markedly different path. Rather than enacting comprehensive legislation, the American approach has been characterised by voluntary guidelines, executive orders, and sector-specific regulations. In late 2023, President Biden issued an executive order establishing safety standards for AI development, but executive orders lack the permanence of legislation and can be reversed by subsequent administrations. The US position reflects a philosophy that prioritises innovation and competitive advantage, particularly in the geopolitical race with China, over precautionary regulation. Silicon Valley's influence on American policymaking is another significant factor: technology companies lobby aggressively against binding rules, arguing that overregulation will stifle the very innovation that drives economic growth.

Which approach is wiser? The question is far from settled. The European model has been criticised for being too rigid and too slow. AI capabilities are advancing at a pace that outstrips the legislative process, and there is a real risk that rules written today will be obsolete by the time they take full effect. Moreover, compliance costs could disadvantage European companies in a global market dominated by American and Chinese competitors. There are already signs of what critics call "regulatory flight" — AI companies choosing to develop and deploy their products in jurisdictions with lighter oversight.

The American model, by contrast, has been criticised for being too permissive. Without binding rules, the responsibility for ensuring AI safety falls largely on the companies developing the technology — an arrangement that creates obvious conflicts of interest when safety measures reduce profits or slow deployment. The risks are not hypothetical. AI systems have already demonstrated the capacity to generate convincing misinformation, perpetuate racial and gender biases in hiring and lending decisions, and enable mass surveillance. Without regulatory guardrails, these harms will multiply as AI becomes more capable and more widely deployed.

The fundamental challenge of regulating AI lies in the nature of the technology itself. Unlike previous technologies, AI systems can learn, adapt, and behave in ways that their creators did not explicitly programme. This makes it extraordinarily difficult to write rules that anticipate all possible harms without also prohibiting beneficial applications. The opacity of many AI systems — the so-called "black box" problem — further complicates oversight, since it can be impossible to determine exactly why a system made a particular decision.

Neither the European nor the American approach alone is sufficient. Effective AI governance will likely require elements of both: clear rules to address known risks, flexibility to adapt to unforeseen developments, and international cooperation to prevent regulatory arbitrage. The EU AI Act is a beginning, not an endpoint. The real test will be whether regulators can keep pace with a technology that shows no sign of slowing down.

## 长难句解析

**1.** The US position reflects a philosophy that prioritises innovation and competitive advantage, particularly in the geopolitical race with China, over precautionary regulation.

- **拆分**：that 引导定语从句修饰 philosophy；prioritises A over B 结构中 A 为 innovation and competitive advantage，B 为 precautionary regulation；particularly in... 为介词短语补充说明优先创新的背景。
- **翻译**：美国的立场反映了一种优先考虑创新和竞争优势——尤其是在与中国的地缘政治竞赛中——而非预防性监管的理念。

**2.** This makes it extraordinarily difficult to write rules that anticipate all possible harms without also prohibiting beneficial applications.

- **拆分**：it 为形式宾语，真正的宾语为 to write rules...；that 引导定语从句修饰 rules；without also prohibiting... 为介词短语表示避免的附带后果。
- **翻译**：这使得制定既能预见所有可能的危害又不会同时禁止有益应用的规则变得极其困难。

## 文章大意

本文对比了欧盟和美国的AI监管路径。欧盟通过了全球首部综合性AI法规——《人工智能法案》，按风险等级分类AI系统并施加相应义务。美国则采取自愿指南和行政命令的方式，优先创新和竞争优势。两种模式各有缺陷：欧盟被批评过于僵化和缓慢，合规成本可能使欧洲公司处于劣势；美国被批评过于放任，缺乏约束性规则导致安全问题。AI监管的根本挑战在于技术本身的不透明性和不可预测性——AI可以学习适应，做出创造者未明确编程的行为。文章认为有效的AI治理需要两种方法的结合，以及国际合作防止监管套利。
