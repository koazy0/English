# AI Is Changing How We Think, Not Replacing It

> **话题标签**: #AI #思维 #社会心理

## 正文

When writer Wendy Liu publicly declared she avoids AI tools because "thinking is supposed to be hard," she ignited a debate that reaches far beyond personal preference. Her argument is straightforward: the mental effort required to write, analyse, and solve problems is not merely a cost but the very substance of understanding. If we outsource that effort to machines, we risk losing something essential about how we learn and grow.

Richard Thackeray, responding to Liu, takes a different view. He argues that AI does not eliminate thinking so much as redirect it. Before AI, a curious student might spend hours tracking down a obscure reference or struggling to draft a presentable paragraph. Now that same student can generate a basic answer in seconds and spend the saved time asking deeper follow-up questions. Thackeray sees this as an expansion of curiosity rather than a contraction of it. The struggle, he suggests, has simply moved to a higher level — from gathering raw material to judging and refining what the machine provides.

Yet Phil Snell pushes back against this optimistic framing. He draws an analogy from music: a student who relies on software to compose a symphony may produce something polished, but the process bypasses the slow, uneven work that builds genuine skill. A musician who has wrestled with wrong notes and awkward phrases develops an intuitive feel for melody that no shortcut can replicate. Snell argues that the same principle applies to writing and reasoning. The messy drafts, the abandoned paragraphs, the moments of confusion — these are not obstacles to thought but the material from which deep understanding is constructed.

The tension between these two positions reflects a broader question about the role of difficulty in learning. There is little doubt that AI can accelerate routine tasks and broaden access to information. Studies have shown that professionals who use AI tools complete certain writing and analysis tasks up to 40 per cent faster, while reporting higher satisfaction with the results. However, speed and satisfaction are not the same as comprehension. A tool that makes work easier may also make it shallower if users never pause to question the output they receive.

Perhaps the most reasonable conclusion is that both sides capture part of the truth. AI can indeed be a powerful catalyst for curiosity when users treat it as a starting point rather than a final answer. But the value of that catalyst depends entirely on whether people retain the habit of pushing further — asking why, checking for errors, and refining rough ideas into genuine insight. The technology itself is neutral; what matters is the discipline we bring to it.

## 长难句解析

**1.** A musician who has wrestled with wrong notes and awkward phrases develops an intuitive feel for melody that no shortcut can replicate.

- **主句**: A musician develops an intuitive feel for melody
- **定语从句 1**: who has wrestled with wrong notes and awkward phrases（修饰 musician）
- **定语从句 2**: that no shortcut can replicate（修饰 an intuitive feel for melody）
- **翻译**: 一个与错音和别扭乐句反复较劲过的音乐家，会培养出一种任何捷径都无法复制的对旋律的直觉感受。

**2.** A tool that makes work easier may also make it shallower if users never pause to question the output they receive.

- **主句**: A tool may also make it shallower
- **定语从句**: that makes work easier（修饰 tool）
- **条件状语从句**: if users never pause to question the output they receive
- **嵌套定语从句**: they receive（修饰 the output，省略了 that）
- **翻译**: 一个让工作变得更轻松的工具，如果使用者从不停下来质疑所得到的输出，也可能使工作变得更浅薄。

## 文章大意（中文概括）

本文围绕"AI是促进还是损害人类思维"展开讨论。Thackeray认为AI将思维引向更高层次，是好奇心的扩展；Snell则以音乐类比，坚持艰辛的试错过程不可替代。作者最终指出，AI本身中性，关键在于使用者是否保持追问与反思的自律。

## 思考题

**1.** According to Thackeray, how does AI change the way people think?

A. It eliminates the need for critical judgment entirely.

B. It shifts mental effort from gathering material to evaluating results.

C. It reduces people's curiosity by providing quick answers.

D. It makes the writing process more mechanical and less creative.

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>B</b>。Thackeray的核心观点是：AI把人的思维"redirect"到了更高层次——从收集原始素材转向评判和完善AI给出的内容（第二段末句："from gathering raw material to judging and refining what the machine provides"）。A与原文相反；C是Liu的担忧，非Thackeray的观点；D原文未提及。
</details>

**2.** Snell uses the analogy of music to make the point that ______.

A. AI tools can help musicians produce more polished work

B. technology cannot replace the emotional depth of human performance

C. genuine skill comes from the imperfect and effortful process of creation

D. software-assisted composition is inherently inferior to traditional methods

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>C</b>。Snell用音乐类比的核心论点是：与错误和不完美反复较劲的过程才能培养真正的技能（第三段："the slow, uneven work that builds genuine skill"），而AI绕过了这个过程。A与Snell立场相反；B偷换概念，原文说的是"intuitive feel for melody"而非"emotional depth"；D过于绝对，Snell并非说软件辅助天生低劣，而是强调过程不可省略。
</details>

**3.** The author's attitude toward AI's impact on thinking can best be described as ______.

A. cautiously optimistic

B. deeply skeptical

C. objectively balanced

D. enthusiastically supportive

<details>
<summary>答案与解析</summary>
<b>C</b>。作者在末段总结"both sides capture part of the truth"，既承认AI可以是好奇心的催化剂，也警示浅层使用的风险，最终落脚于"the technology itself is neutral"，态度是客观中立的。A、D偏向乐观；B偏向悲观；C最符合文章的平衡立场。
</details>
