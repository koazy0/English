## 正文

For decades, the debate over energy seemed straightforward: renewable sources were cleaner but more expensive, while fossil fuels were cheap but environmentally damaging. That assumption has been turned on its head. In many parts of the world, wind and solar power are now cheaper than coal and gas — not because of subsidies, but because the underlying technology has improved dramatically.

The cost of solar panels has fallen by over 80 percent since 2010, while wind turbine prices have dropped by roughly 40 percent. In sun-rich regions like the Middle East and parts of Latin America, solar energy contracts are being signed at prices that would have seemed impossible a decade ago. Analysts now speak of "grid parity" — the point at which renewables can compete with fossil fuels without government support — as a reality rather than a distant ambition.

Yet the transition from fossil fuels to renewables is far from inevitable. One major obstacle is infrastructure. Solar and wind are intermittent sources: the sun does not always shine, and the wind does not always blow. This means that grids designed for steady coal or nuclear output must be rebuilt to handle fluctuating supply. Storage technology — primarily batteries — has improved but remains expensive and insufficient for large-scale needs. Upgrading the grid will require investments running into the trillions.

Political resistance is another formidable barrier. The fossil fuel industry employs millions worldwide and wields enormous political influence. In the United States, coal-producing states have lobbied aggressively to maintain subsidies and weaken environmental regulations. Similar patterns can be observed in Australia, where coal exports remain a cornerstone of the economy, and in parts of Eastern Europe, where governments are reluctant to phase out cheap domestic coal.

Developing nations face a particularly acute dilemma. They need affordable energy to lift their populations out of poverty, and fossil fuels still offer the quickest route. Asking these countries to rely on more expensive or less reliable renewable sources strikes many as unfair — especially when the accumulated carbon emissions causing climate change were produced largely by wealthy nations.

Despite these challenges, the momentum behind renewables appears unstoppable. China, once the world's most prolific builder of coal plants, has become the largest installer of wind and solar capacity. Major corporations are signing renewable energy deals not merely for environmental reasons but because it makes economic sense. The question is no longer whether the energy transition will happen, but whether it will happen fast enough to avoid the worst consequences of climate change. Time, as scientists keep reminding us, is the one resource that cannot be renewed.

## 长难句解析

**1.** "In sun-rich regions like the Middle East and parts of Latin America, solar energy contracts are being signed at prices that would have seemed impossible a decade ago."

- **结构**: 主句(solar energy contracts are being signed) + 定语从句(that would have seemed impossible...)修饰prices；"would have seemed"为虚拟语气，表示与过去事实相反的假设
- **翻译**: 在阳光充沛的中东和拉美部分地区，太阳能合同正以十年前看来不可能的价格签署

**2.** "Asking these countries to rely on more expensive or less reliable renewable sources strikes many as unfair — especially when the accumulated carbon emissions causing climate change were produced largely by wealthy nations."

- **结构**: 动名词短语(Asking these countries...)作主句主语，strikes为谓语；when引导时间状语从句，其中causing climate change为现在分词短语修饰carbon emissions
- **翻译**: 要求这些国家依赖更昂贵或更不可靠的可再生能源，让许多人觉得不公平——尤其是因为造成气候变化的累积碳排放主要来自富裕国家

## 文章大意

本文讨论可再生能源与化石燃料之间的转型问题。文章首先指出可再生能源成本大幅下降，已在多地实现"电网平价"；随后分析了转型面临的两大障碍——基础设施问题（间歇性发电需要储能和电网升级）和政治阻力（化石燃料行业的游说与发展中国家的能源需求）；最后指出尽管挑战巨大，转型势头不可阻挡，但速度是否足够快仍是关键问题。

## 思考题

**1.** According to the passage, what has fundamentally changed about renewable energy?

A. It now receives more government subsidies than fossil fuels
B. It has become cheaper than fossil fuels due to technological improvement
C. It has completely replaced fossil fuels in most countries
D. It is no longer affected by weather conditions

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章第一段明确指出可再生能源更便宜"not because of subsidies, but because the underlying technology has improved dramatically"，B选项准确概括了这一原因。</details>

**2.** Why do developing nations face a "particularly acute dilemma" regarding energy transition?

A. They lack the technology to build renewable energy infrastructure
B. They are pressured by wealthy nations to reduce carbon emissions
C. They need affordable energy urgently but fossil fuels remain the quickest option
D. They have already invested too heavily in renewable energy projects

<details><summary>答案</summary>C。文章第五段指出发展中国家需要负担得起的能源来脱贫，而化石燃料仍是最快途径，这构成了他们的困境。</details>

**3.** What does the author imply about the energy transition?

A. It will be completed within the next decade
B. Its pace is more important than its direction
C. It depends entirely on government policy
D. It has already solved the problem of climate change

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章末句指出问题不再是转型"是否"会发生，而是"是否足够快"以避免气候变化最严重的后果，暗示速度比方向更关键。</details>
