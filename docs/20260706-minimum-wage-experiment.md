# The Minimum Wage Experiment That Defied Conventional Economics

> **Date**: 20260706 · **Source**: Adapted from The Atlantic (May 2026) · **Difficulty**: ★★★☆☆ · **Type**: 📰 同源外刊精选
>
> **Topic**: #经济 #政策 #社会

---

When Seattle voted in 2014 to raise its minimum wage to fifteen dollars an hour—then the highest in the nation—economists on both sides of the debate thought they knew what would happen next. Proponents predicted a surge in consumer spending as low-wage workers gained more disposable income. Opponents warned of inevitable job losses, reduced hours, and businesses fleeing the city for cheaper pastures. A decade later, neither prediction proved entirely correct, but the results have quietly reshaped how economists think about labour markets.

The most striking finding is what did not happen. Restaurants did not close en masse. Small businesses did not abandon Seattle for neighbouring municipalities with lower wage floors. Employment in low-wage sectors remained stable, and in some categories even grew. Workers saw their paychecks rise meaningfully—an increase of roughly 20 percent for those at the bottom of the wage distribution—without suffering corresponding reductions in hours. The catastrophe that textbook economics had forecast simply failed to materialise.

This does not mean the transition was painless. Some workers at the very margin—those in highly competitive sectors with thin profit margins—did see their hours reduced. A few restaurants reported raising prices by modest amounts to absorb higher labour costs. Younger workers and those with less experience found it slightly harder to secure their first job, as employers became marginally more selective. These are real costs, and they should not be dismissed.

But the broader picture confounds the central prediction of neoclassical labour economics: that higher wages inevitably reduce employment. That model assumes a competitive market in which employers pay workers exactly what their labour is worth, so any mandated increase above market rate must reduce demand. In reality, labour markets are far more complex. Many employers possess what economists call monopsony power—the ability to set wages below the competitive level because workers have few alternatives. In such markets, a minimum wage does not destroy jobs; it merely transfers surplus from employers to workers.

The Seattle experience also highlighted an effect that standard models overlook: the productivity response. When workers earn more, they tend to stay longer in their jobs, reducing turnover and the costs associated with hiring and training. Several Seattle employers reported that higher wages improved morale and reduced absenteeism. In low-wage industries where turnover is notoriously expensive, these savings can offset much of the increased wage bill.

None of this proves that minimum wages can be raised indefinitely without consequences. At some level, the textbook prediction will hold. But Seattle's experiment suggests that the optimal minimum wage is considerably higher than conventional economics has long assumed. The neoliberal paradigm—which treated labour as a simple commodity subject to supply and demand—has been found wanting. The evidence points toward a more nuanced understanding: labour markets are shaped by power, institutions, and human behaviour in ways that neat equations cannot fully capture.

## 长难句解析

> The neoliberal paradigm—**which treated labour as a simple commodity subject to supply and demand**—**has been found wanting**.

- **主干**: The neoliberal paradigm has been found wanting
- **which treated labour as a simple commodity** 是非限制性定语从句，修饰 paradigm。
- **subject to supply and demand** 是形容词短语做后置定语，修饰 commodity，表示"受供需支配的"。
- **has been found wanting** 是固定表达，意为"被证明不够格/有缺陷"。
- **翻译**: 将劳动力视为受供需支配的简单商品的新自由主义范式，已被证明存在不足。

> **In such markets, a minimum wage does not destroy jobs**; **it merely transfers surplus from employers to workers**.

- **主干**: a minimum wage does not destroy jobs; it merely transfers surplus
- 这是一个并列句，由分号连接。
- **In such markets** 指代前文提到的存在买方垄断力量的市场。
- **from employers to workers** 说明剩余转移的方向。
- **翻译**: 在此类市场中，最低工资不会摧毁就业岗位；它只是将剩余从雇主转移给工人。

## 文章大意

本文以西雅图 2014 年将最低工资提高到每小时 15 美元为例，探讨最低工资政策的实际效果。十年跟踪表明：就业并未大幅下降，工人收入显著增加，教科书式的灾难预言未成为现实。文章指出边际上确实存在少量负面影响，但整体图景颠覆了新古典经济学关于"工资提高必减少就业"的核心预测。作者引入买方垄断理论和生产力效应解释这一现象，认为新自由主义范式存在缺陷，劳动市场比简单的供需模型更为复杂。

## 思考题

**1. What was the most surprising finding from Seattle's minimum wage experiment?**

A. Consumer spending did not increase as proponents had predicted.

B. The predicted job losses and business closures largely failed to occur.

C. All low-wage workers received an immediate 50 percent pay increase.

D. Small businesses voluntarily raised wages beyond the mandated level.

<details><summary>答案</summary>B。文章指出"the most striking finding is what did not happen"—即预期中的灾难（失业、企业外迁）没有发生。A与"surge in consumer spending"的预测不构成最令人惊讶的发现；C的50%不准确（原文约20%）；D未提及。</details>

**2. According to the passage, what is "monopsony power"?**

A. The ability of workers to negotiate wages above the market rate.

B. The power of governments to set wage levels across industries.

C. The ability of employers to set wages below the competitive level because workers lack alternatives.

D. The tendency of businesses to collude on pricing strategies.

<details><summary>答案</summary>C。文章定义monopsony power为"the ability to set wages below the competitive level because workers have few alternatives"。A与定义相反；B说的是政府权力；D未提及。</details>

**3. What does the author conclude about the neoliberal economic paradigm?**

A. It has been completely discredited and should be abandoned entirely.

B. It remains the best available framework for understanding labour markets.

C. It has been shown to be insufficient for fully capturing how labour markets work.

D. It correctly predicted the outcomes of Seattle's minimum wage experiment.

<details><summary>答案</summary>C。文章指出"the neoliberal paradigm…has been found wanting"并呼吁"a more nuanced understanding"，说明该范式不足但并非全然错误。A过于极端（"completely discredited"）；B与文章结论相反；D与事实相反。</details>
