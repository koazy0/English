## 正文

Ask most people why they work, and the answer is likely to be straightforward: for the money. It seems obvious — people trade their time and effort for wages, and without financial compensation, few would choose to spend forty hours a week in an office or on a factory floor. Yet decades of research in organizational psychology suggest that the relationship between work and motivation is far more complex than this simple exchange implies.

The traditional view of workplace motivation, rooted in the industrial era, assumes that workers are primarily driven by external rewards — pay raises, bonuses, and promotions. According to this view, the task of management is straightforward: set clear expectations, tie compensation to performance, and workers will deliver. This approach works reasonably well for routine, repetitive tasks. When the work is simple and the path to the goal is well defined, larger incentives do indeed lead to greater effort.

But for tasks that require creativity, problem-solving, or genuine engagement, the picture changes dramatically. In a famous series of experiments, psychologists have demonstrated that offering large external rewards for creative tasks can actually reduce performance. When people are too focused on the reward, they tend to narrow their thinking and become less willing to take risks or explore unconventional solutions. The very incentive that was intended to boost productivity ends up undermining it.

What, then, truly motivates people in complex work environments? Researchers increasingly point to three factors: autonomy, mastery, and purpose. Autonomy refers to the desire to direct our own lives — to have a say in what we do, when we do it, and how we do it. Mastery is the urge to get better at something that matters to us. And purpose is the longing to do what we do in the service of something larger than ourselves.

Companies that have embraced these insights have seen impressive results. Technology firms that allow engineers to spend a portion of their time on self-directed projects consistently report higher levels of innovation and employee retention. Organizations that invest in training and development find that employees who feel they are growing professionally are more engaged and more productive. And firms that clearly communicate a meaningful mission beyond profit tend to attract talent that is motivated not merely by salary but by a sense of contribution.

None of this is to suggest that money does not matter. Adequate compensation is a baseline requirement — people cannot be expected to bring their best selves to work if they are worried about paying the rent. But once that baseline is met, the factors that drive exceptional performance are not financial. They are psychological. And understanding that distinction may be the most important lesson that any manager can learn.

## 长难句解析

### 句子1

> When people are too focused on the reward, they tend to narrow their thinking and become less willing to take risks or explore unconventional solutions, and the very incentive that was intended to boost productivity ends up undermining it.

**拆分**：
- **时间状语从句**: When people are too focused on the reward（当人们过于关注奖励时）
- **分句1**: they tend to narrow their thinking and become less willing to take risks or explore unconventional solutions（他们倾向于缩小思维范围，变得更不愿意冒险或探索非传统的解决方案）
- **分句2**: the very incentive that was intended to boost productivity ends up undermining it（本意是提高生产力的激励最终反而削弱了它）
  - that was intended to boost productivity 是定语从句，修饰 incentive

**翻译**: 当人们过于关注奖励时，他们倾向于缩小思维范围，变得更不愿意冒险或探索非传统方案，而本意是提高生产力的激励最终反而削弱了生产力。

### 句子2

> And firms that clearly communicate a meaningful mission beyond profit tend to attract talent that is motivated not merely by salary but by a sense of contribution.

**拆分**：
- **主句**: firms tend to attract talent（公司倾向于吸引人才）
- **定语从句1**: that clearly communicate a meaningful mission beyond profit（明确传达超越利润的有意义使命的）
- **定语从句2**: that is motivated not merely by salary but by a sense of contribution（其动机不仅仅是薪水而是贡献感的）
- **并列结构**: not merely...but...（不仅是……而且是……）

**翻译**: 那些明确传达超越利润的有意义使命的公司，往往能吸引那些动机不仅仅是薪水而是贡献感的人才。

## 文章大意

本文探讨了工作动机的复杂性。传统观点认为人们工作主要是为了外在报酬，但研究表明对于创造性工作，大额奖励反而会降低表现。真正驱动人们在复杂工作环境中表现出色的是三个内在因素：自主权、精通感和目的感。文章指出，虽然基本薪酬是必要的，但超出基准后，卓越表现的动力来自心理需求而非金钱。

## 思考题

1. According to the passage, when do larger incentives fail to improve work performance?
   - A) When the tasks are simple and well defined
   - B) When the incentives are not large enough
   - C) When the work requires creativity and genuine engagement
   - D) When workers lack adequate training

2. Which of the following best describes the relationship between autonomy and motivation?
   - A) Autonomy reduces motivation by creating confusion
   - B) Autonomy enhances motivation by allowing people to direct their own work
   - C) Autonomy is only important for senior employees
   - D) Autonomy and motivation are unrelated according to research

3. What does the author conclude about the role of money in work motivation?
   - A) Money is the only factor that matters for workplace motivation
   - B) Money is irrelevant once people have a job they enjoy
   - C) Money is a necessary baseline but not the key to exceptional performance
   - D) Money should be completely replaced by non-financial incentives

<details>
<summary>参考答案</summary>

1. **C** — 文中指出"for tasks that require creativity, problem-solving, or genuine engagement...offering large external rewards...can actually reduce performance"。

2. **B** — 自主权被定义为"the desire to direct our own lives"，而给予自主权的公司报告了更高的创新力和员工留存率。

3. **C** — 作者指出"adequate compensation is a baseline requirement"，但"once that baseline is met, the factors that drive exceptional performance are not financial"，即金钱是必要基线但不是卓越表现的关键。

</details>
